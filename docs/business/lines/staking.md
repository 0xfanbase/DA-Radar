# Staking services

A bank that holds virtual assets (VAs) for clients may commit them to a blockchain's validation process and pass the returns to those clients. [S:4641e6c404d2, p.1]

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |

## What the bank does

- Banks, and subsidiaries of locally incorporated banks, may offer staking from their custody services under HKMA standards dated 7 April 2025. [S:4641e6c404d2, p.1]
- Staking means locking tokens so a validator can take part in a proof-of-stake network and earn rewards. [S:4641e6c404d2, p.1] A validator runs the computers that confirm new blocks, and puts up tokens as a stake to earn that role. (concept)
- The HKMA says a bank must do all reasonable due diligence on a blockchain before it offers staking on it. [S:4641e6c404d2, section (C), p.2] The bank must also make sure its controls and systems can support staking on that chain. [S:4641e6c404d2, section (C), p.2]
- A bank may outsource staking work to a third party. [S:4641e6c404d2, section (C), p.3] It should check that provider first and then keep monitoring it. [S:4641e6c404d2, section (C), p.3]
- Other firms can use a bank as their staking provider. [S:31b4f67e0233, para 8.2] The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) sets terms for licensed corporations, such as brokers, and for registered institutions, which are banks registered with it. [S:31b4f67e0233, para 8.2] Under those terms, they should stake client assets only through an SFC-licensed platform or a bank. [S:31b4f67e0233, para 8.2]
- The SFC may let the virtual-asset funds it authorises stake through licensed platforms or banks. [S:c79c4686c5e0, paras 28, 31] Such funds need prior consultation with, and approval from, the SFC. [S:c79c4686c5e0, paras 28, 31]

## Revenue levers

- A staking provider can keep a share of the network rewards and pass the rest to the client. It can also charge a fee on staked assets instead. (concept)
- The HKMA expects a bank to disclose its staking fees and charges. [S:4641e6c404d2, section (B), p.2] It should also disclose lock-up and unstaking periods. [S:4641e6c404d2, section (B), p.2]
- Licensed corporations and registered institutions should tell clients the fees agreed with their staking provider and how those fees are worked out. [S:31b4f67e0233, para 8.7(d)]
- An SFC-authorised fund that stakes should report the revenue and the direct and indirect costs of its staking. [S:c79c4686c5e0, para 28(f)]
- Staking gives clients a return on assets that would otherwise sit idle in custody. That can help a bank keep custody balances. (concept)

## Cost and capital drivers

- The HKMA expects a bank to control everything needed to take client assets out of staking. [S:4641e6c404d2, section (A) and fn 2, p.2] Examples are the withdrawal keys and exit messages signed in advance. [S:4641e6c404d2, section (A) and fn 2, p.2]
- A bank should explain the extra risks of staking. [S:4641e6c404d2, section (B), p.2] The circular lists slashing risk, lock-up risk, software bugs and hacking among them. [S:4641e6c404d2, section (B), p.2] Slashing is a network penalty that takes away part of a validator's staked tokens. (concept)
- The bank should also say how such losses would be dealt with. [S:4641e6c404d2, section (B), p.2] Who bears a slashing loss depends on the contracts between client, bank and validator. (concept)
- Licensed corporations and registered institutions should get a signed risk acknowledgement from most clients. [S:31b4f67e0233, para 8.8] Institutional and qualified corporate professional investors are the exception. [S:31b4f67e0233, para 8.8]
- Checking an outside staking provider covers its track record, technology, risk measures and security controls. [S:4641e6c404d2, section (C), p.3]
- A firm that stops staking should take due account of clients' best interests. [S:31b4f67e0233, para 8.6] It should explain their options, such as unstaking. [S:31b4f67e0233, para 8.6]

## Official signals

- In April 2025 the SFC set out staking guidance for platforms and funds under the Products pillar of its roadmap. [S:36753b22e10a, para 2]
- That roadmap (ASPIRe) listed staking as something to consider under clear custody and operating rules. [S:d461c2db6af2, Pillar P, Initiative 8]
- In September 2025 the SFC and HKMA said they are now prepared to let intermediaries offer staking. [S:b79266f3da51, para 4]
- Intermediaries should tell the regulators before they offer staking for the first time. [S:b79266f3da51, para 10(c)]
- The HKMA's May 2026 custody guidance asks banks to take account of the SFC's staking terms for platforms. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 26]
- In April 2025 the SFC said the virtual-asset funds it authorises are required to stake only through licensed platforms and banks. It added that a cap applies, to manage liquidity risk. [S:36753b22e10a, para 4] [S:36753b22e10a, para 4]

## Industry benchmarks

- HashKey Holdings (HashKey), a Hong Kong-listed platform group, reported group-wide figures in its 2025 annual results. It gave its own measure, a staking monetisation rate, of 0.2%, down from 0.3% in 2024. [I:786f45f6ea11, PDF p.4] The same report gave average staked assets of about HK$22.4 billion. [I:786f45f6ea11, PDF p.4]
- HashKey's 2026 interim results, also a company filing, reported average staked assets of HK$14.8 billion in the first half. [I:9ca15dca711d, PDF p.11] The same report gave HK$20.2 billion a year earlier. [I:9ca15dca711d, PDF p.11]
- Coinbase Global's 2025 annual report, global in scope, reported US$677.4 million of blockchain rewards revenue for 2025. [I:6fc50821b9ab, PDF p.94] The same report showed US$427.5 million of blockchain rewards fees as a cost. [I:6fc50821b9ab, PDF p.95]
- China Asset Management (Hong Kong) published a key facts statement for its Hong Kong-listed ether exchange-traded fund (ETF) in April 2026. It said the fund intends to stake up to 30% of its ether. [I:ee50e4efff6f, PDF p.2]

## Analysis

> **Analysis — not official**
> **Question:** Is staking a profit line in its own right, or a feature that keeps custody clients?
> **How to think about it:**
> - Staking income is roughly staked assets times the network reward rate times the bank's share. (concept)
> - Against that sit validator fees, key-control systems, disclosure work and loss handling.
> - A thin share on a large custody book can matter more than a wide share on a small one.
> **What it depends on:** network reward rates; token prices; client appetite; the share the bank keeps; who bears slashing losses; vendor costs.
> **Official signposts:**
> - The SFC staking terms that banks take account of. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 26]
> - Notice to regulators before a firm first offers staking. [S:b79266f3da51, para 10(c)]
> - Faster licensing for banks already assessed for custody. [S:f41a543388bc, para 51]
> **What this is not:** not a forecast, not advice and not the regulator's view.
