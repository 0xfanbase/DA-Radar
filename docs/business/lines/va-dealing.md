# Virtual-asset dealing

A bank buys and sells virtual assets for clients through platforms that hold a Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) licence. [S:8a016a912528, para 17]

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Bank role | Broker that deals for clients |
| Client segments | Retail, private banking, institutional |
| Value chain | Trade |
| Regulatory gate | Registration for Type 1 (dealing in securities), plus SFC conditions for virtual asset (VA) dealing [S:8a016a912528, paras 18-19] |
| Status as of 25 Sep 2026 | In force [S:8a016a912528, para 17] |
| Related modules | C1, C7, C6, D1, D3, B2, E1 |
| Related cases | 4-vatp-incident |

## What the bank does

- A bank registered with the SFC is a registered institution (RI). An RI can introduce clients to a licensed virtual asset trading platform (VATP), or trade for them through one pooled (omnibus) account there. [S:8a016a912528, para 17]
- The RI should offer the service only to clients of its Type 1 business. [S:8a016a912528, para 18] An RI dealing only in Relevant Stablecoins is not limited in this way. [S:a0bd48559762, para (2), p.3] These are stablecoins issued under a licence from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA). [S:a0bd48559762, p.1]
- When it only introduces clients (as an introducing agent), the bank should not pass client orders to the platform or hold client money or tokens. [S:8a016a912528, para 21]
- Since May 2026, an RI may deal in a Relevant Stablecoin with its licensed issuer. It may also serve retail clients through a VATP licensed only for professional investors, if those clients trade only tokens the platform admitted for retail. [S:a0bd48559762, para (1), p.3]
- An RI may trade for clients on a shared order book, which links a platform's order book with those of its global affiliates. [S:e64b7b0267b2, Shared Order Book and fn 8, p.6] Retail clients may join only after a clear risk explanation and their express choice. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 17] The HKMA applies this SFC guidance to registered institutions. [S:e64b7b0267b2, Shared Order Book, p.6]

## Revenue levers

- Clients pay a commission, or a spread (the gap between the buying and selling price), on each trade. (concept)
- Dealing income equals client trading volume times the fee rate the bank keeps after the platform's own charge. (concept)
- Client token deposits and withdrawals should go only through segregated accounts at the partner platform or at a bank meeting HKMA custody standards. [S:8a016a912528, para 20(b)] A bank that also offers custody can charge a holding fee on the same client assets. (concept)

## Cost and capital drivers

- Before serving retail clients, the bank should check each client's VA knowledge and risk tolerance. It should also set an exposure limit based on the client's finances, including net worth. [S:8a016a912528, para 20(a)(i)-(ii)]
- Every RI needs at least two executive officers for each regulated activity, each with the Monetary Authority's written consent. [S:8934af8cc884, para 2.2.4]
- Where clients move tokens in or out, the bank should follow Chapter 12 of the SFC's anti-money laundering (AML) guideline. [S:8a016a912528, para 20(b)] SFC withdrawal safeguards then apply on top of HKMA e-banking and technology risk rules. [S:e64b7b0267b2, Client VA withdrawals, p.6]
- Group 2b cryptoassets (the riskiest capital class) take a 1,250% risk weight. [S:9e8d84c87168, Part XII CAE1 explanatory note, column (c), p.215] The minimum total capital ratio is 8%. [S:5d0c13e09312, para 2.1.2] So 1,250% × 8% = 100%: at the minimum, the bank holds capital equal to the whole exposure. A capital charge is set against assets the bank itself holds, so it matters most when a bank trades on its own account. (concept)
- Under the planned regime, licence and annual fees for VA dealing would be set by reference to Type 1 fees. [S:6186c01c6649, para 38] The conclusions also state that the SFC plans to require VA dealers to keep client tokens with SFC-regulated custodians. [S:6186c01c6649, para 26]

## Official signals

- The SFC's list showed 13 licensed platforms at its 29 May 2026 update. [S:b14be2203d39, Licensed platforms table; Last update line]
- Firms already providing VA dealing, including registered institutions, are to get a faster approval process when the new dealing regime starts. [S:6186c01c6649, paras 35-36]
- The Government and the SFC do not plan to treat existing VA dealers as licensed while they apply. [S:6186c01c6649, para 31] The regime takes full effect on its start date. [S:6186c01c6649, para 31] Providers that do not contact the SFC or HKMA for pre-application would have to stop when the regime starts. [S:6186c01c6649, para 34]
- In June 2026 the Government said it and the SFC would bring a bill before the Legislative Council during 2026. It would cover VA dealing, custody, advice and management. [S:c6df52e680de, Reply (3), 'In addition' para] The start date of the dealing regime is not set. [S:6186c01c6649, para 32]
- Under the June 2025 consultation proposals, the SFC would set the standards and the HKMA would be the frontline regulator for registered banks. [S:7395a53cc85a, para 4]
- The 2026 Policy Address says the SFC will enhance VA licensing regimes and issue guidelines that set out a clear path to compliance. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 49(i)]

## Industry benchmarks

- HashKey Holdings Limited (HashKey) runs an SFC-licensed platform. It reported 2025 omnibus client volume of about HK$86.4 billion, against HK$11.7 billion in 2024. [I:786f45f6ea11, PDF p.3-4] This is a reported group figure covering Hong Kong and other markets. [I:786f45f6ea11, PDF p.3-4]
- HashKey's 2025 annual results (group, reported) give HK$522.8 million of revenue from its trading services segment. [I:786f45f6ea11, PDF p.11] The same results give HK$590.8 billion of trading volume for those services. [I:786f45f6ea11, PDF p.11] We worked out the ratio ourselves from those two figures: HK$522.8 million of revenue on HK$590.8 billion of volume is about 0.09%. HashKey does not publish this ratio. It is our own sum, not an estimate by any publisher.
- Coinbase Global's 2025 annual report covers a mainly US business (global scope, reported). [I:6fc50821b9ab, PDF p.93] It gives US$3,322.8 million of net consumer transaction revenue on US$239 billion of consumer volume. [I:6fc50821b9ab, PDF p.93] It gives US$479.7 million of net institutional transaction revenue on US$982 billion of institutional volume. [I:6fc50821b9ab, PDF p.93] Our own sums from those figures give about 1.39% for consumer trades and about 0.049% for institutional trades. Coinbase does not publish these ratios, and they are not Hong Kong benchmarks.

## Analysis

> **Analysis — not official**
> **Question:** How much client trading does an omnibus dealing service need to cover its fixed compliance costs?
> **How to think about it:** Split costs into fixed and variable parts. Fixed costs include executive officers, platform due diligence, AML checks on token movements and record keeping. Income is client volume times the fee rate the bank keeps after the platform's charge. For example, keeping 0.5% on HK$1 billion of yearly volume gives HK$5 million before costs (illustrative).
> **What it depends on:** The mix of retail and professional clients. The partner platform's charges. The share of clients who trade only Relevant Stablecoins. The final costs of the new dealing regime.
> **Official signposts:** The dealing regime's start date. [S:6186c01c6649, para 32] The bill promised for 2026. [S:c6df52e680de, Reply (3), 'In addition' para] New SFC guidelines for VA firms. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 49(i)] The SFC's list of licensed platforms. [S:b14be2203d39, Last update line]
> **What this is not:** Not a forecast, not advice and not the regulators' view.
