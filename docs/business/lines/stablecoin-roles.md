# Stablecoin roles for banks

A bank can issue a stablecoin, hold the issuer's reserves, sell or trade the coin, or lend against it (concept).

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Bank role | Issuer; reserve bank and custodian; distributor and dealer; lender that takes coins as collateral |
| Client segments | Retail, private banking, corporate, institutional, fintechs (stablecoin issuers) |
| Value chain | Issue, Distribute, Trade, Hold, Settle and pay, Finance |
| Regulatory gate | Issuing needs a stablecoin issuer licence from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA). [S:fe4baea8dd7d, para 2.7.1] Banks may offer licensed coins to investors as 'permitted offerors'. [S:7b6c8dc63140, Reply (1)] |
| Status as of 25 Sep 2026 | In force [S:c6df52e680de, Reply, opening para] |
| Related modules | C4, C1, C6, D1, D3 |
| Related cases | 3-stablecoin-reserve-distributor |

## What the bank does

A stablecoin is a token meant to keep a steady value against a currency (concept).

- **Issue.** A bank may apply for an issuer licence. A bank incorporated overseas does not need a Hong Kong subsidiary. [S:fe4baea8dd7d, paras 3.2.1-3.2.3]
- **Hold reserves.** The HKMA says issuers should appoint qualified custodians (firms that safekeep assets) for reserves. A licensed bank is one eligible custodian. [S:240be6833157, para 2.5.4]
- **Take reserve deposits.** Reserves may include bank deposits of up to three months. [S:240be6833157, para 2.3.1] In June 2026 the Government said HKMA rules have issuers place reserve assets with banks in Hong Kong. [S:c6df52e680de, Reply (1)]
- **Sell.** Banks are one of five types of 'permitted offerors' (firms the law allows to offer stablecoins). They may offer licensed coins to retail and professional investors. [S:7b6c8dc63140, Reply (1)]
- **Deal.** A bank registered with the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) is a registered institution (RI). An RI may deal in licensed coins through the issuer or a licensed virtual asset trading platform (VATP). [S:a0bd48559762, paras (1), (3)(c), pp.3-5]
- **Lend.** An RI may choose to rely on crypto collateral. If so, the HKMA says eligible collateral should be only bitcoin, ether and licensed coins. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (4), pp.3-4] The RI should apply a prudent haircut (a cut to the value it counts). [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (4), pp.3-4]

## Revenue levers

- **Reserve deposits.** A bank holding reserve deposits earns the gap between what it earns on the money and what it pays for it (concept).
- **Issuer income.** An issuer that pays holders nothing keeps the return on its reserves, less its costs (concept). The Stablecoins Ordinance bars issuers from paying interest to holders. [S:fe4baea8dd7d, para 3.13.1]
- **Custody and sales fees.** A custodian charges fees on the value it holds, and a seller earns a commission or a price spread (concept).
- **Payments.** A bank can charge fees for moving money into and out of coins (concept). Both licensed issuers plan cross-border payments, local payments and settling tokenised asset trades. [S:193316443831, Use cases and strategic priorities section]

## Cost and capital drivers

- **Issuer capital.** A licensed issuer must keep paid-up capital of at least HK$25 million, or other approved resources of the same amount. [S:240be6833157, para 5.1.1] A bank licensee is exempt and follows the Banking Ordinance instead. [S:240be6833157, para 5.1.3]
- **Redemption.** Issuers must let holders redeem at par (face value). [S:240be6833157, para 3.3.1] Valid requests should be processed within one business day unless the HKMA approves otherwise. [S:240be6833157, para 3.3.3]
- **Liquidity.** The liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) tests whether a bank has enough liquid assets for a short stress period. A category 1 bank (one that must meet the LCR) that issues a coin redeemable in that period must generally assume a full outflow. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, paras 6-7] A category 1 bank holding such a coin generally counts no cash inflow from it. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 7]
- **Capital.** HKMA guidance puts stablecoins with effective stabilisation in Group 1b. Group 1 assets generally take the same capital as the assets they relate to. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 1.3.2]
- **Anti-money laundering (AML).** An issuer should check customers before an occasional transaction of HK$8,000 or more. [S:4d98c6a80b4d, para 4.4] For transfers at or above that amount, the sending firm must obtain and record fuller sender and recipient data. [S:4d98c6a80b4d, paras 6.5-6.6]

## Official signals

- The HKMA received 36 first-batch applications by 30 September 2025. It licensed two issuers on 10 April 2026. [S:193316443831, Assessment of licence applications, paras 1-2]
- The register lists two licensees from 10 April 2026: Anchorpoint Financial Limited (FRS01) and The Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation Limited (FRS02). [S:270f1f7324ab, Register table]
- Both issuers plan Hong Kong dollar coins first. [S:193316443831, Use cases and strategic priorities section]
- On 28 April 2026 both licensees confirmed they had not yet issued regulated stablecoins. [S:275e7319a841, paras 2-3]
- The HKMA expected launch between mid-2026 and the second half of 2026, based on the issuers' plans. [S:193316443831, Follow-up work, para 1]
- The Government said any further licences will stay very limited in number. [S:3f0fcc104e61, Reply (2)]

## Industry benchmarks

- The Bank for International Settlements (BIS) gave a global, reported market figure in its 2026 Annual Economic Report. It put stablecoin value at about US$320 billion at end-May 2026. [I:efa37f6d77e9, p. 88]
- A 2025 BIS Bulletin, also global in scope, reported that two issuers held about 90% of stablecoin value in mid-2025. [I:2f192e0e1c86, p. 2, Graph 1.B]
- Circle Internet Group, a US-listed issuer, reported in its own results filing US$733 million of reserve income for the fourth quarter of 2025. It reported US$461 million of distribution, transaction and other costs for the same quarter. [I:c835a8109c3f, Fourth Quarter 2025 Financial Highlights]
- These are global reported figures, not Hong Kong figures.

## Analysis

> **Analysis — not official**
> **Question:** Which stablecoin role could suit a bank that does not issue a coin?
> **How to think about it:** List each role by what it earns and what it uses up. Reserve banking earns a deposit margin, but the money can leave fast when holders redeem. Selling and dealing earn fees per trade and need conduct and AML controls. Lending earns interest and uses capital.
> **What it depends on:** Whether licensed coins launch and circulate. How reserve deposit terms fit liquidity limits. Client demand for payment uses. The cost of monitoring transfers.
> **Official signposts:** Launch news, which the HKMA tied to the issuers' plans. [S:193316443831, Follow-up work, para 1] Any further licences. [S:3f0fcc104e61, Reply (2)] SFC steps to promote trading of licensed coins on licensed platforms and their use to settle tokenised money market funds. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 49(iii)]
> **What this is not:** Not a forecast, not advice, and not the HKMA's or SFC's view.
