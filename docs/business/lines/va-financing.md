# Virtual-asset financing and margin

A bank lends to its margin clients so they can buy virtual assets, and may accept some virtual assets as collateral. [S:e64b7b0267b2, items (1) and (4), pp.2-4]

| Client segments | Private banking, retail, corporate |
| Value chain | Finance, Trade |

## What the bank does

- Since 27 May 2026, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) allows registered institutions that provide VA dealing to offer VA financing. They are expected to meet standards similar to those of the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC). [S:e64b7b0267b2, HKMA's guidance for RIs, p.2]
- Before this change, intermediaries could not lend clients money to buy virtual assets. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 2]
- The bank lends based on the client's financial capability and the securities or VA collateral the client posts. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (1), p.2]
- Only the bank's existing securities margin clients can borrow. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (2), p.2]
- The bank should first weigh carefully whether relying on VA collateral is prudent. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (4), p.3] If it does, it should accept only bitcoin, ether and Relevant Stablecoins. These are stablecoins issued under an HKMA licence. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (4), pp.3-4]
- Since 2022 the HKMA has said it does not, for now, intend to stop banks lending against virtual assets. This rests on adequate risk controls being in place. [S:642680fa4467, Prudential supervision section, p.3]

## Revenue levers

- Clients pay interest on the loan, usually set as a margin above the bank's own funding cost. (concept)
- Borrowed money lets clients trade more, which can add dealing commission for the same bank. (concept)
- The SFC says financing lets margin clients with strong credit and collateral trade more, which can improve market liquidity. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 3]
- The bank is not expected to raise a client's credit limit just because VA financing is now available. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (3), p.2]

## Cost and capital drivers

- The haircut on bitcoin and ether collateral should be 60% or more. A haircut is a cut to the collateral's value for lending. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (4) and fn 5, p.4] So HK$100 of bitcoin counts as no more than HK$40 of collateral value. [S:e64b7b0267b2, fn 5, p.4]
- Loans backed only by virtual assets must be treated as clean (unsecured) loans. This is because the Banking (Capital) Rules do not recognise VA collateral. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (9), p.5]
- The bank should not repledge or reuse VA collateral, except to enforce it when a client defaults. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (7)(c), p.5] Repledging means a lender reuses collateral to raise its own funding. (concept)
- If the bank counts a client's VA holdings as wealth, its review should cover seven risk factors. These include price gaps and close links with share prices under stress. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (3)(a)-(g), p.3]
- The bank should track VA collateral prices in real time. It should also keep a plan for a platform outage. [S:e64b7b0267b2, items (5)(b) and (6), p.4]
- HKMA liquidity rules treat cash loans secured on cryptoassets in line with similar transactions. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 10(b)]

## Official signals

- The SFC called VA financing a first step under Pillar P (Products) of its virtual-asset roadmap (ASPIRe). [S:73dac3a5a832, para 3]
- The roadmap lists margin financing and VA borrowing and lending among ideas the SFC plans to explore or consider. [S:d461c2db6af2, Pillar P, Initiatives 7-8]
- The SFC plans to consult on capital rules for VA activities, including financing, and gives no date. Until then, SFC-licensed brokers give VA collateral a 100% haircut in their capital calculations. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 14]
- The HKMA may revise the haircut with prior notice. It sets no number for Relevant Stablecoins. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (4) and fn 5, p.4]
- The SFC's framework for perpetual contracts (futures with no end date) on trading platforms says they should be offered only to professional investors. [S:49f642bdee28, Eligible investors section] Margin should be cash, or HKMA-regulated stablecoins or tokenised deposits. [S:49f642bdee28, Margin arrangements section]

## Industry benchmarks

- A September 2022 study by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS), a global standard setter, covered 19 banks at end-2021. [I:d89ae309f669, pages 104-105, section 2.3] Its "holdings and lending" category, which includes lending to crypto firms, made up about 4% of their reported crypto exposures. [I:d89ae309f669, pages 104-105, section 2.3] This is reported data from a small global sample, with no Hong Kong split.

No reliable industry benchmark was found for Hong Kong.

## Analysis

> **Analysis — not official**
> **Question:** How much lending does a client's bitcoin support, and what does that lending cost the bank in capital?
> **How to think about it:** Look at two tests side by side. The collateral test sets how much the bank can lend after the haircut. The capital test asks how much capital each loan uses. A loan backed only by virtual assets counts as unsecured for capital. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (9), p.5] So the mix of securities and VA collateral in each account shapes the return on capital. For example, HK$10 million of bitcoin would count as at most HK$4 million of collateral, and a loan against it alone counts as unsecured (illustrative).
> **What it depends on:** Demand among existing margin clients. The share of VA collateral against securities. How fast prices move and margin calls are met. Any change to the haircut or to capital rules.
> **Official signposts:** Any HKMA revision of the haircut. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (4), p.4] The SFC's capital consultation. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 14] The SFC's work on VA borrowing and lending. [S:d461c2db6af2, Pillar P, Initiative 8]
> **What this is not:** Not a forecast, not advice and not the regulators' view.
