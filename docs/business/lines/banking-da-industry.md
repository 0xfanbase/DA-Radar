# Banking the digital-asset industry

In this line a bank provides accounts, payments and safekeeping to firms that work with digital assets, such as trading platforms and stablecoin issuers (concept).

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Bank role | Account bank; client-money bank; reserve bank and custodian; payment provider |
| Client segments | Institutional, fintechs |
| Value chain | Hold, Settle and pay |
| Regulatory gate | The bank's existing authorisation plus adequate risk controls [S:642680fa4467, Prudential supervision section, p.3]. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) expects banks to discuss virtual-asset (VA) plans with it, and get its feedback, before launch [S:642680fa4467, AIs' VA-related proposals section, p.5] |
| Status as of 25 Sep 2026 | In force [S:642680fa4467, Prudential supervision section, p.3] |
| Related modules | D3, C7, C4, B2 |
| Related cases | 3-stablecoin-reserve-distributor, 4-vatp-incident |

## What the bank does

- Holds client money for trading platforms. The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) expects each licensed VA trading platform (VATP) to keep client money in separate accounts [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.11(a)]. The platform's associated entity (the linked company that holds client assets) should open these accounts [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.11(a)]. They sit with a Hong Kong authorized financial institution, or an overseas bank the SFC agrees to [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.11(a)]. Within one business day, client money received should be paid into such an account or paid out as the client directs [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.11(b)].
- Holds stablecoin reserves. The HKMA expects a licensed stablecoin issuer to appoint qualified custodians for its reserves [S:240be6833157, para 2.5.4]. The issuer should use a licensed bank, or another custodian set-up that the HKMA accepts [S:240be6833157, para 2.5.4]. Reserves may include bank deposits with a term of up to three months [S:240be6833157, para 2.3.1].
- Runs payments for customers who buy virtual assets (VAs). In January 2022 the HKMA said it did not then intend to stop this [S:642680fa4467, Prudential supervision section, p.3]. The condition is adequate risk controls with senior management oversight [S:642680fa4467, Prudential supervision section, p.3].
- Moves VAs for customers. The HKMA says the anti-money-laundering (AML) law allows this if the transfer rules in section 13A of its Schedule 2 are met [S:16fd7cfab136, Part II, para 4.3].

## Revenue levers

- Account and payment fees are charged per transaction or per month on operating accounts (concept).
- Deposit balances earn the bank net interest margin: the gap between what it earns on the money and what it pays the depositor (concept).
- Reserve custody earns a fee, usually a small yearly share of the assets held (concept).
- Foreign-exchange conversion on payments in and out of platforms adds a spread, the gap between buying and selling rates (concept).

## Cost and capital drivers

- Customer due diligence (CDD): the HKMA expects a risk assessment of each client that is a VA service provider (VASP) [S:642680fa4467, AML/CFT section (b), p.4]. Checks may cover its business model, its licence status and its AML controls [S:642680fa4467, AML/CFT section (b), p.4].
- Overseas clients: for a VASP outside Hong Kong, the bank should apply its correspondent-banking checks (extra checks used for banks that serve other banks) [S:7bfdad704174, para 11.23]. It should find out whether the account serves the VASP itself or the VASP's own customers [S:7bfdad704174, para 11.24]. It could consider asking for separate accounts for the two [S:7bfdad704174, para 11.25].
- Monitoring: the HKMA expects banks to watch for frequent transfers to or from VA platforms [S:642680fa4467, AML/CFT section (a), pp.3-4]. Where there are grounds for suspicion, banks should file a report [S:642680fa4467, AML/CFT section (a), pp.3-4].
- Scam money: the SFC cites police intelligence on scam money moved by bank transfer to licensed firms [S:be46918f8aa2, para 8]. Some is turned into VAs and withdrawn to unhosted wallets (wallets not held by a regulated firm) [S:be46918f8aa2, para 8].
- Direct debits: the SFC has flagged risks in simplified electronic Direct Debit Authorisation (eDDA) set-ups used by licensed firms to collect client deposits [S:cfc4a0d9f1be, para 1].

## Official signals

- The SFC listed 13 licensed trading platforms at its 29 May 2026 update [S:b14be2203d39, Licensed platforms table; Last update line].
- The HKMA licensed two stablecoin issuers from a first batch of 36 applicants [S:193316443831, Assessment of licence applications, paras 1-2].
- The Government told the Legislative Council in February 2024 that VA-related crime cases rose from 1,397 in 2021 to 3,415 in 2023 [S:4bd22ccf481d, Reply (1)-(2)]. Money involved rose from HK$824 million to HK$4,398 million [S:4bd22ccf481d, Reply (1)-(2)].
- The HKMA warned in November 2024 that some overseas crypto firms called themselves banks without a Hong Kong licence [S:19c8611c621a, paras 1-2].
- The SFC plans to switch on the AML surveillance part of its crypto data initiative (CrypTech) in 2027 [S:ef3648ff1610, para 51(iii)].
- The Customs and Excise Department plans a Digital Asset Transaction Analytics System by 2029 [S:ef3648ff1610, Annex (I) New Indicators, item 209].

## Industry benchmarks

- OSL Group Limited (OSL), a listed digital-asset firm, reported in its interim results for the first half of 2026 (Hong Kong and global, reported figures) [I:23e4b81d8271, PDF p.30]. Its payment volume was about HK$61.0 billion, against HK$36.9 billion a year earlier [I:23e4b81d8271, PDF p.30].
- The accounting firm KPMG (China, Hong Kong office) reviewed Hong Kong banks' 2025 accounts in its Hong Kong Banking Report 2026 (reported figures) [I:1dfc7a0205ff, Performance section, page 8]. Surveyed banks spent on average 41.9% of their income on running costs (the cost-to-income ratio) [I:1dfc7a0205ff, Performance section, page 8]. Staff costs rose 6.7% [I:1dfc7a0205ff, Performance section, page 8].
- The same KPMG report (a consultancy view) says many local and regional banks are waiting before building digital-asset capability [I:1dfc7a0205ff, page 42]. It notes that, in the short term, spending on technology, people and compliance can look like cost rather than value [I:1dfc7a0205ff, page 42].

## Analysis

> **Analysis — not official**
> **Question:** Does banking digital-asset firms pay for the extra checks it needs?
> **How to think about it:**
> - Income side: deposit balances, payment fees and reserve custody fees from a small number of large clients.
> - Cost side: enhanced due diligence, transaction monitoring and reporting for every client, much of it fixed.
> - Concentration: a few clients can bring most of the balances, so losing one client moves the whole result.
> - Example (illustrative): if 2 of 10 platform clients hold most of the deposits, one exit changes the whole result.
> **What it depends on:** the number of licensed platforms and issuers; how much client money and reserves each keeps at banks; fraud and scam trends; monitoring tools.
> **Official signposts:** the SFC platform list [S:b14be2203d39, Licensed platforms table]; the HKMA's stance on further stablecoin licences [S:193316443831, Follow-up work section, third paragraph]; SFC AML surveillance plans [S:ef3648ff1610, para 51(iii)].
> **What this is not:** Not a forecast, not advice, and not the regulator's view.
