# Trade finance and electronic bills of lading

In this line a bank finances trade using digital records, such as shared cargo data, electronic trade documents and tokenised money. (concept)

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Bank role | Trade financier; payment bank; data user; pilot participant |
| Client segments | Corporate, small and medium-sized firms, institutional |
| Value chain | Finance, Settle and pay |
| Regulatory gate | Banks placing customer tokenised deposits should follow the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) tokenised-products circular [S:b2719f3dcb37, fn 1, p.1]. They should discuss plans with the HKMA first [S:b2719f3dcb37, Implementation, p.6] |
| Status as of 25 Sep 2026 | Exploratory [S:eb28508e5032, para 2] |
| Related modules | E2, C5, C4, B2 |
| Related cases | none |

## What the bank does

- Tests trade use cases in Project Ensemble, the HKMA's tokenisation project. Its sandbox has a trade theme covering supply chain finance and trade payments [S:5f8362ecd97f, p.2].
- Draws on cargo data through Project Cargo x, an HKMA trial with private firms [S:cc2fa9fc0772, para 110(i) and fn 8]. The trial uses the Commercial Data Interchange (CDI), an HKMA data-sharing system [S:cc2fa9fc0772, para 110(i)]. It aims to cut the credit costs and handling time that banks and small firms face [S:cc2fa9fc0772, para 110(i)].
- Explores electronic bills of lading (eBL), the shipping documents that give control of goods. Under a March 2026 agreement with Shanghai bodies, the HKMA is to explore eBL use under Project Ensemble [S:eb28508e5032, para 2].
- Pays in tokenised deposits for corporate clients. For the issuing bank, the HKMA says such deposits should get the operational-deposit liquidity treatment. This applies where they are mainly a means of payment within an operational relationship with wholesale clients [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4].

## Revenue levers

- A trade financier earns interest on the funds it advances and fees for handling documents. (concept)
- Trusted shipment data can let a bank lend with less manual checking, which lowers cost per deal. (concept)
- Payment and foreign exchange fees come with the trade flow the bank finances. (concept)
- Programmable payments release money when agreed conditions are met, such as proof of delivery. (concept)

## Cost and capital drivers

- Controls: for tokenised products, the HKMA expects due diligence, disclosure and risk management [S:b2719f3dcb37, p.3; sections (A)-(C), pp.3-6].
- Compliance: in the 2022 mBridge report, each bank ran its own anti-money laundering and sanctions checks off the cross-border platform [S:947fa49606ab, s.6.5.2, p.30]. Each bank did so before sending or confirming a payment [S:947fa49606ab, s.6.5.2, p.30].
- Technology: links to the CDI, the Port Community System and partner ledgers need building and testing. (concept)
- Legal: a lender needs the law to treat a digital document like the paper one. (concept) The 2026 Policy Address states the Government will introduce a bill on digital business-to-business trade documents in 2026 [S:ef3648ff1610, para 98].

## Official signals

- The Port Community System (a shared logistics data platform) had over 8,000 registered firms in 2026 [S:ef3648ff1610, para 62]. Firms had made more than 20 successful trade-finance applications using its cargo-flow data [S:ef3648ff1610, para 62].
- The HKMA is to study trade-finance uses of tokenised deposits with the Mainland, with pilot deals done by end-2026 [S:ef3648ff1610, para 99].
- The Trade Single Window is to connect with the CDI in mid-2027, so banks can refer to trade data on finance applications [S:ef3648ff1610, para 97].
- The HKMA and Banco Central do Brasil agreed in 2024 to link their pilot platforms to test settlement uses, including trade finance [S:8d9f12b43ada, para 1].
- The 2025 Policy Address said the HKMA and Banco Central do Brasil would test eBL and tokenised deposits for commodity trade [S:cc2fa9fc0772, para 111(iii)].
- In 2024 the HKMA and the Bank of Thailand agreed to explore cross-border tokenisation uses, including trade payments [S:fcea64733871, para 3].

## Analysis

> **Analysis — not official**
> **Question:** Does digital trade finance mainly cut a bank's cost per deal, or does it open lending to firms the bank could not serve before?
> **How to think about it:**
> - Cost view: data and digital documents replace manual checks. Savings depend on how many deals move off paper.
> - Reach view: trusted cargo data may make small firms creditworthy on evidence the bank never had.
> - Settlement view: tokenised deposits and conditional payments tie money to shipment events. In this view, the bank earns on payment flow as well as on credit.
> **What it depends on:** legal recognition of electronic documents; how many shippers and banks join shared data systems; links with Mainland and overseas platforms.
> **Official signposts:** the trade-documents bill [S:ef3648ff1610, para 98]; tokenised-deposit trade pilots [S:ef3648ff1610, para 99]; the Trade Single Window link to the CDI [S:ef3648ff1610, para 97].
> **What this is not:** Not a forecast, not advice, and not the regulator's view.
