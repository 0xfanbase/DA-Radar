# Virtual-asset distribution and brokerage

In this line a bank sells clients products linked to virtual assets (VAs), such as funds that invest in them [S:8a016a912528, para 5]. It may also buy and sell VAs for clients through a licensed trading platform [S:8a016a912528, para 17].

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Bank role | Distributor; broker through a licensed platform; stablecoin offeror |
| Client segments | Retail, private banking, corporate, institutional |
| Value chain | Distribute, Trade |
| Regulatory gate | Registration with the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) for dealing in securities, plus SFC conditions for virtual asset (VA) dealing [S:8a016a912528, paras 18-19 and fn 22] |
| Status as of 25 Sep 2026 | In force [S:8a016a912528, paras 17-19] |
| Related modules | C1, C2, C4, C5, C7, D3 |

## What the bank does

- Sells SFC-authorised VA funds to retail clients as well as to professional investors (PIs) [S:8a016a912528, para 9].
- Offers other complex VA products to PIs only, as the regulators expect, apart from a small set of listed funds and derivatives [S:8a016a912528, para 6.1].
- Deals in VAs through a licensed platform, either by introducing clients or through an omnibus account (one pooled account in the bank's name) [S:8a016a912528, para 17].
- Since May 2026, may serve retail omnibus clients through platforms otherwise limited to PIs, for tokens admitted for retail trading [S:a0bd48559762, para (1)].
- May execute client trades through a licensed platform's over-the-counter (off-exchange) service [S:b79266f3da51, para 6].
- May offer stablecoins from issuers licensed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) to retail clients and PIs. Banks are one of five kinds of permitted offeror [S:7b6c8dc63140, Reply (1), para 1].
- Distributes tokenised SFC-authorised investment products, a role the SFC expects regulated firms such as banks to fill [S:58ea8520974d, para 18]. It can also route client trades in them to a licensed platform as a "Connecting Broker" [S:a64e364fd912, para 13].

## Revenue levers

- A fund distributor may receive part of the manager's management fee, often called a trailer fee. (concept)
- A broker earns commission or a price spread on each trade. Its take rate is that income as a share of trading value. (concept)
- The bank's margin on brokerage is its client fee minus what the platform charges it. (concept)
- The 2023 joint circular expects client orders to be pre-funded, meaning paid for before the trade [S:8a016a912528, fn 25]. Since 27 May 2026, the HKMA lets banks that deal in VAs lend to their securities margin clients for VA trades, under set standards [S:e64b7b0267b2, p.2 and items (1)-(2)]. Cash held for trades can bring deposit and currency conversion income. (concept)

## Cost and capital drivers

- Suitability: regulators treat VA products as very likely complex, so suitability checks apply even without a recommendation [S:8a016a912528, para 5]. Some exchange-traded products are exceptions [S:8a016a912528, para 5].
- Knowledge test: apart from institutional and qualified corporate PIs, clients should be checked for VA knowledge first [S:8a016a912528, para 6.2]. Those without it may trade only after training [S:8a016a912528, para 6.2].
- Retail limits: before VA dealing for retail clients, the bank should set a reasonable exposure limit for each one [S:8a016a912528, para 20(a)].
- Product checks: distributors should do due diligence on each VA product, with extra checks for funds the SFC has not authorised [S:8a016a912528, para 12.3].
- Enforcement: in January 2026 the SFC fined a licensed broker HK$4 million over VA product sales [S:415e4629a80f, para 1]. Between 2018 and 2022, retail clients had traded products meant for PIs only, without a knowledge check [S:415e4629a80f, paras 2-4].
- Stablecoins: for coins from HKMA-licensed issuers, the bank should disclose how each coin keeps its value and how redemption works [S:a0bd48559762, para (5)]. Commission rebates should not be the main reason to recommend one [S:a0bd48559762, para (8)].
- Notice: the bank should tell the SFC and the HKMA before it changes its VA activities, such as serving new client types [S:b79266f3da51, para 10].
- People: the bank needs at least two executive officers for each regulated activity, each approved in writing by the HKMA [S:8934af8cc884, para 2.2.4].

## Official signals

- Hong Kong had 11 VA spot exchange-traded funds (ETFs) at end-2025, worth over HK$5.4 billion [S:c28638fc5ccc, para 4].
- In March 2026, 13 tokenised products were available to the public, with about HK$10.7 billion in their tokenised classes [S:bbd10edd41cf, para 3].
- The SFC listed 13 licensed VA trading platforms at its 29 May 2026 update [S:b14be2203d39, Licensed platforms table; Last update line].
- In June 2026 the Government said regulated stablecoins were expected to launch between mid-2026 and the second half of 2026 [S:c6df52e680de, Reply, opening para 2]. It based this on the two licensed issuers' business plans [S:c6df52e680de, Reply, opening para 2].
- The 2026 Policy Address says the SFC will improve its rules so tokenised gold can trade on licensed platforms [S:ef3648ff1610, para 49(ii)].
- Banks already dealing in VAs are to get faster approval under the planned dealing regime [S:6186c01c6649, paras 35-36].

## Industry benchmarks

- HashKey Holdings Limited (HashKey), 2025 annual results (Hong Kong and other markets, reported): trading by omnibus clients rose to HK$86.4 billion in 2025 [I:786f45f6ea11, PDF p.4]. That was up from HK$11.7 billion in 2024 [I:786f45f6ea11, PDF p.4].
- Bosera Asset Management (International) reported a fee in its March 2026 Hong Kong fund prospectus [I:d81073907a14, PDF p.96]. Its bitcoin fund currently charges a 0.60% yearly management fee [I:d81073907a14, PDF p.96].
- The same prospectus says the manager may pay distributors a fee out of that management fee [I:d81073907a14, PDF p.24].

## Analysis

> **Analysis — not official**
> **Question:** How do selling VA funds and running a VA brokerage service differ as ways to earn from client demand?
> **How to think about it:**
> - Fund sales earn a share of recurring fees. They reuse existing sales, suitability and custody set-ups.
> - Brokerage earns per trade. It needs a platform link, pre-funding, per-client limits and VA custody.
> - Both need VA knowledge checks for most clients other than institutional investors [S:8a016a912528, para 6.2].
> **What it depends on:** client mix; how often clients trade; platform charges; the size of the product shelf.
> **Official signposts:** the launch of regulated stablecoins [S:c6df52e680de, Reply, opening para 2]; the tokenised product framework [S:ef3648ff1610, para 49(ii)]; faster approval under the dealing regime [S:6186c01c6649, paras 35-36].
> **What this is not:** Not a forecast, not advice, and not the regulator's view.
