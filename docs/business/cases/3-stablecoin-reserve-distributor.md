# Case 3: The reserve bank that also sells the coin

*Fictional case. All people and firms are invented. Facts about Hong Kong rules are real and cited.*

## The situation

Gullstone Quay Bank is a fictional, mid-sized Hong Kong bank. It is a registered institution (RI), meaning a bank registered with the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) for securities business. Driftmoth Digital Money Limited is a fictional stablecoin issuer licensed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA). A stablecoin is a token meant to keep a steady value against a currency. Driftmoth plans a Hong Kong dollar coin.

Driftmoth asks Gullstone for two things. First, hold a large part of its reserves (the assets that back every coin) as deposits. Second, sell the coin to Gullstone's clients. You are head of compliance (digital assets). The chief executive (CEO) wants one joined-up answer by the next board meeting.

## Cast

- **Marisol Mistlecove, CEO.** Sees a new deposit source and a new product in one deal.
- **Arlo Quayburrow, chief financial officer (CFO).** Owns the funding plan and the profit and loss (P&L) account.
- **Greta Gullwick, chief risk officer (CRO).** Owns liquidity and concentration limits.
- **Dorian Driftholme, head of wealth.** Wants to offer the coin to retail and private banking clients.
- **Driftmoth Digital Money Limited.** The fictional licensed issuer and prospective client.
- **Brightkeel Analytics.** A fictional vendor selling blockchain monitoring tools.
- **The supervisor.** The HKMA case team. It acts only as its published guidance says.
- **You.** Head of compliance (digital assets).

## Exhibit A: the rules that apply

As of 25 Sep 2026.

- Reserve assets should be held in listed forms. [S:240be6833157, para 2.3.1] These include cash, bank deposits with a term of up to three months and certain short-dated public-sector debt. [S:240be6833157, para 2.3.1]
- An issuer should appoint qualified custodians for its reserves. Each custodian should be either a licensed bank or another custodian that the HKMA accepts. [S:240be6833157, para 2.5.4]
- Reserves should sit under trust arrangements that keep them apart from the issuer's own assets. [S:240be6833157, para 2.5.2]
- In June 2026 the Government said HKMA rules have issuers place eligible reserve assets with banks in Hong Kong. [S:c6df52e680de, Reply (1)]
- Holders must be able to redeem at par (face value). [S:240be6833157, para 3.3.1] Valid requests should be processed within one business day unless the HKMA approves otherwise. [S:240be6833157, para 3.3.3]
- An issuer should set internal limits on its credit exposure to each counterparty, including banks holding its reserves. [S:240be6833157, para 6.4.2] Its liquidity planning should weigh deposit terms, early withdrawal options and concentration. [S:240be6833157, para 6.4.3]
- Authorized institutions (banks) are one of five kinds of "permitted offerors". They may offer licensed coins to retail and professional investors. [S:7b6c8dc63140, Reply (1)]
- An issuer using outside distributors should check they are permitted offerors and should assess their governance and controls. [S:240be6833157, para 3.4.2]
- For a bank's customers that are virtual asset service providers (VASPs), the HKMA expects a risk assessment of each one. [S:642680fa4467, AML/CFT section (b), p.4] Depending on the relationship, a bank may need extra checks on the firm's business, its licence status and its anti-money laundering (AML) controls. [S:642680fa4467, AML/CFT section (b), p.4]
- The HKMA asks banks to discuss virtual asset plans with it before launch. [S:642680fa4467, AIs' VA-related proposals section, p.5]

## Act 1: How much of the reserve to take

Driftmoth wants to place most of its reserve with Gullstone as call deposits (money it can withdraw on demand). Arlo likes the balance. Greta notes that one client would then be the bank's largest depositor. Every redemption pulls money out of the same account.

### Decision 1: On what terms does Gullstone take the reserve deposits?

- **A.** Take the whole amount on call, at a low rate, and hold matching liquid assets.
- **B.** Take a capped share, split between call money and term deposits of up to three months.
- **C.** Decline the deposits and offer only the distribution service.

### Discussion notes

- Trade-offs. Option A brings the largest balance but the most run risk. Option B earns less on paper but may be steadier. Option C avoids concentration but may weaken the case for the distribution deal.
- What the rules require. Reserve deposits should have a term of no more than three months [S:240be6833157, para 2.3.1]. Driftmoth itself should set and enforce limits on its credit exposure to each bank [S:240be6833157, para 6.4.2]. Those limits could rule out placing the whole reserve with one bank.
- Redemption pace matters. The HKMA expects Driftmoth to process valid requests within one business day, unless it approves otherwise [S:240be6833157, para 3.3.3]. A strong answer asks whether Gullstone's liquidity plan can match that pace.
- A strong answer covers the trust structure. The reserve belongs under a trust for holders, not in Driftmoth's own account [S:240be6833157, para 2.5.2].
- A strong answer also tests a stress day, such as a large redemption wave alongside a market shock (concept).
- Trap: treating the reserve as sticky retail money. It behaves more like wholesale funding from one client (concept).

## Act 2: Driftmoth as a client

Brightkeel pitches a monitoring tool that traces coin transfers on the blockchain. Dorian says onboarding is a formality because Driftmoth is licensed. You are less sure.

### Decision 2: How does Gullstone onboard Driftmoth?

- **A.** Onboard with standard corporate checks, relying on the HKMA licence.
- **B.** Onboard with the extra checks used for VASPs, plus blockchain monitoring from Brightkeel.
- **C.** Onboard for the reserve account only, and keep payment flows out until controls are tested.

### Discussion notes

- Trade-offs. Option A is fast and cheap. Option B costs more but gives a fuller picture. Option C limits risk but also limits income.
- What the rules require. The HKMA expects a risk-based assessment for VASPs that bank with it [S:642680fa4467, AML/CFT section (b), p.4]. Depending on the relationship, extra checks may cover the firm's business model, licence status and AML controls [S:642680fa4467, AML/CFT section (b), p.4].
- Driftmoth has its own duties. The HKMA expects it to check customers before an occasional transaction of HK$8,000 or more, such as an issue or redemption [S:4d98c6a80b4d, para 4.4]. When it sends a stablecoin transfer of HK$8,000 or more, it must first obtain and record the required details on the sender and recipient [S:4d98c6a80b4d, para 6.5].
- Ongoing monitoring counts too. The HKMA expects banks to watch for frequent transfers to or from virtual asset platforms [S:642680fa4467, AML/CFT section (a), pp.3-4]. Where there are grounds for suspicion, banks should file a suspicious transaction report [S:642680fa4467, AML/CFT section (a), pp.3-4].
- A strong answer asks what a licence proves and what it does not. A licence shows the issuer met the licensing tests. It does not replace the bank's own view of risk (concept).
- A strong answer also tests the vendor. The questions cover data coverage, false alerts and exit terms (concept).
- Trap: letting the value of the deal set the risk appetite.

## Act 3: Selling the coin

Dorian wants to sell the coin to all wealth clients at launch. In April 2026 the HKMA linked launch timing to the issuers' own business plans. [S:193316443831, Follow-up work, para 1] The supervisor has asked banks to discuss virtual asset plans with it before launch. [S:642680fa4467, AIs' VA-related proposals section, p.5] Gullstone books a meeting to hear the supervisor's feedback on its controls.

### Decision 3: How does Gullstone launch distribution?

- **A.** Launch to retail and private banking clients at once, using stablecoin-only accounts.
- **B.** Launch to professional investors first, then widen after a review.
- **C.** Offer the coin only through an omnibus account (one pooled account) at a licensed trading platform.

### Discussion notes

- Trade-offs. Option A reaches the most clients and brings the most conduct risk. Option B is slower but lets controls settle. Option C uses the platform's systems but shares the fee.
- What the rules allow. An RI need not test the virtual asset knowledge of clients in accounts limited to licensed coins [S:a0bd48559762, para (3)(a), p.4]. It should still consider each such client's reason for trading [S:a0bd48559762, para (3)(a), p.4].
- An RI can partner with the coin's issuer or with an SFC-licensed trading platform to offer dealing in a licensed coin [S:a0bd48559762, para (1), p.3]. It should disclose how the coin keeps its value and how redemption works [S:a0bd48559762, para (5), p.5].
- Timing with regulators. Intermediaries are to tell the SFC in advance, and the HKMA where relevant, before starting virtual asset activities [S:8a016a912528, para 31]. The same applies to later changes, including the type of clients served [S:8a016a912528, para 31].
- A strong answer names the conflict. The bank earns on reserve deposits and on sales of the same coin. The sales team may push volume that also swells the deposit (concept).
- A strong answer checks the issuer's side. The HKMA expects Driftmoth to confirm that Gullstone is a permitted offeror and to review its controls [S:240be6833157, para 3.4.2].
- Trap: forgetting that clients who redeem pull money from the reserve that Gullstone holds (concept).

## What this case teaches

- A reserve deposit is funding with a direct link to redemptions. Redemptions can pull such money out quickly (concept).
- The issuer's own limits shape the deal. Driftmoth should set limits on its exposure to each bank. [S:240be6833157, para 6.4.2]
- A licence does not end the bank's due diligence. The HKMA expects a risk assessment for VASPs that bank with it. [S:642680fa4467, AML/CFT section (b), p.4]
- Two roles with one client create a conflict of interest (concept).
