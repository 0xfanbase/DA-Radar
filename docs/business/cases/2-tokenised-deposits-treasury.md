# Case 2: Tokenised deposits for corporate treasury

*Fictional case. All people and firms are invented. Facts about Hong Kong rules are real and cited.*

## The situation

A tokenised deposit is an ordinary bank deposit recorded as a token on a shared digital ledger. (concept) The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) started EnsembleTX (the real-value pilot of Project Ensemble) on 13 November 2025. [S:8b67e8dc510f, para 1] Its first uses include managing liquidity and treasury needs in real time. [S:8b67e8dc510f, para 2] The HKMA invites anyone with a real-value use for tokenised deposits to take part. [S:8b67e8dc510f, para 5]

In June 2026 the HKMA held a seminar on tokenisation for corporate treasurers. [S:05369e6084ea, para 1] It said it would follow up with corporate treasury centres (CTCs) that showed interest. [S:05369e6084ea, para 4] Each of these centres is a company unit that manages cash for a whole group. (concept)

Saltmarsh Harbour Bank is a fictional, mid-sized Hong Kong bank. It does not yet offer tokenised deposits. Its largest corporate client, the fictional Pemberwick Tidewater Group, runs its Asian cash from a treasury centre in Hong Kong. The group wants to move cash between its companies at any hour. It asks whether Saltmarsh can do this with tokens. You are head of compliance for digital assets. The chief executive asks you to steer the proposal.

## Cast

- **You**: head of compliance (digital assets).
- **Imogen Quillbrook**: chief executive (CEO). She wants to keep Pemberwick's business.
- **Barnaby Saltglen**: chief financial officer (CFO). He owns the profit and loss account (P&L), the record of income and costs.
- **Petra Tidewhistle**: treasurer. She runs asset-liability management (ALM): matching the bank's funding to its loans and investments.
- **Gideon Harrowmere**: chief risk officer (CRO).
- **Nadia Lanternby**: head of corporate banking. She owns the client relationship.
- **Brightspar Ledger Works**: a fictional vendor selling a ready-made token platform.
- **The supervisor**: the bank's HKMA case team. It acts only as the HKMA's published expectations describe.

## Exhibit A: the rules that apply

- When customers place tokenised deposits, the HKMA says banks should also meet its standards for tokenised products. [S:b2719f3dcb37, p.1 fn 1]
- Those standards expect due diligence on vendors, such as tokenisation platform providers. [S:b2719f3dcb37, section (A), p.3]
- They expect clear disclosure of risks, including doubt about when settlement becomes final. [S:b2719f3dcb37, section (B), pp.4-5]
- Before starting, a bank is expected to have controls in place and discuss the plan with the HKMA. [S:b2719f3dcb37, Implementation, p.6]
- The HKMA's liquidity guidance says when a bank that issues qualifying tokens may count them as deposits or unsecured funding. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4]
- The HKMA's note on distributed ledger technology (DLT) expects the board and senior management to own DLT risks in full. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 1, p.2] The note is non-binding. [S:1884530ff235, Introduction, p.1]
- The Supervisory Incubator for DLT lets a bank check its risk controls with a dedicated HKMA team before a full launch. [S:1b1a0de5de5a, para 3]

## Act 1: Is there real client demand?

Nadia says Pemberwick is keen. Barnaby asks whether one client makes a product. Petra notes that Pemberwick already sweeps cash each evening through normal accounts.

### Decision 1: Which first use does the proposal put forward?

- **A.** Round-the-clock cash moves between Pemberwick's own companies, all banking with Saltmarsh.
- **B.** Paying for tokenised money market fund (MMF) trades, where corporate cash sits between uses. An MMF invests in short-term, low-risk debt. (concept)
- **C.** No product yet: join the HKMA's follow-up with CTCs and survey ten corporate clients first.

### Discussion notes

- **Trade-offs.** Option A serves a named client fast, but only inside one bank. Option B matches an initial EnsembleTX focus, but needs fund partners. Option C builds evidence and delays income.
- **What the rules require.** EnsembleTX's first focus covers both tokenised MMF deals and real-time treasury uses. [S:8b67e8dc510f, para 2] Interbank settlement of these deals first runs through the Hong Kong dollar Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system. [S:8b67e8dc510f, para 3] The HKMA plans settlement in central bank digital currency (CBDC) and 24/7 running of EnsembleTX by around end-2026. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 50] The HKMA has noted bank views that tokens usable only in one bank's network add less value for clients. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 6, p.4]
- **What a strong answer covers.** It asks what Pemberwick cannot do today, and what that gap costs the client. It separates one client's request from a segment's demand. It checks which uses need other banks to connect.
- **Traps.** Treating a seminar audience as signed clients. Treating the pilot as a finished rulebook. Pricing only for Pemberwick.

## Act 2: What happens to the balance sheet?

Barnaby hopes tokenised balances count as sticky funding. Petra warns that the liquidity treatment depends on the design. The liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) checks that a bank holds enough easy-to-sell assets to cover a short stress. (concept)

### Decision 2: How does the token design treat holders?

- **A.** Closed design: only checked Saltmarsh clients can hold tokens, and the bank knows every holder at all times.
- **B.** Open design: tokens can pass to parties the bank cannot always identify, to reach more users.
- **C.** Payments design: tokens exist mainly to make payments within a client's operating account relationship.

### Discussion notes

- **Trade-offs.** Option A keeps control and limits reach. Option B widens reach and loosens knowledge of who holds the tokens. Option C fits treasury use and ties balances to day-to-day payments.
- **What the rules require.** The guidance covers tokens issued by a bank that is subject to the LCR (a "category 1 institution"). [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4] The tokens are a legally binding claim on the bank, and holders can redeem them in fiat money (ordinary government-issued currency) at face value. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4] Their value is kept stable by the bank's own credit, not by a separate pool of assets. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4] Such a bank may treat the tokens as deposits or unsecured funding, subject to conditions. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4] It must set their maturity by the date holders first gain the right to redeem. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4] If the bank can identify holders at all times, outflow rates must follow the earliest redemption date and the holders' type. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4] Even then, the bank must not count the tokens as stable funding from retail or small business customers. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4] If the bank cannot identify holders at all times, it must treat the tokens as other unsecured wholesale funding. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4] For tokens used mainly for payments within an operational relationship with wholesale clients, the guidance says the operational deposit rules should be followed. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4] A bank holding another bank's qualifying tokens must treat them like unsecured loans to financial institutions. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 5]
- **What a strong answer covers.** It models each design's outflow treatment with Petra before pricing. It checks whether balances are new money or moved from existing accounts. It asks how net interest margin changes: the gap between what the bank earns on funds and what it pays depositors. (concept) It links identity checks to the liquidity result.
- **Traps.** Assuming tokens count as retail deposits. Counting moved balances as new funding. Forgetting the bank's own holdings of other banks' tokens.

## Act 3: Build, buy or partner?

Brightspar offers its platform with a discount for a signature this quarter. Gideon asks who controls changes to the ledger. Imogen asks how fast the bank can go live. The supervisor expects a discussion before any launch, as the HKMA's circular sets out. [S:b2719f3dcb37, Implementation, p.6]

### Decision 3: How does Saltmarsh deliver the service?

- **A.** Build its own platform and connect it to EnsembleTX.
- **B.** License Brightspar's platform and run it under the bank's name.
- **C.** Partner with a larger fictional bank, Corvid Point Bank, that already issues tokenised deposits, and refer clients there for now.

### Discussion notes

- **Trade-offs.** Option A gives most control and costs most time and money. Option B is faster and adds reliance on one vendor. Option C costs least, and the deposits sit with another bank.
- **What the rules require.** The tokenised-products circular expects checks on vendors' track record, systems and controls. [S:b2719f3dcb37, section (A), p.3] A bank issuing tokens itself should take those vendor checks into account. [S:b2719f3dcb37, section (A), p.4] The DLT note expects the bank to update technology risk, continuity and outsourcing policies. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 1, p.2] It says shared ownership of a solution could affect a bank's control over changes. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 1 fn 1, p.2] It expects the bank to judge whether node operators, the firms that confirm ledger entries, are reliable. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 5, p.4] It advises banks to consider widely accepted technical standards so systems can link to others. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 6, p.4] The Incubator offers optional live trials, and the HKMA judges any supervisory flexibility case by case. [S:fae36c380174, p.1]
- **What a strong answer covers.** It compares control, speed and cost for each option. It names who fixes the platform in an outage, at any hour. It tests the vendor's deadline against the board and supervisor timetable. It sets an exit plan for each option.
- **Traps.** Letting a discount set the timetable. Treating a vendor's controls as the bank's own. Reading Incubator entry as approval to launch.

## What this case teaches

- EnsembleTX is a pilot with stated plans, not a finished rulebook. [S:8b67e8dc510f, paras 1-3]
- The token's design sets its liquidity treatment. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4]
- Vendor checks stay with the bank that serves the customer. [S:b2719f3dcb37, section (A), p.3]
- Balances moved from old accounts are not new income. (concept)
- Control, speed and cost pull in different directions in build, buy and partner choices. (concept)
