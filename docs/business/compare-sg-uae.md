# Hong Kong, Singapore and Dubai: the rules side by side

This page lets you compare, fact by fact, how three financial centres treat five digital-asset topics. It covers Hong Kong, Singapore and Dubai in the United Arab Emirates (UAE). After reading it, you can explain where the rules line up and where they differ, with the source for each point.

*For general information only. Not legal or regulatory advice. Always check the official source.*

## How to read this page

- Each table puts one fact per cell. Every cell ends with a small source button. Tap it to see the document and the exact paragraph.
- Hong Kong facts link to official Hong Kong sources. Their buttons are solid blue.
- Singapore facts link to documents from the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS), Singapore's central bank and financial regulator.
- Dubai facts link to documents from the Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (VARA), Dubai's regulator for virtual assets.
- MAS and VARA are official in their own countries, but not in Hong Kong. So their buttons have a dotted outline and start with the word *Foreign*.
- Where a source we reviewed gives no fact for a cell, the cell says *Not stated in the sources reviewed*. That does not mean no rule exists.
- Force words follow each source. *Must* or *requires* marks a binding rule. *Should* or *expects* marks guidance. *Stated target* marks a government aim.
- This page compares facts only. It does not rank the three places or say which is better.
- Hong Kong facts are as of 25 Sep 2026. MAS and VARA pages were saved on 26 Sep 2026.

## Words used on this page

- **Virtual asset (VA), plural VAs:** a crypto token such as bitcoin, in the legal sense each regulator uses.
- **VA trading platform (VATP):** a Hong Kong crypto exchange holding a licence from the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC).
- **Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA):** Hong Kong's banking regulator and central bank.
- **Authorized institution (AI):** a bank or deposit-taker authorized by the HKMA.
- **Registered institution (RI), plural RIs:** a bank registered with the SFC to carry on securities business.
- **Virtual asset service provider (VASP), plural VASPs:** a firm licensed by VARA for a VA activity.
- **Digital payment token (DPT):** Singapore's legal term for payment-type crypto tokens.
- **Fiat-referenced virtual asset (FRVA), plural FRVAs:** VARA's term for a stablecoin tied to a government currency.
- **Asset-referenced virtual asset (ARVA):** VARA's term for a token tied to the value of other assets.
- **Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE):** the UAE's federal central bank.
- **UAE dirham (AED):** the UAE's currency.
- **Distributed ledger technology (DLT):** shared digital record-keeping, such as a blockchain.
- **Cold storage:** keeping the keys that move tokens on devices that are not connected to the internet.
- **Tokenisation:** recording an asset, such as a bond or a deposit, as a token on a distributed ledger.

## 1. Trading-platform licensing

| Item | Hong Kong | Singapore (MAS) | UAE / Dubai (VARA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who needs a licence | From 1 June 2023, a centralised VA trading platform operating in Hong Kong has had to seek an SFC licence [S:3d48d4bc2922, opening paragraph, p.1]. | MAS's 2025 media release says firms serving only customers outside Singapore have needed a digital token service provider (DTSP) licence since 30 June 2025 [I:ded457589f4e, para 2]. | VARA's 2023 Regulations (version of 19 May 2025) bar any firm from carrying on a VA activity as a business in Dubai without a VARA licence, unless it is exempt [I:51494a8caf05, Part III.A.1, p.10]. |
| Law used | The platform licence is granted under the Securities and Futures Ordinance, the anti-money-laundering ordinance (Cap. 615), or both [S:3d48d4bc2922, opening paragraph, p.1]. | The same MAS release says firms serving customers in Singapore are regulated under the Payment Services Act 2019, the Securities and Futures Act 2001 or the Financial Advisers Act 2001 [I:ded457589f4e, para 3]. | VARA's Virtual Assets and Related Activities Regulations 2023 require a firm to hold a licence for each VA activity it carries on in Dubai [I:51494a8caf05, Part IV.A.1, p.12]. |
| Licensing stance | Not stated in the sources reviewed. | MAS says it has set a high bar for the offshore-only DTSP licence and will generally not grant one [I:ded457589f4e, para 2]. | VARA's register says a firm holding an in-principle approval (IPA), an early conditional step, may not operate or serve clients until fully licensed [I:da2385bdf350, Register introduction text]. |
| Count | The SFC list showed 13 licensed platforms at its 29 May 2026 update [S:b14be2203d39, Licensed platforms table; Last update line]. | The MAS directory showed 38 firms offering DPT services on 26 Sep 2026, all with Major Payment Institution licences (reported register count; covers all DPT services, not only exchanges) [I:cb4e769d4c67, Directory results counter ('38 result(s)') and Licence Type filter ('Major Payment Institution 38')]. | VARA's public register showed 56 fully licensed VASPs on 26 Sep 2026 (reported register count; covers all VA activities, not only exchanges) [I:da2385bdf350, Register results counter ('56 results found')]. |
| Minimum capital | A platform must hold at least HK$5 million of paid-up share capital at all times [S:f1bbafccd556, para 6.2, p.36]. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. |
| Retail clients | A platform should assess each investor's VA knowledge before opening an account. Institutional investors and qualified corporate professional investors are exempt [S:f1bbafccd556, para 9.4]. | MAS's 2024 guideline on DPT service providers sets retail-specific expectations, such as limits on lending and staking retail clients' assets [I:03d1274f44b1, para 2.2.1, p.8]. | VARA's 2025 Market Conduct Rulebook lets a firm serve only the investor types VARA permits: Retail, Qualified or Institutional [I:1730f7a4a760, Part IV.A.1-2, p.10]. |
| Trading for own account | A platform should not trade VAs for its own account. The exceptions are off-platform back-to-back deals (buying and selling at the same time to fill a client order) and cases the SFC allows [S:f1bbafccd556, para 13.2]. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. | VARA prohibits VASPs from actively investing their own or their group's portfolio; prudent treasury management is allowed [I:1730f7a4a760, Part VII.A.1-2, p.17]. |
| Settlement time | Not stated in the sources reviewed. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. | VARA's 2026 Exchange Services Rulebook requires final settlement within 24 hours of a trade, subject to factors outside the exchange's control [I:4a75e390e632, Part III.D.1, p.12]. |
| Status and as-of date | In force; SFC list as updated 29 May 2026 [S:b14be2203d39, Last update line]. | DTSP rule in force from 30 June 2025, per MAS's release of 6 June 2025 [I:ded457589f4e, para 2]. | Regulations version dated 19 May 2025 [I:51494a8caf05, cover page, p.1]. Register captured 26 Sep 2026 [I:da2385bdf350, Register results counter ('56 results found')]. |

## 2. Stablecoin regime

| Item | Hong Kong | Singapore (MAS) | UAE / Dubai (VARA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal basis and start | The Stablecoins Ordinance (Cap. 656) came into operation on 1 August 2025 [S:0df2de5234dc, para 1]. | MAS announced its finalised stablecoin framework in a media release on 15 August 2023 [I:08889860a424, para 1]. The framework is not yet in law; the law changes to bring it in went to consultation on 1 September 2026 [I:08889860a424, Related News, 1 September 2026 item]. | VARA's 2025 Virtual Asset Issuance Rulebook makes issuing an FRVA a Category 1 activity that needs a VARA licence [I:5aa70da4a239, Part I.C.1, p.8]. |
| Law change still pending | Not stated in the sources reviewed. | On 1 September 2026 MAS consulted on proposed changes to the Payment Services Act 2019 to put the framework into law [I:08889860a424, Related News, 1 September 2026 item]. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. |
| Which coins are covered | A licence is needed to issue a specified stablecoin (the kind the Ordinance covers) in Hong Kong. It is also needed to issue one anywhere that tracks the Hong Kong dollar, even partly [S:fe4baea8dd7d, para 2.3.1]. | The framework is for single-currency stablecoins issued in Singapore and tied to any G10 currency (a set of major world currencies) or the Singapore dollar [I:08889860a424, para 3]. | VARA does not approve stablecoins that track the AED; those stay solely with the CBUAE [I:5aa70da4a239, Annex 1 Part I.A.2, p.29]. |
| Reserve backing | The market value of reserves must always be at least the par value (face value) of coins in circulation [S:240be6833157, para 2.2.1]. | The framework sets requirements on the make-up, valuation, custody and audit of reserve assets [I:08889860a424, para 3]. | An FRVA issuer must keep reserves that back its coins at least 100% at all times [I:5aa70da4a239, Annex 1 Part III.B.1, p.34]. |
| Redemption | Issuers must honour valid requests as soon as practicable, without an unreasonable fee or unduly burdensome condition. The guideline adds that valid requests should be processed within one business day, unless otherwise approved [S:240be6833157, para 3.3.3]. | Under the framework (not yet in law), issuers must pay holders the par value within five business days of a request [I:08889860a424, para 3]. | Issuers must complete valid par redemptions within one working day and without fees. If a major disruption affects the reserve assets, the one day counts from when the disruption ends [I:5aa70da4a239, Annex 1 Part III.C.2-3, p.36]. |
| Issuer capital | A licensee must hold at least HK$25 million of paid-up capital [S:240be6833157, para 5.1.1]. A bank licensee follows the Banking Ordinance instead [S:240be6833157, para 5.1.3]. | Under the framework, issuers must hold minimum base capital and liquid assets; the release gives no amount [I:08889860a424, para 3]. | An FRVA issuer must keep paid-up capital of AED 1.5 million plus 2% of the value of its available coin supply [I:5aa70da4a239, Annex 1 Part III.F.1, p.37]. |
| Interest to holders | Licensees must not pay interest or any yield linked to holding a coin [S:fe4baea8dd7d, para 3.13.1]. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. |
| Audit and disclosure | The HKMA expects an independent auditor to attest regularly that reserves fully back the coins [S:240be6833157, para 2.7.3]. | Under the framework, issuers must disclose how value is kept stable, what rights holders have, and reserve audit results [I:08889860a424, para 3]. | Issuers must commission an independent audit of coins and reserves every month [I:5aa70da4a239, Annex 1 Part III.D.1, p.37]. |
| Marketing and labels | A licensee may engage only listed *permitted offerors* to offer its coins, and banks are on that list [S:fe4baea8dd7d, para 2.10.1]. | Under the framework, only issuers meeting every requirement may apply for the *MAS-regulated stablecoin* label; misusing it may bring penalties [I:08889860a424, para 4]. | Marketing must make clear that no deposit guarantee or investor protection scheme covers FRVAs [I:5aa70da4a239, Annex 1 Part III.E.2, p.37]. |
| Licensed issuers | The HKMA register lists two licensed issuers, both from 10 April 2026 [S:270f1f7324ab, Register table]. On 28 April 2026 both had confirmed they had not issued any regulated stablecoin [S:275e7319a841, para 3]. On 16 September 2026 a Government spokesman said the first regulated stablecoin in Hong Kong had launched in August 2026 [S:8be6d15c6641, para 10]. The official sources reviewed do not name the issuer. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. |
| Status and as-of date | In force since 1 August 2025 [S:0df2de5234dc, para 1]. | Framework finalised on 15 August 2023 [I:08889860a424, para 1]. Not yet in law; changes to the Payment Services Act 2019 under consultation from 1 September 2026 [I:08889860a424, Related News, 1 September 2026 item]. | Rulebook version dated 19 May 2025; captured 26 Sep 2026 [I:5aa70da4a239, cover page, p.1]. |

## 3. Custody rules

| Item | Hong Kong | Singapore (MAS) | UAE / Dubai (VARA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who the rules cover | The HKMA's May 2026 custody standards apply to banks, and to subsidiaries of local banks, that hold digital assets for clients [S:ed61d7c335e7, scope paragraph, p.2]. The SFC's platform guidelines cover licensed platforms [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.1]. | MAS's 2024 guideline covers DPT service providers, including some exempt payment service providers named in the Payment Services Act [I:03d1274f44b1, para 2.1.1, pp.5-6]. | VARA's 2025 Custody Services Rulebook covers VASPs licensed for custody [I:dd24ab7cc375, Introduction, p.4]. |
| Cold storage share | The HKMA expects a bank to keep 98% of client VAs in cold storage [S:ce05e67bcdce, fn 8]. A platform should do the same [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.6(c), p.63]. | MAS expects at least 90% of customer assets held in trust to sit in cold wallets at all times [I:03d1274f44b1, para 3.4.4, p.13]. | VARA says a custodian should choose its mix of hot and cold wallets through a risk-based analysis [I:dd24ab7cc375, Part III.C.1, p.11]. |
| Keeping client assets apart | The HKMA expects a bank to keep clients' digital assets in their own client accounts, apart from the bank's assets [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 6]. | Where a provider runs the customer trust account itself, MAS expects it to keep customers' assets at different blockchain addresses from its own [I:03d1274f44b1, para 3.2.3, p.11]. | A custodian must keep each client's VAs in separate wallets that hold only that client's assets [I:dd24ab7cc375, Part III.B.3, p.10]. |
| Lending or pledging client assets | The HKMA expects a bank not to lend or pledge client digital assets, except for settlement and fees, with prior explicit written consent, or where law requires [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 7]. | MAS expects providers not to let retail customers pledge, lend or stake their assets [I:03d1274f44b1, para 3.8.1, p.18]. | A custodian must not allow reuse of custody assets as collateral, even with client consent [I:dd24ab7cc375, Part III.B.2, p.10]. |
| Where keys or staff sit | The HKMA expects seeds and private keys to be created, stored and backed up in Hong Kong [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b)]. | MAS expects the staff and senior managers who control movements of customer assets to live in Singapore [I:03d1274f44b1, para 3.4.8, p.14]. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. |
| Legal structure | A platform should hold client assets on trust through its associated entity (a group company set up to hold client assets) [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.1]. | Customers' assets sit in a trust account. A provider may run it itself or use an outside safeguarding person; MAS does not require the outside option [I:03d1274f44b1, para 3.2.1, p.10]. | A custodian must be a legal entity separate from group companies that run other VA activities [I:dd24ab7cc375, Part III.B.5, p.10]. |
| Planned changes | The Government and SFC aim to put a VA custodian licensing bill to the Legislative Council in 2026 (stated target) [S:f41a543388bc, para 65]. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. |
| Status and as-of date | HKMA standards issued 27 May 2026 and in force [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2]. | Guideline in effect from 4 October 2024 [I:03d1274f44b1, para 1.1.6, p.3]. | Rulebook version dated 19 May 2025; captured 26 Sep 2026 [I:dd24ab7cc375, cover page, p.1]. |

## 5. Tokenisation initiatives

|---|---|---|---|

| Government bonds | In November 2025 the Government priced about HK$10 billion of digital green bonds, with an option to settle the HK dollar and renminbi parts of the issue in tokenised central bank money [S:ac6cd085a325, para 1; 'Integration of digital money' paragraph]. The bonds cleared and settled through the Central Moneymarkets Unit (CMU) [S:ac6cd085a325, key features]. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. |

| Tokenised deposits in stablecoin rules | Not stated in the sources reviewed. | Not stated in the sources reviewed. | VARA's FRVA rules exclude tokenised bank deposits used only for settlement between banks [I:5aa70da4a239, Annex 1 Part I.A.3(c), p.29]. |

## Common mix-ups

- **Myth:** Dubai means the whole UAE. **Fact:** VARA's rules cover Dubai but exclude the Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC) [I:51494a8caf05, Schedule 4 'Emirate', p.46].
- **Myth:** MAS's 38 DPT firms are all exchanges. **Fact:** The MAS directory count covers every firm licensed for DPT services, not only trading venues [I:cb4e769d4c67, Directory results counter ('38 result(s)') and Licence Type filter ('Major Payment Institution 38')].
- **Myth:** The three redemption deadlines are the same. **Fact:** Hong Kong expects one business day [S:240be6833157, para 3.3.3]. Singapore's framework, not yet in law, sets five business days [I:08889860a424, para 3]. Dubai requires one working day [I:5aa70da4a239, Annex 1 Part III.C.2, p.36].
- **Myth:** *Not stated* means there is no rule. **Fact:** It means only that the sources on this page do not say. The regulator may have other documents on the point.

## Open items

- Singapore's detailed rules for platforms serving Singapore customers were not captured. For the DTSP regime, only the June 2025 media release was reviewed.
- The CBUAE's rules for AED-linked stablecoins were not captured, so the UAE stablecoin row covers Dubai's non-AED coins only.
- No source reviewed gives the number of licensed stablecoin issuers in Singapore or Dubai.
- No source reviewed says whether Singapore or Dubai banks need a separate licence to offer VA dealing or custody.
- The Hong Kong custodian and dealing bills were not yet law on 25 Sep 2026.
