# CMU OmniClear digital asset platform and tokenised Exchange Fund Bills

Plans for a digital asset platform run by the operator of the Central Moneymarkets Unit (CMU), Hong Kong's central securities depository, and for HKMA tests of tokenised Exchange Fund Bills. [S:9c8855ec55a1, para 2]; [S:ef3648ff1610, para 35(ii), (iv)]

## What it is

CMU OmniClear was set up, wholly owned by the Exchange Fund, to run the CMU under a contract with the HKMA. [S:d43a14fd959f, paras 1-2] The HKMA stays the owner and system operator of the CMU, holds the legal relationships with CMU participants and makes CMU policy decisions. [S:d43a14fd959f, para 3]; [S:9c8855ec55a1, para 6] Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing (HKEX) is taking a 20% stake in the holding company, CMU OmniClear Holdings; the Exchange Fund keeps 80%. [S:9c8855ec55a1, para 2] The 2026-27 Budget said CMU OmniClear would set up a digital asset platform in 2026 for digital bond issuance and settlement. It would later be extended to other digital assets and linked to other tokenisation platforms in the region. [S:654fa5eba8cf, para 114] The 2026 Policy Address repeats the platform target. It also says the HKMA will test tokenising Exchange Fund Bills by year-end, so that banks can use the more than $1.3 trillion of these bills at any hour. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 35(ii), (iv)] The Hong Kong Mortgage Corporation's (HKMC's) June 2026 digital bonds were created on a CMU-operated distributed ledger technology (DLT) platform, with the CMU as clearing and settlement system. [S:6aa27ee8b400, para 4]

## What it means for your bank

- Nothing binding yet. Both items are stated targets, and no platform rulebook or test design appears in the sources reviewed. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 35(ii), (iv)]
- The Policy Address links the Exchange Fund Bills tests to banks' asset and liability management. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 35(ii)]
- Issuing on a CMU-operated DLT platform satisfies one of the two alternative Basic Requirements of the Digital Bond Grant Scheme; the HKMA decides case by case whether a platform counts. [S:5a0563de8142, s.2 and fn 6, p.3]
- The CMU is deemed a designated clearing and settlement system, so its settlements have statutory finality. Another DLT platform gets that only if the HKMA designates it; otherwise parties can agree finality by contract. [S:a0b44be27c40, paras 36-38, pp.14-15]
- The HKMA's February 2024 tokenised-products circular does not cover products regulated under the Securities and Futures Ordinance; those follow the relevant Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) and HKMA requirements. [S:b2719f3dcb37, 'Coverage' and fn 2, p.1]

## Talking points

- **To the CCO:** No rules have been published yet for the platform or the Exchange Fund Bills tests; both are stated targets. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 35(ii), (iv)]
- **To business heads:** The platform is meant as a one-stop service for digital bond issuance and settlement. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 35(iv)]
- **To the CEO:** The HKMA plans tests by end-2026 on tokenising Exchange Fund Bills, aimed at helping banks use more than $1.3 trillion of these bills round the clock. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 35(ii)]
- **To Risk:** A DLT platform has statutory finality only if the HKMA designates it; otherwise finality rests on contract. [S:a0b44be27c40, paras 37-38, p.15]
