# Project mBridge

Project mBridge is a shared platform where several central banks each issue their own central bank digital currency (CBDC). [S:947fa49606ab, s.1 Executive summary, p.4] A CBDC is digital money issued by a central bank. It matters to your bank because banks can use the platform to settle cross-border payments and foreign exchange (FX) trades directly. [S:947fa49606ab, s.1 Executive summary, p.4] The Government calls it part of Hong Kong's financial market infrastructure. [S:2ffb8727bb2f, Reply (4)]

## What it is

The project began as Inthanon-LionRock, a study by the HKMA and the BoT on using CBDC for cross-border payments. [S:6b0fae230518, para 1 and footnote 1] In February 2021 the PBC's Digital Currency Institute and the CBUAE joined. [S:6b0fae230518, para 1] The project was then renamed the m-CBDC Bridge. [S:6b0fae230518, para 1]

The platform tests FX deals in which both payments settle at the same moment (payment versus payment). [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Project mBridge' section] It tests these deals in real time, around the clock, across several jurisdictions. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Project mBridge' section]

In the 2022 pilot, 20 banks in four jurisdictions made more than 160 payment and FX trades. [S:947fa49606ab, s.1 Executive summary, p.4] The trades were worth over US$22 million in total. [S:947fa49606ab, s.1 Executive summary, p.4]

The platform reached minimum viable product (MVP) stage in June 2024. [S:db56d97e2206, paras 1-2] Each founding central bank's jurisdiction hosts a validating node, meaning a computer that checks and records platform transactions. [S:db56d97e2206, paras 1-2] In October 2025 the Government told the Legislative Council that participating Hong Kong banks set their own fees for mBridge payments. [S:2ffb8727bb2f, Reply (4)]

## What it means for your bank

- Each bank runs its own compliance checks off the platform before it sends or confirms a payment. [S:947fa49606ab, s.6.5.2, p.30] These include anti-money laundering (AML), counter-terrorist financing (CTF) and sanctions checks. [S:947fa49606ab, s.6.5.2, p.30]
- Each participating bank must follow the AML, CTF and sanctions laws that apply to it. [S:947fa49606ab, s.7.2(d), p.35] The platform only attaches a pass or fail certificate from the bank's own off-platform check. [S:947fa49606ab, s.7.2(d), p.35]
- Banks keep know-your-customer checks (confirming who customers are), AML and counter-terrorist financing compliance off the platform. [S:89b8fcadfaa8, p.3] Each jurisdiction's own rules apply. [S:89b8fcadfaa8, p.3]
- Foreign banks can hold and use another jurisdiction's CBDC. [S:947fa49606ab, s.7.1.1, p.32; S:89b8fcadfaa8, p.2] Only that jurisdiction's domestic banks can ask its central bank to issue or redeem it. [S:947fa49606ab, s.7.1.1, p.32; S:89b8fcadfaa8, p.2]
- Private firms can propose add-on technology or new uses through a BISIH participation form. [S:db56d97e2206, para 4]

## Talking points

- **To the CCO:** On mBridge, AML and sanctions checks stay with each bank, off the platform. [S:947fa49606ab, s.6.5.2, p.30; s.7.2(d), p.35]
- **To business heads:** Participating Hong Kong banks set their own fees for mBridge payments. [S:2ffb8727bb2f, Reply (4)]
- **To the CEO:** Project mBridge reached MVP in June 2024. [S:db56d97e2206, para 1] The Government calls it part of Hong Kong's financial market infrastructure. [S:2ffb8727bb2f, Reply (4)]
- **To Risk:** The 2022 pilot ran on a single central cloud in Hong Kong. The report says keeping data in one cloud raised privacy concerns. [S:947fa49606ab, s.5.1, p.16; s.7.1.3, p.33]
