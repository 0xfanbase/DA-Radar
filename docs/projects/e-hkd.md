# e-HKD (digital Hong Kong dollar)

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority's (HKMA's) long-running work on a possible Hong Kong central bank digital currency (CBDC), now focused on wholesale use by financial institutions. [S:d51dc8884c8a, opening paragraph]

## What it is

The HKMA began in 2017 with Project LionRock, a proof of concept on large-value payments and delivery-versus-payment settlement. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'e-HKD for financial institutions' section] It then consulted on a retail e-HKD through a 2021 technical paper and a 2022 policy paper. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'HKMA's Policy Stance on e-HKD used in retail applications'] The technical paper proposed a two-tier model in which commercial banks distribute e-HKD, and said it was not a blueprint. [S:59a20457a2fc, Executive Summary points 3-4] In September 2022 the HKMA set out a three-rail approach. Its initial legal research suggested existing law may not give enough certainty to issue a digital form of legal tender. [S:35cb94c89a11, Rail 1 section, p.10] Industry pilots ran in two phases, and the project was renamed e-HKD+ in September 2024 to cover tokenised deposits too. [S:2f93aa2b9560, paras 1-2] In October 2025 the HKMA said the near-term priority lies beyond retail, in wholesale payments. [S:6bd9e65817c4, para 4] It has not decided if, or when, it will introduce an e-HKD. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Policy Stance and Three-Rail Approach']

## What it means for your bank

- The HKMA has made no policy decision on if, or when, an e-HKD would be introduced. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Policy Stance and Three-Rail Approach'] The 2021 technical paper says its design is not a blueprint for e-HKD. [S:59a20457a2fc, Executive Summary point 4]
- The April 2022 paper said full anonymity is not a plausible option because of anti-money laundering and counter-financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) requirements. It noted that tiered wallets are a common answer. [S:3b42966a786e, Section 1, p.4; Section 5.3, p.34]
- The same paper said that in a two-tier model, customer-facing institutions could be tasked with customer due diligence and record-keeping. [S:3b42966a786e, Section 5.4, p.35]
- Phase 2 found the public sees e-HKD and tokenised deposits in a similar light, because of high trust in banks. [S:6bd9e65817c4, para 3]
- The HKMA says it will publish common token standards from the e-HKD Industry Forum. [S:6bd9e65817c4, para 6]

## Talking points

- **To the CCO:** No decision exists to introduce an e-HKD. The 2022 paper saw distributing institutions as the ones that could handle customer due diligence. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Policy Stance and Three-Rail Approach']; [S:3b42966a786e, Section 5.4, p.35]
- **To business heads:** The HKMA's e-HKD focus is now wholesale payments, supporting tokenisation and cross-border settlement. [S:6bd9e65817c4, para 4]
- **To the CEO:** The Government's November 2025 digital green bonds were the first digital bond offering worldwide to use e-HKD and e-CNY (the Mainland's digital renminbi) in settlement. [S:ac6cd085a325, 'Integration of digital money' paragraph]
- **To Risk:** In Phase 2, zero-knowledge proof privacy tools made transactions up to 320 times more expensive in gas fees (network transaction fees). [S:bdefe7fc69d4, Section 4 'Privacy', PDF p.34]
