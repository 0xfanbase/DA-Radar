# e-HKD (digital Hong Kong dollar)

e-HKD is the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) study of a possible Hong Kong central bank digital currency (CBDC). [S:d51dc8884c8a, opening paragraph] A CBDC is digital money issued by a central bank. It matters to your bank because the work now focuses on wholesale use by financial institutions such as banks. [S:d51dc8884c8a, opening paragraph] The HKMA has not decided whether to launch an e-HKD. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Policy Stance and Three-Rail Approach']

## What it is

The HKMA began in 2017 with Project LionRock. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'e-HKD for financial institutions' section] It was a proof of concept (an early test that an idea works) on large-value payments and delivery-versus-payment settlement. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'e-HKD for financial institutions' section] Delivery versus payment means an asset and its payment change hands at the same moment.

The HKMA then consulted on a retail e-HKD, meaning one for the public. It did so through a 2021 technical paper and a 2022 policy paper. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'HKMA's Policy Stance on e-HKD used in retail applications'] The technical paper proposed a two-tier model, in which commercial banks distribute e-HKD. [S:59a20457a2fc, Executive Summary points 3-4] The paper said its design was not a blueprint. [S:59a20457a2fc, Executive Summary points 3-4]

In September 2022 the HKMA set out a three-rail approach (three parallel tracks of work). [S:35cb94c89a11, Rail 1 section, p.10] Its early legal research suggested existing law may not give enough certainty to issue a digital form of legal tender. [S:35cb94c89a11, Rail 1 section, p.10] Legal tender is money that the law recognises for paying debts.

Industry pilots ran in two phases. [S:2f93aa2b9560, paras 1-2] In September 2024 the HKMA renamed the project e-HKD+ so that it also covers tokenised deposits (digital versions of bank deposits). [S:2f93aa2b9560, paras 1-2] In October 2025 the HKMA said the near-term priority is wholesale payments rather than retail use. [S:6bd9e65817c4, para 4] The HKMA has not decided whether, or when, it will introduce an e-HKD. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Policy Stance and Three-Rail Approach']

## What it means for your bank

- The HKMA has made no policy decision on whether, or when, it would introduce an e-HKD. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Policy Stance and Three-Rail Approach'] The 2021 technical paper says its design is not a blueprint for e-HKD. [S:59a20457a2fc, Executive Summary point 4]
- The April 2022 paper said full anonymity is not a realistic option. [S:3b42966a786e, Section 1, p.4; Section 5.3, p.34] The reason is the requirements against money laundering and terrorist financing. [S:3b42966a786e, Section 1, p.4; Section 5.3, p.34] It noted that tiered wallets are a common answer. [S:3b42966a786e, Section 1, p.4; Section 5.3, p.34] Tiered wallets give users higher limits when they share more identity details.
- The same paper looked at a two-tier model. [S:3b42966a786e, Section 5.4, p.35] It said customer-facing institutions could be given the job of customer due diligence (checking who customers are) and record-keeping. [S:3b42966a786e, Section 5.4, p.35]
- Phase 2 found that the public sees e-HKD and tokenised deposits in a similar light, because people trust banks highly. [S:6bd9e65817c4, para 3]
- The HKMA says it will publish common token standards (shared technical rules for digital tokens) from the e-HKD Industry Forum. [S:6bd9e65817c4, para 6]

## Talking points

- **To the CCO:** No decision exists to introduce an e-HKD. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Policy Stance and Three-Rail Approach'] The 2022 paper saw the firms that distribute e-HKD as the ones that could handle customer due diligence. [S:3b42966a786e, Section 5.4, p.35]
- **To business heads:** The HKMA's e-HKD focus is now wholesale payments, supporting tokenisation and cross-border settlement. [S:6bd9e65817c4, para 4]
- **To the CEO:** The Government's November 2025 digital green bonds offered settlement in e-HKD and e-CNY (the Mainland's digital renminbi). No digital bond offering anywhere had offered this before. [S:ac6cd085a325, 'Integration of digital money' paragraph]
- **To Risk:** In Phase 2, privacy tools called zero-knowledge proofs made transactions up to 320 times costlier in gas fees (network transaction fees). [S:bdefe7fc69d4, Section 4 'Privacy', PDF p.34]
