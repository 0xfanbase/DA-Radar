# Tokenised Bond Expert Group and the DLT fixed-income law review

Distributed ledger technology (DLT) is a shared digital record kept in step across many computers. The Tokenised Bond Expert Group (TBEG) is a Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) panel of industry experts on tokenised bonds. [S:fc3eb72031c6, paras 1, 3] Tokenised bonds are bonds whose ownership is recorded as digital tokens on DLT. The group works alongside a joint review by the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau (FSTB) and the HKMA. [S:fc3eb72031c6, paras 1, 3] The review asks whether Hong Kong's laws and rules support using DLT in the bond market. [S:279f10054c6e, para 1] For a bank that issues, arranges or holds tokenised bonds, the review shows where current law already fits. It also shows which legal questions are still open. Fixed income, in the title, means bonds and similar debt.

## What it is

Policy Statement 2.0 said the FSTB and HKMA would lead a review of law and regulation on tokenisation, with bonds as the first focus. [S:a29b51bffc6b, para 9] Tokenisation means representing an asset, such as a bond, as a digital token. The 2026-27 Budget said the Government would issue guidance on registers of debenture holders (official lists of who holds a company's bonds). [S:654fa5eba8cf, para 107] The guidance is to confirm that these registers may be kept on a distributed ledger. [S:654fa5eba8cf, para 107] The Budget further said the Government would explore e-signatures for bond documents. [S:654fa5eba8cf, para 107] It also named digitised bearer bonds (bonds owned by whoever holds the certificate) as a topic to explore. [S:654fa5eba8cf, para 107] The HKMA announced the TBEG on 5 June 2026. [S:fc3eb72031c6, paras 1-2] Its members come from: [S:fc3eb72031c6, paras 1-2]

- industry bodies;
- financial institutions;
- law firms; and
- infrastructure and technology providers.

The group's discussions in May 2026 gave input to the joint review. [S:fc3eb72031c6, para 3] Phase 1 of the review found that current law and regulation are already flexible enough for tokenised bond issues. [S:279f10054c6e, para 1] On 29 June 2026, when the phase 1 findings came out, the Companies Registry published answers to frequently asked questions (FAQs). [S:279f10054c6e, para 6] The FAQs confirm that a register of debenture holders kept on DLT can meet the record-keeping rules in the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). [S:279f10054c6e, para 6]

## What it means for your bank

- Phase 1 created no new obligation. It found the current legal and regulatory setting already flexible enough for tokenised bond issues. [S:279f10054c6e, paras 1, 3]
- The Companies Registry FAQs are the reference point for DLT registers of debenture holders. [S:279f10054c6e, para 6] They matter if the bank keeps or relies on such a register for a Hong Kong company. [S:279f10054c6e, para 6]
- Phase 2 will look at letting issuers sign tokenised bond issue documents electronically. One example is using electronic signatures to create trusts for tokenised bond and fund issues. [S:279f10054c6e, paras 9-11] It will also look at how possession and transfer work for tokenised fixed-income instruments. [S:279f10054c6e, paras 9-11]
- The HKMA says it will review and update the group's membership when needed. [S:fc3eb72031c6, para 4]

## Talking points

- **To the CCO:** Phase 1 changed no law. It confirmed the current rules are flexible enough and added Companies Registry FAQs on DLT registers. [S:279f10054c6e, paras 1, 6]
- **To business heads:** Phase 2 will explore signing tokenised bond issue documents electronically. The goal is a more efficient and automated issuance process. [S:279f10054c6e, para 10]
- **To the CEO:** The HKMA set up the TBEG to explore policy measures, market practices and innovations with industry. [S:fc3eb72031c6, paras 1-2]
- **To Risk:** Possession and transfer of tokenised instruments are topics for the phase 2 review. That review is set for H2 2026. [S:279f10054c6e, paras 8, 11]
