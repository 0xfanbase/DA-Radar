# Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) and amended CRS

A Hong Kong bill would update the Common Reporting Standard (CRS) and bring in the Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF). [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview] Since 2018, Hong Kong has used the CRS to exchange financial account information with other tax authorities each year. [S:252a7f86684e, paras 2-4] CARF is a yearly crypto-asset tax reporting standard from the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD). [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview] Why banks care: a group entity offering crypto exchange services to customers as a business can become a reporting crypto-asset service provider (RCASP). [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50M] If the bill passes, CARF duties start on 1 January 2027. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(3)]

## What it is

CARF is an OECD standard. Under it, jurisdictions automatically exchange crypto-asset tax information each year. [S:ea81f92ea327, para 2] Hong Kong aimed to have its law in place by 2026 and to start exchanges from 2028. [S:ea81f92ea327, para 4]

Under the bill, an RCASP is a person that, as a business, carries out crypto exchange transactions for customers. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50M "reporting crypto-asset service provider"] This includes acting as a counterparty (the other side of a trade) or an intermediary, or running a trading platform. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50M "reporting crypto-asset service provider"]

An RCASP with a Hong Kong nexus (a close link to Hong Kong) would have to: [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, ss.50O-50Q, 50S]

- register;
- carry out due diligence (checks on its customers);
- file returns (reports to the tax authority);
- keep records.

Central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) and specified e-money products are outside CARF. [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 1.5, 4.3] The amended CRS brings them in instead. [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 1.5, 4.3] If the bill passes, CARF starts on 1 January 2027. The amended CRS starts on 1 January 2028. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(3)-(4)]

## What it means for your bank

- Under the bill, any group entity would be an RCASP if, as a business, it carries out crypto exchange transactions for customers. Acting as an intermediary counts. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50M]
- The Hong Kong nexus tests look at: [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50N(1)-(2)]
  - tax residence;
  - incorporation;
  - management;
  - a regular place of business;
  - Hong Kong branches.
- A Hong Kong RCASP would have to register by 31 January of the year after it first meets a nexus test. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50O(1)-(2)]
- An RCASP would need self-certifications of tax residence (customers' own statements of where they are tax resident) from new customers. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Due diligence requirements'] For existing users, the same applies within 12 months of the rules taking effect. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Due diligence requirements']
- Under the amended CRS, crypto-linked derivatives count as financial assets. [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 4.3-4.4] CBDCs and specified e-money products are added to the definition of a depository account (a deposit-type account). [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 4.3-4.4]
- Registration and returns would go to the IRD through its planned CARF Portal. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Reporting portal and mandatory registration requirement']
- Hiring a service provider would not, on its own, be a reasonable excuse (a legal defence). [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(2)] This covers failing to register, do due diligence or file returns. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(2)]

## Talking points

- **To the CCO:** The bill is not yet law. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview] If passed, CARF duties start on 1 January 2027 and amended CRS on 1 January 2028. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(3)-(4)]
- **To business heads:** A customer crypto exchange service run as a business can make the entity an RCASP. This applies even when the entity acts as an intermediary. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50M]
- **To the CEO:** Hong Kong committed to CARF in December 2024, with first data exchanges targeted from 2028. [S:ea81f92ea327, para 4]
- **To Risk:** Proposed fines can scale. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(4)-(5)] Some continuing offences carry a further HK$500 a day after conviction. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(4)-(5)] For due-diligence offences, the fine is the higher of two amounts: a level 3 fine, or HK$1,000 per affected user or controlling person. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(4)-(5)] A level 3 fine is a set point on Hong Kong's scale of fines.
