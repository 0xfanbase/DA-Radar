# E3 Glossary and timeline

This page is a quick reference for the bank's Head of Compliance (digital assets). Look up a term in plain English. Or see where any document sits in the 2017–2026 sequence. Every line gives its official source. As of 25 Sep 2026.

## How to use this page

Abbreviations used on this page:

- AI (AIs) = authorized institution(s)
- AML = anti-money laundering
- AMLO = Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance
- BIS = Bank for International Settlements
- CBDC = central bank digital currency
- CFT = counter-financing of terrorism
- CMU = Central Moneymarkets Unit
- CP = consultation paper
- DLT = distributed ledger technology (shared databases, such as blockchains)
- e-CNY = the digital renminbi
- e-HKD = a possible digital Hong Kong dollar
- FATF = Financial Action Task Force
- FSTB = Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau
- HKMA = Hong Kong Monetary Authority
- IRD = Inland Revenue Department
- KYC = know your customer (identity checks)
- LC (LCs) = licensed corporation(s)
- LegCo = Legislative Council
- ML/TF: ML = money laundering; TF = terrorist financing
- MoU = memorandum of understanding (a formal agreement to work together)
- OECD = Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development
- OTC = over the counter (trading directly with a dealer, not on an exchange)
- RI (RIs) = registered institution(s)
- SFC = Securities and Futures Commission
- SFO = Securities and Futures Ordinance
- VA (VAs) = virtual asset(s)
- VASP (VASPs) = virtual asset service provider(s)
- VATP (VATPs) = virtual asset trading platform(s)

How the page works:

- Part A defines about 80 terms. Each one points to the official document and paragraph that sets out the idea.
- Part B lists about 100 dated events from 2017 to September 2026. Each event is a fact about what was published or done. Targets appear only as the source states them.
- Dates are as the source gives them. If a source gives only a month or a year, so does this page.

## Part A: Glossary

**Institutions and legal status**

| Term | Plain definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Authorized Institution (AI) | A bank or other institution that the HKMA has authorised. The HKMA's anti-money laundering guideline (its AML/CFT Guideline) sets the standards AIs should meet, including AIs that are also RIs. The HKMA issues that guideline under s.7 of the AMLO and s.7(3) of the Banking Ordinance. | [S:7bfdad704174, paras 1.1, 1.3] |
| Registered Institution (RI) | An AI that the SFC has registered to carry on one or more regulated activities (types of securities business, such as dealing or advising). The HKMA supervises RIs day to day, as frontline supervisor. Ultimate responsibility stays with the SFC. | [S:8934af8cc884, paras 2.1.2, 2.1.3, 2.2.1] |
| Licensed corporation (LC) | A firm that the SFC has licensed to carry on regulated activities. The joint circulars cover LCs and RIs together, calling both 'intermediaries'. | [S:b79266f3da51, paras 4-6] |
| HKMA | The Hong Kong Monetary Authority, Hong Kong's banking regulator. Among its other roles, it grants licences to stablecoin issuers under the Stablecoins Ordinance. | [S:35e1ac2b815a, para 2]; [S:afb6cd0da6ff, para 1] |
| SFC | The Securities and Futures Commission, Hong Kong's securities regulator. It licenses VA trading platforms. The Government proposes that the SFC lead licensing and standard-setting for VA dealing and VA custody (safekeeping). | [S:2e5c35b38cc3, para 1]; [S:a29b51bffc6b, paras 5-6] |
| FSTB | The Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau, the government bureau for financial policy. It issued Policy Statement 2.0. It runs the VA licensing consultations jointly with the SFC. | [S:a29b51bffc6b, para 2]; [S:7395a53cc85a, para 1] |
| Frontline regulator | The body that supervises a firm day to day. This is the HKMA's proposed role for banks under the coming VA dealing and custody regimes. Under these regimes, the HKMA would supervise banks, while the SFC sets the standards. | [S:7395a53cc85a, para 4] |
| SB-1 | A chapter of the HKMA's Supervisory Policy Manual (its supervision handbook). It covers how the HKMA supervises RIs' regulated activities. It is a statutory guideline, issued under s.7(3) of the Banking Ordinance. | [S:8934af8cc884, p.1 'Purpose', 'Classification'] |
| Executive officer | A person who directly supervises a regulated activity at an RI. The RI appoints this person with the HKMA's written consent. Each activity needs at least two. | [S:8934af8cc884, paras 2.2.4, 4.3.9] |

**Virtual assets and platforms**

| Term | Plain definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Virtual asset (VA) | The legal term for crypto and similar digital tokens. The AMLO defines it in s.53ZRA. Policy Statement 2.0 (June 2025) says the Government will now say 'digital assets' instead. | [S:8a016a912528, footnote 1]; [S:a29b51bffc6b, footnote 1] |
| Digital asset (HKMA custody usage) | A broad term in the HKMA's custody guidance. It covers VAs, tokenised securities and other tokenised assets (assets recorded as digital tokens). It also covers the keys, seeds and backups (the secret codes that give access) used to reach those assets. | [S:ed61d7c335e7, footnote 1, p.1] |
| VASP | A business that provides VA services, such as trading or transfers, to customers. The HKMA expects banks to assess each VASP customer's ML/TF risk on its own facts. That assessment includes extra due diligence (deeper checks) where needed. | [S:642680fa4467, p.4, item (b)] |
| VATP | An online platform where people buy and sell VAs. In SFC usage, it is a company licensed under the SFO for Types 1 and 7 (dealing in securities, and automated trading services). A company holding an AMLO VA service licence also counts. | [S:19d52ade6849, footnote 1] |
| Deemed-to-be-licensed | A platform that may keep operating while the SFC decides its licence application. It was operating before 1 June 2023 and met the conditions for this status. It is not licensed and may never be. | [S:c787f990209d, Note 1, 'Reminder for investors', Note 2] |
| Non-contravention period | A grace period of 12 months, from 1 June 2023 to 31 May 2024. VATPs already running, with a genuine Hong Kong presence, could operate during it. Doing so did not breach the AMLO licensing rule. | [S:2e5c35b38cc3, para 6] |
| Associated Entity | A company that holds a VATP's client assets on trust (for the clients, not for itself). It is a Hong Kong subsidiary wholly owned by the VATP. It does no other business. | [S:f1bbafccd556, paras 1.1, 10.1] |
| Cold storage | Keeping the keys to VAs offline, away from the internet. The SFC expects VATPs to keep 98% of client VAs in cold storage. | [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.6(c)] |
| Compensation arrangement | Cover that repays clients if their VAs are lost. A VATP should hold an SFC-approved arrangement of this kind. It should cover potential loss of 50% of client VAs in cold storage. It should also cover 100% of those in hot storage (kept online) and other storage. For bank custody, the HKMA's May 2026 guidance instead expects the bank to be liable to clients and to have adequate financial resources. | [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.22]; [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)] |
| Professional investor | An institution, or a wealthy person or firm, that meets a legal test. The SFO defines the term in Schedule 1, Part 1, s.1. Any other person is a 'retail client'. | [S:19d52ade6849, footnote 4] |
| Complex product | An investment product whose terms and risks are hard for a typical investor to understand. It triggers the SFC's complex-product rules. These include a suitability check (whether the product fits the client), even if the firm did not solicit (actively offer) the sale. The joint circular says VA-related products are very likely complex. | [S:8a016a912528, paras 5-6.1] |
| Joint circular | A notice from the SFC and HKMA together on intermediaries' VA activities. The December 2023 version replaced the January 2022 and October 2023 versions. A supplement followed in September 2025. | [S:8a016a912528, note after para 3, p.1]; [S:b79266f3da51, para 1] |
| Staking | Locking a client's VAs into a proof-of-stake network to earn rewards. In such a network, holders who lock up tokens help check and confirm transactions. | [S:b79266f3da51, para 4] |
| Shared order book | One pool of buy and sell orders shared by an SFC-licensed VATP and an overseas platform in its group. Every order is fully pre-funded (paid for up front). Trades settle delivery-versus-payment: the asset and the payment change hands together. | [S:f009f1a6d615, paras 1, 3, 10, 13] |
| Perpetual contract | A contract that tracks a VA's price, uses leverage (borrowed exposure) and has no end date. Under the SFC's February 2026 framework, licensed VATPs may propose to offer them. They should be offered to professional investors only. | [S:49f642bdee28, 'Scope of application', 'Eligible investors and reference assets'] |
| Funding rate | Regular payments between the two sides of a perpetual contract. One side gains if the price rises (long) and the other if it falls (short). The payments keep the contract's price near the spot (current market) price. The SFC expects settlement at least once every 24 hours. | [S:49f642bdee28, 'Scope of application', 'Trading and settlement'] |
| Travel Rule (VA transfers) | The rule that sender and recipient details travel with a transfer. For banks, the HKMA Guideline sets customer due diligence (checks on the customer) for one-off VA transfers of HK$8,000 or more. It points to AMLO Schedule 2 s.13A. RIs must also take into account the VA parts of Chapter 12 of the SFC AML Guideline. | [S:7bfdad704174, para 4.2.1(b)(iii), fn 15; para 1.3, fn 1] |
| Unhosted wallet | A VA wallet (software or hardware) through which the owner holds and moves VAs, keeping the private key (the secret code that moves the VAs). The HKMA's May 2025 consultation proposed a check by licensed stablecoin issuers. They would check whether each customer's wallet is custodial (run by a provider) or unhosted. | [S:0d6e6d0253ff, footnote 4, paras 3.2.2-3.2.4] |
| Suspicious VATPs list | A public SFC list of suspicious trading platforms. It helps the public spot suspicious platforms doing business in Hong Kong. | [S:ea8889eb1fc4, item 2]; [S:dddd919625e9, para 3] |

**Coming VA regimes**

| Term | Plain definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| VA dealing service | Dealing in VAs (buying and selling them) as a business. The June 2025 proposal covers this business in Hong Kong. It runs from simple conversion (swapping one asset for another) to brokerage and block trades (very large deals). Banks would register with the SFC, with the HKMA as frontline regulator. | [S:27d27650e820, para 1.10] |
| VA custodian service | Keeping VAs safe for others as a business. Under the December 2025 conclusions, it means holding, for others, the private keys or similar tools that can move VAs. Trustees or managers that only hand custody to someone else are out of scope. | [S:f41a543388bc, paras 6-8] |
| VA advisory / VA management | Advising clients on VAs, and managing VA portfolios for them. Both are proposed licensing regimes. Their scope will match the SFO regimes for advising on securities (Type 4) and asset management (Type 9). | [S:4a0342268bf2, para 2] |
| Deeming / grandfathering | Letting firms already in business carry on under a new regime, as if licensed, while they apply. None is planned for the VA dealing or VA custodian regimes. | [S:6186c01c6649, para 31]; [S:f41a543388bc, para 48] |
| Expedited approval | A faster route to approval. It is promised for SFC-licensed VATPs. It is also promised for LCs and RIs that already provide VA dealing services. | [S:6186c01c6649, paras 35-36] |
| MPC (multi-party computation) provider | A technology firm that splits control of a private key among several parties. Whether it needs a custodian licence depends on the facts of each case. The main tests are whether it can move client VAs on its own, and whether clients can always reach their assets independently. | [S:f41a543388bc, para 11] |
| VA OTC regime | A scheme to license OTC VA trading businesses. The February 2024 proposal was for the Commissioner of Customs and Excise to license spot VA trading (buying and selling for immediate delivery). The proposal also covered shops and crypto cash machines. The June 2025 dealing proposal revised the scheme. | [S:efcdb0ae3849, para 2.6]; [S:27d27650e820, para 1.1] |

**Stablecoins and bank capital**

| Term | Plain definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Stablecoins Ordinance (Cap. 656) | Hong Kong's stablecoin law. Stablecoins are tokens designed to hold a steady value. The law set up licensing for regulated stablecoin activities and came into force on 1 August 2025. | [S:0df2de5234dc, paras 1-2] |
| Fiat-referenced stablecoin (FRS) | A stablecoin whose value is tied to one or more fiat currencies (ordinary government-issued money). An HKMA licence is needed to issue one in Hong Kong as a business. A licence is also needed to issue one tied to the Hong Kong dollar as a business, in or outside Hong Kong. | [S:79b5b51a1a1b, p.4 'What to regulate']; [S:35e1ac2b815a, para 2] |
| Relevant Stablecoin | A stablecoin issued by an HKMA-licensed issuer under its licence. It is a 'specified stablecoin', a category set out in law. | [S:19d52ade6849, para 2] |
| Register of licensed stablecoin issuers | The HKMA's public list of licensed stablecoin issuers. The HKMA keeps it under s.21 of Cap. 656. | [S:270f1f7324ab, intro paragraph] |
| Stablecoin issuer sandbox | A controlled space where would-be stablecoin issuers could test their plans with the HKMA. The HKMA launched it in March 2024 to share its supervisory expectations. Admission was not an HKMA endorsement. Participants had to apply separately for a licence. | [S:f2a668eb3b93, Annex, Objectives] |
| Cryptoasset (capital rules) | In the bank capital rules, a digital form of value. It relies mainly on cryptography (code-based security) and DLT or similar technology. It can be used to pay, to invest, or to access goods or services. | [S:b7d630146e6c, para 1.2.1] |
| Group 1a / 1b | Classes of cryptoasset treated like the traditional asset behind them. Group 1a is tokenised traditional assets. Group 1b is stablecoins with effective stabilisation mechanisms (ways to keep the price steady). Both carry the same capital requirements as the underlying traditional asset. An infrastructure add-on (an extra charge for technology risk) may apply on top. | [S:b7d630146e6c, para 1.3.2] |
| Group 2a / 2b | Cryptoassets with the toughest capital treatment. They are unbacked cryptoassets, and tokenised assets or stablecoins that fail the classification conditions. They are fully capitalised: a 1,250% risk weight times the 8% minimum total capital ratio means the bank holds capital equal to the whole exposure [S:5d0c13e09312, para 2.1.2]. Limited hedging (offsetting positions) is recognised for 2a, and none for 2b. | [S:b7d630146e6c, para 1.3.3] |
| CRP-1 | The HKMA's guidance on sorting cryptoassets into these groups. It is non-statutory (not issued under a law). If it differs from the Banking (Capital) Rules, the Rules prevail. | [S:b7d630146e6c, p.1 'Classification', para 1.1.4] |
| Negative vetting | LegCo's 28-day review of subsidiary legislation (detailed rules made under an existing law). The amendment rules on cryptoasset capital, disclosure and exposure limits completed this review in August 2025. | [S:974899bbef4d, p.1] |

**Tokenisation and digital money**

| Term | Plain definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tokenised deposit | A commercial bank deposit in digital token form. The bank itself issues it. | [S:5a9cad53f2be, para 2]; [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Project Ensemble'] |
| Tokenised product (HKMA) | A digital token version of a real-world investment product, recorded on DLT. In HKMA usage, it covers products not regulated under the SFO, and excludes stablecoins. The sales rules for the underlying product generally apply to its tokenised form too. | [S:b2719f3dcb37, 'Coverage', fn 1; 'General principle', pp.1-2] |
| Tokenised security (SFC) | A traditional security, such as a share or bond, wrapped in a digital token. The SFC judges whether it is complex by looking through to the underlying security. | [S:a9aa06fbcc12, paras 23-24] |
| Cash token | A digital token that stands in for cash. In Project Evergreen, the CMU minted (created) each token as a claim on the HKMA for one Hong Kong dollar. | [S:a0b44be27c40, para 33] |
| Non-native issuance | A bond issued first in the normal way, then turned into tokens on a DLT platform. In Project Evergreen, the CMU made its book entries first. The investors' beneficial interests (ownership rights) were then recorded on the ledger. | [S:a0b44be27c40, paras 25-26] |
| CMU / CMU OmniClear | CMU: the Central Moneymarkets Unit, a bond settlement system. CMU OmniClear: the company that runs it for the HKMA. The HKMA owns the CMU. CMU OmniClear Limited is a wholly owned subsidiary of the Exchange Fund (Hong Kong's official reserves), set up in October 2024. | [S:d43a14fd959f, paras 1-3] |
| Digital Bond Grant Scheme | An HKMA grant for issuing digital bonds in Hong Kong. It pays up to HK$2.5 million per eligible digital bond issue. It is open for an initial three years from November 2024. | [S:633ce27d4f9b, paras 2-3] |
| Supervisory Incubator | An HKMA arrangement that lets banks check their DLT risk controls before a full launch. Banks can choose to run live trials. | [S:1b1a0de5de5a, paras 1, 3] |
| DLT | Distributed ledger technology: a database shared and copied across many computers, such as a blockchain. The HKMA's non-binding considerations expect an AI's board and senior management to take full responsibility for DLT risks. | [S:1884530ff235, Introduction; item 1] |
| CBDC / e-HKD | CBDC: central bank digital currency, meaning money a central bank issues in digital form. The e-HKD is the HKMA's possible digital Hong Kong dollar, for wholesale and retail uses. The HKMA currently gives priority to wholesale use (between banks and other financial institutions). | [S:d51dc8884c8a, introduction] |
| Wholesale CBDC (wCBDC) | Central bank digital money used for settlement between banks. It is the basis of Project Ensemble. | [S:5a9cad53f2be, paras 1-2] |
| Project e-HKD+ | The new name for Project e-HKD from September 2024. The project widened from the e-HKD alone to new forms of digital money, including tokenised deposits. | [S:2f93aa2b9560, para 1] |
| Three-rail approach | The HKMA's three-part path for the e-HKD. Rail 1 lays the foundations. Rail 2 covers use cases and pilots. Rail 3 covers implementation planning. | [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Three-rail approach'] |
| Project Ensemble / EnsembleTX | Project Ensemble: HKMA work on settling deals in tokenised money. EnsembleTX: its pilot phase using real money, from November 2025. | [S:8b67e8dc510f, paras 1-2] |
| Ensemble Architecture Community | A group the HKMA formed in May 2024. Its members are regulators, banks and technology firms. It develops industry standards so that systems can work together (interoperability). | [S:0169bc8fbb4d, paras 1-3] |
| mBridge | A shared platform for cross-border payments in several central banks' digital currencies. It settles payment-versus-payment (both currencies swap at once), in real time, 24/7. | [S:6b0fae230518, para 2] |
| Off-bridge compliance | Doing compliance checks outside the mBridge platform. Banks meet each jurisdiction's KYC and AML/CFT rules off the platform. | [S:89b8fcadfaa8, p.3] |
| PvP / DvP | PvP: payment-versus-payment. DvP: delivery-versus-payment. In PvP, two payments in different currencies settle together. In DvP, an asset and its payment settle together. The Ensemble Sandbox was built to test both for settlement between banks. The SFC describes DvP as a simultaneous exchange of assets that lowers the risk of one side not delivering. | [S:460620ab7f2b, para 2]; [S:f009f1a6d615, para 13] |
| Atomic settlement | Settling both sides of a deal at the same instant. Each side depends on the other. | [S:9f8886b0fb21, s.4.1.3, p.30] |
| Purpose-bound money | Money with rules attached about how it can be used. It works as a 'wrapper' that sets conditions while leaving the underlying value intact. Phase 2 pilots examined it as an alternative to a natively programmable e-HKD (one with rules built into the money itself). A natively programmable e-HKD could weaken fungibility (every unit being interchangeable). | [S:bdefe7fc69d4, Section 4 'Programmability', p.33; Glossary, p.43] |
| Access Enabler | In Project Sela, a new type of go-between that serves retail CBDC users directly. It handles tasks such as onboarding and KYC. It does not hold users' CBDC or its own liquidity (cash reserves). | [S:f9000467b0ee, Section 2, p.8] |
| e-CNY tier-4 wallet | A basic personal wallet for the digital renminbi. It is opened with a Hong Kong mobile number, without real-name checks. The balance is capped at 10,000 renminbi. It allows retail payments only, not person-to-person transfers. | [S:08caafe0cfe7, Annex 2] |

**Tax and financial-crime data**

| Term | Plain definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| CARF | Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF): an OECD standard for tax authorities to swap crypto-asset tax data automatically each year. | [S:ea81f92ea327, para 2] |
| RCASP / HKRCASP | RCASP: a reporting crypto-asset service provider. HKRCASP: an RCASP that meets a test of its link to Hong Kong. Under the 2026 Bill, an RCASP is a business that carries out crypto exchange transactions for customers. | [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, ss.50M-50N] |

| SEMP | Specified electronic money product (SEMP): a type of e-money product. CARF leaves it out, but the amended CRS brings it in. | [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 1.5, 4.3] |

| DATAS / AMLAS | DATAS: the Digital Asset Transaction Analytics System. AMLAS: the Anti-Money Laundering Analytics System. Both are data-analysis systems the Customs and Excise Department has planned (by 2029). | [S:ef3648ff1610, item 209, p.112] |
| CrypTech | An SFC technology initiative. Its big-data tools for watching markets and for AML surveillance are to switch on in 2027. | [S:ef3648ff1610, para 51(iii)] |
| FATF targeted update | A regular FATF report on how countries apply its standards for VAs and VASPs. The sixth was published on 26 June 2025. | [S:1183e71e6dda, p.3, para (e)] |

## Notes on sources

- Where a draft and a final text differ, this page uses the final. The final CRP-1 lets them qualify if extra measures are in place. [S:c77e42e17f7a, Item 13.3]; [S:b7d630146e6c, para 1.3.1]
