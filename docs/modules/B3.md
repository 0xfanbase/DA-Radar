# B3 Governance, risk and technology expectations

For the bank's Head of Compliance (digital assets): after this module you can explain what the HKMA expects of the board, of new-product risk assessment, of DLT and vendor risk, and of private-key control, and point to the paragraph for each.

## In 60 seconds

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) puts digital-asset risk on the board's desk. Its Pillar 2 guideline says the board and senior management should understand the risks of new or complex activities, and names cryptoasset activities as an example. Before any new product or new technology goes live, the bank's anti-money laundering (AML) guideline requires a money laundering and terrorist financing (ML/TF) risk assessment. For distributed ledger technology (DLT), the HKMA published ten non-binding risk considerations in April 2024 and a longer research study in March 2025. Custody, staking and tokenised-product circulars add concrete vendor due-diligence and key-management expectations: keys in tamper-resistant hardware, generated and backed up in Hong Kong, no single point of failure, and 98% of client virtual assets (VAs) in cold storage. Where controls fall short, the HKMA may add capital, require provisions or impose limits.

## Key facts

- Directors and senior managers should understand, across the whole firm, the risks of new or complex activities; CA-G-5 now names cryptoasset activities as an example. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 4.2.8]
- CA-G-5 notes, "Cryptoasset activities and associated risks are relatively new and evolving." The HKMA may respond to gaps with capital add-ons, provisions for foreseeable losses, limits or other measures. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 3.6.6]
- The HKMA expects each bank's board to oversee and endorse a strategic business plan covering products, revenue models, customer engagement, risk management and operations. [S:3ec5b269b5dc, letter body, para 4]
- A bank should spot and weigh the ML/TF risks of new products, business practices, delivery channels and new technologies, before launch, and put mitigants in place. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 2.10-2.11]
- In the HKMA AML/CFT Guideline, the word should is as mandatory as must. [S:7bfdad704174, para 1.6]
- The April 2024 DLT considerations are non-binding and non-exhaustive; the HKMA reviews whether a bank's systems and controls cover DLT's extra risks, without favouring any technology. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, pp.1-2]
- The board and senior management carry full responsibility for DLT adoption; the bank should update technology-risk, business-continuity and outsourcing policies and keep enough in-house DLT expertise. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 1]
- A bank should justify its choice of network type and check that node operators are trustworthy, reliable and diverse; a permissionless network is likely unsuitable for critical functions without compensating controls. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, items 2 and 5]
- Business continuity plans for critical DLT use should test DLT scenarios such as cyberattacks, key loss or theft and forks. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 10]
- The March 2025 study (input from the SFC, the Insurance Authority and the MPF Schemes Authority; 113 firms surveyed) repeats and expands these points; it is research, not a new rule. [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Introduction, p.4]
- For outsourced DLT platforms, the study suggests vetting vendors throughout, contract terms on performance and data security, and backup vendors or failover plans. [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Section 4.2.4, item 1, p.48]
- Before launching custody, a bank should complete a full risk assessment, with board and senior-management oversight before and after launch. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 1]
- VA custody may be delegated only to another AI (or local AI subsidiary), an SFC-licensed VA trading platform, or an HKMA-licensed stablecoin issuer with HKMA consent for its own coins. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 14]
- Custody delegate due diligence now covers independent code review and the vendor's release process, with ongoing security and disaster-recovery testing; ultimate responsibility stays with the bank. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 15, 19]
- Key management expectations: tamper-resistant devices such as hardware security modules (HSMs), with HSM vendor due diligence; seeds and keys generated, stored and backed up in Hong Kong; key sharding against single points of failure; readable details for signers. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a), (b), (i)]
- Where the client assets are VAs, 98% should sit in offline cold storage. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(l) and fn 8]
- For tokenised products, due diligence on issuers and technology vendors should cover key management, theft and hacking safeguards, smart-contract audits and DLT failure plans. [S:b2719f3dcb37, Section A, pp.3-4]
- For staking, the bank should keep control of withdrawal keys, check each blockchain protocol, and vet and monitor any third-party staking provider. [S:4641e6c404d2, p.2, section A; pp.2-3, section C]
- CRP-1 requires policies, governance, staff and IT able to assess cryptoasset risk, with documented evidence for each classification available to the HKMA. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.1]

## What your bank must do

**Obligations**
- [ ] Run the ML/TF risk assessment before launching any VA, tokenisation or stablecoin service or new technology. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 2.10-2.11]
- [ ] Brief the board so it understands cryptoasset risks firm-wide and sets limits within risk appetite. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 4.2.8]
- [ ] Complete a full risk assessment before custody launch, under board oversight. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 1]

**Controls & monitoring**
- [ ] Update technology-risk, continuity and outsourcing policies for DLT factors. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 1]
- [ ] Apply the para 11 key controls to client VAs, including 98% cold storage. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11 and fn 8]
- [ ] Monitor custody systems 24/7, test changes before deployment and track dependencies. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 23-25]
- [ ] Keep classification evidence for every cryptoasset. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.1]

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- [ ] Use the DLT considerations when preparing DLT supervisory submissions. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, p.2]
- [ ] Seek HKMA consent where a licensed stablecoin issuer is to hold keys outside Hong Kong for its own coins. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b) and fn 7]

**Counterparty due diligence**
- [ ] Document due diligence on custody delegates and HSM vendors, and review them regularly. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 11(a), 15]
- [ ] Vet tokenisation platforms and staking providers before use and monitor them after. [S:b2719f3dcb37, Section A, p.3]; [S:4641e6c404d2, section C, p.3]
- [ ] Check that DLT node operators are trustworthy, reliable and diverse. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 5]

## Talking points

**To the CCO**
- The HKMA AML/CFT Guideline requires an ML/TF risk assessment before any new product or technology launch; should there is mandatory. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 1.6, 2.10-2.11]
- The DLT considerations are non-binding, but the HKMA asks banks to use them in DLT submissions. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, p.2]

**To business heads**
- VA custody can only be outsourced to another bank, an SFC-licensed platform, or a licensed stablecoin issuer (own coins, with consent). [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 14]
- Tokenised-product launches need issuer and technology-vendor due diligence first. [S:b2719f3dcb37, Section A, p.3]

**To the CEO**
- The HKMA expects the board to understand cryptoasset risks and to endorse a digital-transformation business plan. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 4.2.8]; [S:3ec5b269b5dc, letter body, para 4]
- Weak crypto controls can bring capital add-ons, provisions or activity limits. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 3.6.6]

**To Risk / CRO**
- Custody keys: HSMs, Hong Kong location, sharding, anti-blind-signing, 98% cold storage for client VAs. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11]
- DLT continuity plans should test key loss, cyberattacks and forks. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 10]

## Common mix-ups

- "The DLT considerations are rules with deadlines." They are non-binding and have no deadline; the custody Annex is where the firm numbers sit. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, p.2]; [S:ce05e67bcdce, fn 8]
- "The March 2025 DLT study sets new rules." It is research that restates the 2024 circular. [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Introduction, p.4]
- "Outsourcing custody moves the risk away." Ultimate responsibility stays with the bank. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 19]
- "Hot-wallet insurance must cover 100%." That fixed cover came from the 2024 Annex; the 2026 Annex makes the bank liable and requires adequate financial resources. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]
- "SFC's VA AML chapter sets our new-product rule." For a bank, the HKMA AML/CFT Guideline governs; RIs only have regard to the SFC chapter. [S:7bfdad704174, Ch.1 fn 1]
