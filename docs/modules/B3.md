# B3 Governance, risk and technology expectations

For the bank's Head of Compliance (digital assets): after this module you can explain what the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) expects of the board, of new-product risk assessment, of distributed ledger technology (DLT) and vendor risk, and of private-key control, and point to the paragraph for each.

## In 60 seconds

The HKMA's Pillar 2 guideline for locally incorporated authorized institutions (AIs) says the board and senior management should understand the risks of new or complex activities, and names cryptoasset activities as an example. [S:349a543ae2f0, Application, p.1; para 4.2.8, p.63] Before any new product, new business practice or new technology goes live, the HKMA anti-money laundering (AML) guideline requires a money laundering and terrorist financing (ML/TF) risk assessment. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 1.6, 2.10-2.11] For DLT, the HKMA published ten non-binding risk considerations in April 2024 and a longer research study in March 2025. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, p.2]; [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Introduction, p.4] Custody, staking and tokenised-product circulars add concrete vendor due-diligence and key-management expectations: keys in tamper-resistant devices, generated and backed up in Hong Kong, no single point of failure, and 98% of client virtual assets (VAs) in cold storage. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a), (b), (i), (l) and fn 8]; [S:4641e6c404d2, section C, pp.2-3]; [S:b2719f3dcb37, Section A, p.3] Where capital or risk management around cryptoassets falls short, the HKMA may consider capital add-ons, provisioning, limits or other measures. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 3.6.6]

## Key facts

- Directors and senior managers of a locally incorporated AI should understand, across the whole firm, the risks of new or complex products and activities; CA-G-5 names cryptoasset activities as an example. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 4.2.8, p.63]
- CA-G-5 notes, "Cryptoasset activities and associated risks are relatively new and evolving." For related gaps in capital adequacy or risk management, the HKMA may consider capital add-ons, provisioning for foreseeable and estimable cryptoasset losses, limits or other measures. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 3.6.6, p.53]
- The HKMA expects each AI's board to oversee and endorse, within six months of the 9 March 2026 circular, a formal strategic business plan that looks at adapting products, revenue models, customer engagement, risk management and operations. [S:3ec5b269b5dc, p.2, para 4]
- A bank should spot and weigh the ML/TF risks of new products, new business practices (including delivery mechanisms) and new or developing technologies, do so before launch, and take measures to manage and mitigate them. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 2.10-2.11]
- In the HKMA AML/CFT Guideline, the word should signals a mandatory requirement, just as must does. [S:7bfdad704174, para 1.6]
- The April 2024 DLT considerations are non-binding and non-exhaustive; in line with a risk-based, technology-neutral approach, the HKMA checks whether a bank has adequate systems and controls for the extra risks DLT may bring. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, pp.1-2]
- The board and senior management carry full responsibility for DLT adoption; the bank should update technology-risk, business-continuity and outsourcing policies for DLT factors as needed, and have enough staff with DLT expertise. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 1]
- A bank should choose a DLT network type that suits the nature and risks of the application, and consider whether node operators are trustworthy, reliable and diverse; a permissionless network would likely be inappropriate for highly critical or sensitive functions without adequate compensating controls. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, items 2 and 5]
- Where DLT supports critical functions, business continuity plans should include DLT test scenarios such as cyberattacks, loss or theft of private keys and forks. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 10]
- The March 2025 study drew on input from the Insurance Authority, the Mandatory Provident Fund Schemes Authority and the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), surveyed 113 financial institutions, and builds on the 2024 circular; it is research, not a new rule. [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Foreword, p.3; Introduction, p.4; Section 4.2.4, p.48]
- For outsourced DLT work, the study suggests assessing vendors at every stage, contract terms on performance and data security, and predefined backup vendors or failover plans. [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Section 4.2.4, item 1, p.48]
- Before launching custody, a bank should complete a comprehensive risk assessment, with effective board and senior-management oversight before and after launch. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 1]
- As a general principle, VA custody may be delegated or outsourced only to another AI (including a locally incorporated AI's subsidiary), an SFC-licensed VA trading platform, or an HKMA-licensed stablecoin issuer that has HKMA consent to hold the stablecoins it issues. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 14]
- Due diligence on a custody delegate covers, among other things, independent code reviews and the provider's software release process, followed by ongoing review of its security controls and disaster-recovery testing; ultimate responsibility stays with the bank. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 15, 19]
- Key management expectations for client VAs: tamper-resistant devices such as hardware security modules (HSMs), with HSM vendor due diligence; seeds and keys generated, stored and backed up in Hong Kong; key sharding or similar against single points of failure; transaction details shown in human-readable form for signers. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a), (b), (i)]
- Where the client assets in custody are VAs, 98% should sit in cold storage. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(l) and fn 8]
- For tokenised products outside the Securities and Futures Ordinance, due diligence on issuers and their technology vendors should cover key management, theft and hacking safeguards, smart-contract audits and contingency plans for DLT failures. [S:b2719f3dcb37, Section A, pp.3-4]
- For staking, the bank should keep possession or control of the means to unstake client VAs (for example, withdrawal-address private keys), do due diligence on each blockchain protocol, and vet and monitor any third-party staking provider. [S:4641e6c404d2, p.2, section A and fn 2; pp.2-3, section C]
- CRP-1 says AIs must have policies, governance, staff and IT able to assess cryptoasset risk, and must document the basis for each classification and make it available to the HKMA on request. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.1]

## What your bank must do

**Obligations**
- [ ] Run the ML/TF risk assessment before launching any new product (for example, a VA, tokenisation or stablecoin service), new business practice or new technology. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 2.10-2.11]
- [ ] Brief the board so it understands cryptoasset risks firm-wide and sets limits within risk appetite. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 4.2.8, pp.63-64]
- [ ] Complete a comprehensive risk assessment before custody launch, under board oversight. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 1]

**Controls & monitoring**
- [ ] Update technology-risk, continuity and outsourcing policies for DLT factors. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 1]
- [ ] Apply the para 11 key controls to client VAs, including 98% cold storage. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11 and fn 8]
- [ ] Monitor custody security 24/7, test system changes before deployment and monitor dependencies. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 23-25]
- [ ] Keep documented classification evidence for each cryptoasset exposure. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.1]

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- [ ] Take the DLT considerations into account when preparing DLT-related submissions, as the HKMA encourages. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, p.2]
- [ ] Obtain HKMA consent before appointing an HKMA-licensed stablecoin issuer to hold the stablecoins it issues; its keys must be kept in Hong Kong or a location the HKMA accepts. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b) and fn 7; para 14]

**Counterparty due diligence**
- [ ] Document due diligence on custody delegates and HSM vendors, and review them regularly. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 11(a), 15]
- [ ] Vet tokenisation platforms and staking providers before use and monitor them after. [S:b2719f3dcb37, Section A, p.3]; [S:4641e6c404d2, section C, p.3]
- [ ] Consider whether DLT node operators are trustworthy, reliable and diverse. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 5]

## Talking points

**To the CCO**
- The HKMA AML/CFT Guideline requires an ML/TF risk assessment before any new product, practice or technology launch; should there is mandatory. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 1.6, 2.10-2.11]
- The DLT considerations are non-binding, but the HKMA encourages banks to take them into account in DLT-related submissions. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, p.2]

**To business heads**
- As a general principle, VA custody can only be outsourced to another AI, an SFC-licensed platform, or a licensed stablecoin issuer (its own coins, with HKMA consent). [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 14]
- Tokenised-product sales need issuer and technology-vendor due diligence before offering to customers. [S:b2719f3dcb37, Section A, p.3]

**To the CEO**
- The HKMA expects the board to understand cryptoasset risks and to endorse a digital-transformation business plan within six months of 9 March 2026. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 4.2.8]; [S:3ec5b269b5dc, p.2, para 4]
- Crypto-related gaps in capital or risk management may lead the HKMA to consider capital add-ons, provisioning or limits. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 3.6.6]

**To Risk / CRO**
- Custody keys: HSMs, Hong Kong location, sharding, anti-blind-signing, 98% cold storage for client VAs. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11 and fn 8]
- DLT continuity plans should test key loss, cyberattacks and forks. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 10]

## Common mix-ups

- "The DLT considerations are rules with deadlines." They are non-binding and set no deadline; the custody Annex is where the firm numbers sit. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, p.2]; [S:ce05e67bcdce, fn 8]
- "The March 2025 DLT study sets new rules." It is a research study that builds on the 2024 circular. [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Introduction, p.4; Section 4.2.4, p.48]
- "Outsourcing custody moves the risk away." Ultimate responsibility stays with the bank. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 19]
- "Insurance must cover 100% of hot-wallet assets." That fixed cover (50% cold, 100% hot and other storage) came from the February 2024 guidance, which the May 2026 circular superseded; the 2026 Annex instead makes the bank liable for losses attributable to it and requires adequate financial resources, which may include insurance. [S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8]; [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.2]; [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]
- "SFC's VA AML chapter sets our new-product rule." For a bank, the HKMA AML/CFT Guideline governs; RIs must also have regard to the SFC guideline, including its VA chapter. [S:7bfdad704174, para 1.3 and fn 1]

## Open items

- The HKMA AML/CFT Guideline for AIs has no stablecoin, unhosted-wallet or VA red-flag provisions; it mentions VAs only in passing (VA transfers as occasional transactions, VA service providers in correspondent-type relationships). See D3 for how the gap is handled. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 4.2.1, 11.23]
- No general HKMA outsourcing or technology-risk module is in this corpus; only DLT- and custody-specific outsourcing text is covered.
- Two CA-G-5 V.7 copies exist: one dated 27 November 2025 with a placeholder header date (xx.12.2025) and the 19 December 2025 version. This module cites the 19 December 2025 text; the crypto paragraphs match. [S:04bbb675089e, p.1]; [S:349a543ae2f0, p.1]
