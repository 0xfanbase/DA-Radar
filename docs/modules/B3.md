# B3 Governance, risk and technology expectations

This module is for the bank's Head of Compliance (digital assets). After it, you can explain what the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) expects in four areas. You can also point to the paragraph for each:

- the board's role;
- risk assessment of new products;
- distributed ledger technology (DLT), the shared-database technology behind crypto, and vendor risk;
- control of private keys (the secret codes that move digital assets).

## In 60 seconds

The HKMA's Supervisory Policy Manual (SPM) has a Pillar 2 module, CA-G-5. [S:349a543ae2f0, Application, p.1; para 4.2.8, p.63] Pillar 2 is the HKMA's review of whether a bank holds enough capital for all its risks. [S:349a543ae2f0, Application, p.1; para 4.2.8, p.63] CA-G-5 applies to locally incorporated authorized institutions (AIs), meaning banks and deposit-takers set up as companies in Hong Kong. [S:349a543ae2f0, Application, p.1; para 4.2.8, p.63] CA-G-5 says the board and senior management should understand the risks of new or complex activities. [S:349a543ae2f0, Application, p.1; para 4.2.8, p.63] It names cryptoasset activities as an example. [S:349a543ae2f0, Application, p.1; para 4.2.8, p.63]

The HKMA has a guideline on anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-financing of terrorism (CFT). [S:7bfdad704174, paras 1.6, 2.10-2.11] Before any new product, business practice or technology goes live, that guideline requires a risk assessment. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 1.6, 2.10-2.11] The assessment covers money laundering (ML) and terrorist financing (TF) risks. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 1.6, 2.10-2.11]

For DLT, the HKMA published ten risk considerations in April 2024, and they are not binding. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, p.2] It followed with a longer research study in March 2025. [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Introduction, p.4]

Circulars on custody (holding assets for clients), staking and tokenised products add concrete expectations. They cover vendor due diligence (checks on suppliers) and key management. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a), (b), (i), (l) and fn 8]; [S:4641e6c404d2, section C, pp.2-3]; [S:b2719f3dcb37, Section A, p.3] The main key-management expectations are:

- keys held in tamper-resistant devices; [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a), (b), (i)]
- keys generated and backed up in Hong Kong; [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a), (b), (i)]
- no "single point of failure" (one part whose loss stops everything); [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a), (b), (i)]
- 98% of client virtual assets (VA) in cold storage, with keys kept offline. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(l) and fn 8]

If a bank's capital or risk management around cryptoassets falls short, the HKMA may consider extra measures. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 3.6.6] These include capital add-ons (extra capital), provisioning (money set aside for losses), limits or other measures. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 3.6.6]

## Key facts

- Directors and senior managers of a locally incorporated AI should understand the firm-wide risks of new or complex products and activities. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 4.2.8, p.63] CA-G-5 names cryptoasset activities as an example. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 4.2.8, p.63]
- CA-G-5 notes: "Cryptoasset activities and associated risks are relatively new and evolving." [S:349a543ae2f0, para 3.6.6, p.53] For related gaps in capital adequacy (having enough capital) or risk management, the HKMA may consider these measures: [S:349a543ae2f0, para 3.6.6, p.53]
  - capital add-ons (extra capital on top of the minimum);
  - provisioning (setting money aside) for foreseeable and estimable cryptoasset losses;
  - limits;
  - other measures.
- The HKMA expects each AI's board to oversee and endorse a formal strategic business plan. [S:3ec5b269b5dc, p.2, para 4] The board has six months from the 9 March 2026 circular. [S:3ec5b269b5dc, p.2, para 4] The plan looks at adapting products, revenue models, customer engagement, risk management and operations. [S:3ec5b269b5dc, p.2, para 4]
- A bank should identify and weigh the ML/TF risks of: [S:7bfdad704174, paras 2.10-2.11]
  - new products;
  - new business practices, including delivery mechanisms (how the product reaches customers);
  - new or developing technologies.

  It should do this assessment before launch. It should then take measures to manage and reduce the risks it finds. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 2.10-2.11]
- In the HKMA AML/CFT Guideline, the word "should" signals a mandatory requirement, just as "must" does. [S:7bfdad704174, para 1.6]
- The April 2024 DLT considerations are non-binding and non-exhaustive (not a complete list). [S:cd5d66ba08a2, pp.1-2] The HKMA supervises on a risk-based, technology-neutral basis. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, pp.1-2] It checks whether a bank has adequate systems and controls for the extra risks DLT may bring. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, pp.1-2]
- The board and senior management carry full responsibility for DLT adoption. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 1] The bank should update its technology-risk, business-continuity and outsourcing policies for DLT factors as needed, and have enough staff with DLT expertise. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 1]
- A bank should choose a DLT network type that suits the application's nature and risks. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, items 2 and 5] It should also consider whether the node operators (the parties that validate and confirm changes to the ledger) are trustworthy, reliable and diverse. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, items 2 and 5] A permissionless network (one anyone can join) would likely be inappropriate for highly critical or sensitive functions without adequate compensating controls. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, items 2 and 5]
- Where a bank uses DLT for critical functions, its business continuity plans should include DLT test scenarios such as: [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 10]
  - cyberattacks;
  - loss or theft of private keys;
  - forks (splits of a blockchain into two versions).
- The March 2025 study drew on input from three other regulators. [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Foreword, p.3; Introduction, p.4; Section 4.2.4, p.48] They are the Insurance Authority, the Mandatory Provident Fund Schemes Authority and the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC). [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Foreword, p.3; Introduction, p.4; Section 4.2.4, p.48] It surveyed 113 financial institutions and builds on the 2024 circular. [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Foreword, p.3; Introduction, p.4; Section 4.2.4, p.48] The study is research, not a new rule. [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Foreword, p.3; Introduction, p.4; Section 4.2.4, p.48]
- For outsourced DLT work, the study suggests three steps: [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Section 4.2.4, item 1, p.48]
  - assess vendors at every stage;
  - set contract terms on performance and data security;
  - line up backup vendors or failover plans (switching to a backup) in advance.
- Before launching custody, a bank should complete a comprehensive risk assessment, with effective oversight by the board and senior management before and after launch. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 1]
- As a general principle, a bank may delegate or outsource VA custody only to one of three types of firm: [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 14]
  - another AI, or a subsidiary of an AI that is itself incorporated in Hong Kong;
  - a VA trading platform licensed by the SFC;
  - an HKMA-licensed stablecoin issuer that has HKMA consent to hold the stablecoins it issues.
- Due diligence on a custody delegate covers, among other things, independent code reviews and the provider's software release process. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 15, 19] After that, the bank keeps reviewing the provider's security controls and disaster-recovery testing. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 15, 19] Ultimate responsibility stays with the bank. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 15, 19]
- The HKMA sets these key-management expectations for client VAs: [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a), (b), (i)]
  - Keep keys in tamper-resistant devices such as hardware security modules (HSM), and do due diligence on the HSM vendor.
  - Generate, store and back up seeds (master secrets that create keys) and keys in Hong Kong.
  - Use key sharding (splitting a key into parts held separately) or similar, so no single point of failure exists.
  - Show signers the transaction details in human-readable form.
- If the client assets in custody are VAs, 98% should sit in cold storage. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(l) and fn 8]
- Some tokenised products (assets recorded as tokens on a DLT network) fall outside the Securities and Futures Ordinance. [S:b2719f3dcb37, Section A, pp.3-4] For these, due diligence on issuers and their technology vendors should cover: [S:b2719f3dcb37, Section A, pp.3-4]
  - key management;
  - safeguards against theft and hacking;
  - smart-contract audits (checks of the code that runs on the blockchain);
  - contingency plans for DLT failures.
- For staking (locking up tokens to help run a blockchain, for rewards), the bank should do three things: [S:4641e6c404d2, p.2, section A and fn 2; pp.2-3, section C]
  - Keep possession or control of the means to unstake client VAs, such as the private keys of withdrawal addresses.
  - Do due diligence on each blockchain protocol.
  - Vet and monitor any third-party staking provider.
- CRP-1 says AIs must have policies, governance, staff and information technology able to assess cryptoasset risk. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.1] They must document the basis for each classification and make it available to the HKMA on request. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.1]

## What your bank must do

**Obligations**
- [ ] Run the ML/TF risk assessment before launching any new product, new business practice or new technology. Examples of new products are a VA, tokenisation or stablecoin service. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 2.10-2.11]
- [ ] Brief the board so it understands cryptoasset risks firm-wide and can set limits. Those limits sit within the bank's risk appetite (the level of risk it is willing to accept). [S:349a543ae2f0, para 4.2.8, pp.63-64]
- [ ] Complete a comprehensive risk assessment before custody launch, under board oversight. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 1]

**Controls & monitoring**
- [ ] Update technology-risk, continuity and outsourcing policies for DLT factors. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 1]
- [ ] Apply the para 11 key controls to client VAs, including 98% cold storage. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11 and fn 8]
- [ ] Monitor custody security 24/7, test system changes before deployment and monitor dependencies. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 23-25]
- [ ] Keep documented classification evidence for each cryptoasset exposure. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.1]

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- [ ] Take the DLT considerations into account when preparing DLT-related submissions. The HKMA encourages this. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, p.2]
- [ ] Obtain HKMA consent before appointing an HKMA-licensed stablecoin issuer to hold the stablecoins it issues. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b) and fn 7; para 14] The issuer's keys must be kept in Hong Kong or a location the HKMA accepts. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b) and fn 7; para 14]

**Counterparty due diligence**
- [ ] Document due diligence on custody delegates and HSM vendors, and review them regularly. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 11(a), 15]
- [ ] Vet tokenisation platforms and staking providers before use, and monitor them afterwards. [S:b2719f3dcb37, Section A, p.3]; [S:4641e6c404d2, section C, p.3]
- [ ] Consider whether DLT node operators are trustworthy, reliable and diverse. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 5]

## Talking points

**To the CCO**
- The HKMA AML/CFT Guideline requires an ML/TF risk assessment before any new product, practice or technology launch. In that guideline, "should" is mandatory. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 1.6, 2.10-2.11]
- The DLT considerations are non-binding. Even so, the HKMA encourages banks to take them into account in DLT-related submissions. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, p.2]

**To business heads**
- As a general principle, we can outsource VA custody only to another AI, an SFC-licensed platform, or a licensed stablecoin issuer. A stablecoin issuer qualifies only for the coins it issues, and only with HKMA consent. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 14]
- Before we offer tokenised products to customers, we need due diligence on the issuer and its technology vendors. [S:b2719f3dcb37, Section A, p.3]

**To the CEO**
- The HKMA expects the board to understand cryptoasset risks. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 4.2.8] It also expects the board to endorse a digital-transformation business plan within six months of 9 March 2026. [S:3ec5b269b5dc, p.2, para 4]
- If our capital or risk management for crypto falls short, the HKMA may consider capital add-ons, provisioning or limits. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 3.6.6]

**To Risk / CRO**
- For custody keys, the HKMA looks for HSM devices, a Hong Kong location and sharding. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11 and fn 8] It also looks for no blind signing (approving unseen details) and 98% of client VAs in cold storage. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11 and fn 8]
- DLT continuity plans should test key loss, cyberattacks and forks. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, item 10]

## Common mix-ups

- **Myth:** "The DLT considerations are rules with deadlines." **Fact:** They are non-binding and set no deadline. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, p.2] The firm numbers sit in the custody Annex. [S:ce05e67bcdce, fn 8]
- **Myth:** "The March 2025 DLT study sets new rules." **Fact:** It is a research study. It builds on the 2024 circular. [S:e0fd558b3dbc, Introduction, p.4; Section 4.2.4, p.48]
- **Myth:** "Outsourcing custody moves the risk away." **Fact:** Ultimate responsibility stays with the bank. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 19]
- **Myth:** "Insurance must cover 100% of hot-wallet assets." **Fact:** That fixed cover came from the February 2024 guidance. [S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8] It set 50% cover for cold storage, and 100% for hot storage (keys kept online) and other storage. [S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8] The May 2026 circular replaced that guidance. [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.2] The 2026 Annex instead makes the bank liable for losses attributable to it. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)] It also requires adequate financial resources, which may include insurance. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]
- **Myth:** "The SFC's VA chapter on money laundering sets our new-product rule." **Fact:** For a bank, the HKMA guideline governs. [S:7bfdad704174, para 1.3 and fn 1] RIs must also take into account the SFC guideline, including its VA chapter. [S:7bfdad704174, para 1.3 and fn 1]

## Open items

- The HKMA AML/CFT Guideline for AIs has no provisions on stablecoins, unhosted wallets (wallets held without a service provider) or VA red flags (warning signs). [S:7bfdad704174, paras 4.2.1, 11.23] The guideline mentions VAs only in passing, in two places: [S:7bfdad704174, paras 4.2.1, 11.23]
  - VA transfers as occasional transactions (one-off dealings outside an ongoing relationship);
  - VA service providers in correspondent-type relationships (a bank providing services similar to correspondent banking to another firm).
  
  See D3 for how the gap is handled.
- No general HKMA outsourcing or technology-risk module is among the documents reviewed for this guide. Only DLT- and custody-specific outsourcing text is covered.
- Two copies of CA-G-5 V.7 exist. One is dated 27 November 2025 and has a placeholder header date (xx.12.2025). The other is the 19 December 2025 version. [S:04bbb675089e, p.1]; [S:349a543ae2f0, p.1] This module cites the 19 December 2025 text, and the crypto paragraphs match. [S:04bbb675089e, p.1]; [S:349a543ae2f0, p.1]

## Check yourself

1. Which HKMA guideline requires a money-laundering and terrorist-financing risk assessment before a new VA product launches?
   **Answer:** The HKMA's AML/CFT Guideline requires this assessment before any new product, business practice or technology goes live. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 1.6, 2.10-2.11]
2. What percentage of client virtual assets should sit in cold storage under the custody Annex?
   **Answer:** 98% of client VAs should sit in cold storage, with keys kept offline. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(l) and fn 8]
3. Are the HKMA's April 2024 DLT risk considerations binding rules?
   **Answer:** No. They are non-binding and non-exhaustive. The HKMA supervises DLT on a risk-based, technology-neutral basis. [S:cd5d66ba08a2, pp.1-2]
4. As a general principle, to which three types of firm may your bank delegate or outsource VA custody?
   **Answer:** First, another AI or a locally incorporated AI's subsidiary. Second, an SFC-licensed VA trading platform. Third, an HKMA-licensed stablecoin issuer, for the coins it issues and with HKMA consent. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 14]
5. Does outsourcing VA custody move ultimate responsibility away from the bank?
   **Answer:** No. Ultimate responsibility stays with the bank, even after due diligence and outsourcing. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 15, 19]
