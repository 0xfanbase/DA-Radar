# F2 How regulation writes the P&L

A P&L (profit and loss statement) shows what a business earns and what it spends. After reading, you can trace the main cost lines of a digital-asset business back to the rule that sets them. You can explain to a Chief Financial Officer (CFO) why the same client asset can cost very different amounts of capital, liquidity and staff time.

## In 60 seconds

Capital comes first. The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) is the global body that writes bank capital standards. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) brought the Basel cryptoasset standard into force in Hong Kong on 1 January 2026. It amended its capital, disclosure and exposure-limits rules to do so. [S:100b982aadca, p.90] Group 2b, the group for cryptoassets that fail the other tests, carries a 1,250% risk weight. [S:9e8d84c87168, Part XII CAE1 explanatory note, column (c), p.215] A risk weight scales how much capital a bank holds against an asset. The minimum total capital ratio is 8%. [S:5d0c13e09312, para 2.1.2] So 1,250% times 8% is 100%: at the minimum, the bank holds capital equal to the whole exposure.

Liquidity comes second. The HKMA sets how tokenised deposits and stablecoins count in its liquidity tests. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, paras 4-7] A stablecoin is a token built to keep a steady value against a currency. A bank that issues a stablecoin redeemable within the 30-day test period must generally assume all of it flows out. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 7]

Custody brings liability. The HKMA expects a bank custodian to be liable to clients for loss of their digital assets from an incident attributable to the bank. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)] Licences bring fixed costs. Non-bank firms face minimum capital. [S:f1bbafccd556, para 6.2; S:f41a543388bc, paras 41-42] There are licence fees. [S:6186c01c6649, para 67] A registered bank needs named senior managers for each activity. [S:8934af8cc884, para 2.2.4] A bill for the Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF), a global tax-reporting standard, was introduced into the Legislative Council (LegCo) on 3 June 2026. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview section]

## Key facts

**Capital**
- Every cryptoasset exposure sits in Group 1a (tokenised traditional assets), Group 1b (stablecoins with an effective stabilisation mechanism) or Group 2 (the rest). [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 1.3.1-1.3.3]
- Group 1a exposures in the banking book generally follow the capital rules for the underlying traditional asset. [S:5d0c13e09312, para 8.7.1]
- A Group 2 asset reaches Group 2a, where limited hedging counts, only if it meets all three hedging recognition criteria. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 3.1.1] One test is an average market capitalisation of at least HK$78 billion over the previous year. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 3.1.1] Group 2 assets that get no hedging recognition are Group 2b. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 1.3.3]
- Group 2b exposures take a 1,250% risk weight. [S:9e8d84c87168, Part XII CAE1 explanatory note, column (c), p.215] This conservative treatment generally also covers credit valuation adjustment (CVA) risk, the risk of loss from a trading counterparty's weaker credit. [S:5d845665ba83, para 4.3.2]
- A bank must notify the HKMA before acquiring a new type of cryptoasset. It treats the asset as Group 2b until the HKMA agrees the bank's classification. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.2]
- The HKMA's disclosure notes describe a Group 2 exposure limit of 1% of Tier 1 capital (a bank's core capital). [S:9e8d84c87168, Part XII CAE1 explanatory note, row 8, p.215] Only a bank designated as globally or domestically systemically important faces a capital add-on when its Group 2 exposure ratio exceeds the set limits. [S:5d0c13e09312, para 8.7.4(d)] For such a bank, the add-on covers Group 2 exposure between 1% and 2% of Tier 1 capital. [S:9e8d84c87168, Part XII CAE1 explanatory note, row 9, p.215] Above 2%, such a bank reports one total risk-weighted amount for all its Group 2 exposures. [S:9e8d84c87168, Part XII CAE1 explanatory note, row 10, p.215]
- A registered institution (RI) is a bank that the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has registered for securities business. The HKMA says an RI must treat a loan backed only by virtual asset (VA) collateral as a clean (unsecured) loan for capital. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (9), p.5]

**Liquidity**
- The liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) tests whether a bank holds enough easy-to-sell assets for 30 days of stressed outflows. The net stable funding ratio (NSFR) tests whether long-term assets have stable funding. (concept)
- The HKMA's liquidity rules for cryptoassets below apply to category 1 institutions, the banks that run the LCR. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, paras 1-9]
- High-quality liquid assets (HQLA) are assets a bank can sell fast in a crisis. A cryptoasset counts as HQLA only if it is a Group 1a token of an eligible asset. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 1] It must also pass the usual HQLA tests. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 1]
- A bank that issues tokenised deposits meeting set conditions may treat them as deposits or unsecured funding. Even then, they cannot count as stable retail deposits. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4] If the bank cannot always identify the holders, it must treat them as other unsecured wholesale funding. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4]
- A bank holding another bank's tokenised deposits treats them like unsecured loans to financial institutions. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 5]
- A bank holding a stablecoin generally counts a 0% LCR inflow and an 85% required stable funding factor. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 7] A bank issuing one must apply a 100% LCR outflow if holders can redeem within the LCR period. Lower rates may apply if the backing assets would qualify as HQLA. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 7]
- The HKMA may apply stricter liquidity treatment to a cryptoasset. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 9] One example it gives: a bank that issues a stablecoin, or is a major service provider to one, may need to fund redemptions without a contract, to protect its franchise. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, fn 98]
- Stablecoin reserves may include bank deposits with terms of up to three months. [S:240be6833157, para 2.3.1] In June 2026 the Government said HKMA rules have issuers place reserve assets with banks in Hong Kong. [S:c6df52e680de, Reply (1)]
- The HKMA expects an issuer to manage the reserves' intraday liquidity, taking into account trading hours and early-withdrawal terms on deposits. [S:240be6833157, para 6.4.3]
- The 2026 Policy Address says the HKMA plans 24/7 operations for EnsembleTX (its tokenisation pilot) by around end-2026. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 50] It also plans settlement in central bank digital currency by then. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 50] The HKMA also plans tests of tokenised Exchange Fund Bills (short-term HKMA debt) by end-2026, to help banks use them round the clock. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 35(ii)]

**Custody liability and insurance**
- The HKMA expects a bank custodian to keep adequate financial resources for potential losses, which may include insurance. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)] The May 2026 circular replaced the HKMA's February 2024 custody circular. [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.2]
- The HKMA expects 98% of client VA holdings in cold storage, meaning keys kept offline. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(l) and fn 8] It also expects security monitoring to run 24/7. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 23]
- For licensed trading platforms, the SFC expects an approved compensation arrangement covering 50% of client VA holdings in cold storage and 100% elsewhere. [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.22]

**Licence capital and fees**
- A licensed trading platform must always hold HK$5 million or more in paid-up capital. [S:f1bbafccd556, para 6.2] Its required liquid capital is at least HK$3 million. [S:f1bbafccd556, para 6.3]
- Under the planned custodian regime, the SFC is to set baseline minimums for non-bank VA custodians only. These are HK$10 million paid-up capital and HK$3 million liquid capital. [S:f41a543388bc, paras 41-42] Banks are left out because HKMA capital requirements already apply to them. [S:f41a543388bc, para 41]
- A licensed stablecoin issuer needs paid-up capital of at least HK$25 million. [S:240be6833157, para 5.1.1] A bank licensee follows the Banking Ordinance instead. [S:240be6833157, para 5.1.3]
- Licence fees for VA dealing are to be benchmarked to fees for Type 1 (securities dealing) under the securities law. [S:6186c01c6649, para 38] For VA advice and management, the May 2026 conclusions benchmark fees to Types 4 and 9 (securities advice and asset management). [S:01c5c5c4045f, para 59] As at December 2025, each of those types cost an RI HK$23,500 to apply and HK$35,000 a year. [S:6186c01c6649, para 67]
- An RI needs at least two executive officers for each regulated activity, each approved in writing by the Monetary Authority. [S:8934af8cc884, para 2.2.4]
- Banks already assessed by the HKMA for VA custody, and already providing it, are to get faster licensing under the custodian regime. [S:f41a543388bc, para 51]

**Compliance and technology**
- The HKMA requires registered institutions to take into account the SFC's anti-money laundering (AML) guideline for VA matters. [S:7bfdad704174, para 1.4 and fn 1] Under that guideline, for a VA transfer of HK$8,000 or more, the sending firm must obtain fuller sender and recipient details. It must pass them to the receiving firm securely and immediately. [S:08a762f7233e, paras 12.11.5-12.11.10] This is known as the travel rule.
- The SFC expects internet brokers and SFC-licensed VA trading platforms to adopt phishing-resistant login and device binding by 8 July 2027. [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 5, 21(c)]
- The HKMA doubled its AML on-site examinations of banks, from 14 in 2024 to 28 in 2025. [S:100b982aadca, p.85]
- On 9 March 2026 the HKMA said it expects every bank's board to oversee and endorse a strategic business plan within six months. The plan responds to distributed ledger technology (DLT) and other technology change. [S:3ec5b269b5dc, letter body, p.2] The HKMA expects banks to discuss new digital-asset activities with it before launch. [S:3ec5b269b5dc, letter body, p.2]

**Tax and accounting**
- The Inland Revenue Department (IRD) says profits from selling digital assets held as long-term investments are not chargeable to profits tax. Whether an asset is capital or trading stock depends on the facts. [S:1afc18524517, para 46]
- Hong Kong-sourced profits from trading or exchanging cryptocurrency as a business are chargeable to profits tax. [S:1afc18524517, para 47]
- The 2026-27 Budget said the Government would classify digital assets as qualifying investments for fund and family-office tax concessions. [S:654fa5eba8cf, para 98] A bill widening those regimes was gazetted on 12 June 2026. It is a bill, not yet law. [S:fca785b80cff, paras 1-2] In August 2026 it was still before a LegCo Bills Committee. If passed, it would apply from the 2025/26 year of assessment. [S:103ce885d306, para 6]
- The SFC reports that the Government confirmed the stamp duty waiver for transfers of Hong Kong-listed exchange-traded funds (ETFs) also covers tokenised ETFs. [S:8e1426ec511f, p.65, 'Driving tokenised products']
- If passed, the CARF bill would start on 1 January 2027. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(3)-(4)] A Hong Kong reporting crypto-asset service provider would have to register with the Commissioner of Inland Revenue. The deadline would be 31 January of the year after it first meets the Hong Kong link tests. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50O(1)-(2)] Failing to comply without reasonable excuse would be an offence punishable by a fine. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(1)-(5)]
- Hong Kong-incorporated banks with cryptoasset exposures must publish a template every six months. It shows how their accounts classify and measure those exposures. [S:9e8d84c87168, Template CAE2, p.216]

## Industry view

- KPMG (an accounting and advisory firm) published its Hong Kong Banking Report 2026, with reported figures for surveyed Hong Kong banks. It found their average cost-to-income ratio fell from 42.1% to 41.9% in 2025. [I:1dfc7a0205ff, Performance section, p.8] The same report found staff costs rose 6.7%. [I:1dfc7a0205ff, Performance section, p.8] A cost-to-income ratio is operating costs divided by operating income.
- Morgan McKinley (a recruitment firm) publishes a salary guide. Its 2026 Hong Kong guide (reported pay ranges) puts a head of compliance at a median of HK$180,000 a month. [I:79e4be81501c, salary table, Compliance rows] Its range runs from HK$130,000 to HK$250,000. [I:79e4be81501c, salary table, Compliance rows]
- A 2022 BCBS study (global, reported, 19 banks) found about half of banks' reported crypto exposures came from custody, wallet, insurance and similar services. [I:d89ae309f669, pp.104-105, section 2.3]
- Two 2026 Hong Kong fund documents are the source here (reported figures). One is from China Asset Management (Hong Kong), the other from Bosera Asset Management (International). Each sets the custodian and administrator fee at up to 1% a year of net asset value. That fee includes the VA sub-custodian. [I:ee50e4efff6f, PDF p.11; I:d81073907a14, PDF p.95] The Bosera prospectus says its sub-custodian will have cover at 50% for cold-storage client assets and 100% for hot-storage ones. [I:d81073907a14, PDF p.118]
- The International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) Interpretations Committee (global standard-setter, 2019 agenda decision) said a cryptocurrency holding is not cash or a financial asset. [I:21d967bdefd5, pp.1-2] Such holdings fall under the inventory standard when held for sale in ordinary business, and otherwise under the intangible-asset standard. [I:21d967bdefd5, p.1, 'Which IFRS Standard applies']

## How it works commercially

- **Capital cost.** A business line uses capital. The bank expects a return on it, often called a hurdle rate (the minimum return a project must beat). A higher risk weight means more capital, so the line must earn more to clear the hurdle. (concept)
- **Balance-sheet or fee business.** Holding a coin on the bank's own books uses capital. Holding it for a client in segregated custody mainly uses operations and liability cover. (concept) The HKMA expects client assets to be kept apart from the bank's own. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 6 and fn 6]
- **Funding value.** Funding that liquidity rules treat as stable is worth more to a bank than wholesale funding. (concept)
- **Net interest margin.** Net interest margin is the gap between what a bank earns on assets and pays on funding. Reserve deposits from a stablecoin issuer earn this gap for the bank that holds them. (concept)
- **Fixed and variable costs.** Key management, monitoring and licences cost roughly the same at low and high volume. Fees on trades or assets grow with volume. So scale decides whether a line covers its costs. (concept)

The business lines show these levers one by one. See [Virtual-asset custody](#b-custody), [Stablecoin roles for banks](#b-stablecoin-roles), [Tokenised deposits and payments](#b-tokenised-deposits), [Virtual-asset dealing](#b-va-dealing) and [Virtual-asset financing and margin](#b-va-financing).

> **Analysis — not official**
> **Question:** For a new digital-asset product, what does one extra unit of client business add to profit after the rules take their share?
> **How to think about it:**
> - Start with income per unit: a fee on assets held, a spread on trades, or a margin on deposits.
> - Take off variable costs per unit: the travel-rule data, blockchain monitoring and any insurance priced on value held.
> - Charge the capital the unit uses, at the bank's hurdle rate. A unit on the bank's own books in Group 2b carries a 1,250% risk weight [S:9e8d84c87168, Part XII CAE1 explanatory note, column (c), p.215]. The HKMA expects a unit held for a client in custody to be kept apart from the bank's own assets [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 6].
> - Charge the liquidity cost: funding that counts as wholesale is worth less than stable retail funding [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4].
> - Then compare the total contribution with the fixed cost of keys, staff, licences and named managers. A line with a small contribution per unit needs high volume to break even.
> **What it depends on:** the asset's capital group; whether the bank owns the asset or only safekeeps it; volumes and fee levels; the hurdle rate; the final licensing rules.
> **Official signposts:** HKMA classification decisions on new asset types [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.2]; the custodian bill and its start date [S:f41a543388bc, para 65]; EnsembleTX moving to 24/7 [S:ef3648ff1610, para 50]; progress of the CARF bill [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview section].
> **What this is not:** Not a forecast, not advice, and not the regulator's view.

## Common mix-ups

- **Myth:** Custody of client coins costs the same as holding coins ourselves. **Fact:** The 1,250% weight applies to Group 2b cryptoasset exposures. [S:9e8d84c87168, Part XII CAE1 explanatory note, column (c), p.215] For custody, the HKMA expects segregation, and liability backed by adequate financial resources. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 6, 11(n)]
- **Myth:** Tokenised deposits are just deposits for liquidity. **Fact:** They cannot get stable retail deposit rates. Unknown holders mean wholesale treatment. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3, para 4]
- **Myth:** Banks need the 50%/100% insurance cover. **Fact:** That is the SFC's expectation for trading platforms. [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.22] The HKMA's 2026 guidance for banks uses liability plus adequate financial resources. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]
- **Myth:** Crypto gains are always tax-free in Hong Kong. **Fact:** Gains on long-term investments are not chargeable to profits tax. [S:1afc18524517, para 46] Hong Kong-sourced profits from a crypto trading business are taxed. [S:1afc18524517, para 47]
- **Myth:** The fund tax concession for digital assets is already law. **Fact:** It is in a bill gazetted in June 2026. [S:fca785b80cff, paras 1, 4] In August 2026 LegCo was still scrutinising it. [S:103ce885d306, para 6]
