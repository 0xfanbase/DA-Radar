# F4 Timing and scenarios

This module sets out what Hong Kong's regulators and Government have scheduled up to 2029. After reading, you can brief a board on the official pipeline with a source for every date. You can explain why industry market-size forecasts disagree, without backing any of them. You can also test a plan against three scenarios for 2030–35, and name the signposts to watch for each.

## In 60 seconds

Most dated items in the official pipeline below fall in 2026. Fewer fall in 2027–29. The Government said it and the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) would put one bill to the Legislative Council (LegCo) in 2026. It would cover virtual asset (VA) dealing, custody, advice and management. [S:c6df52e680de, Reply (3), 'In addition' para] The start dates of those regimes are not yet set. [S:6186c01c6649, para 32; S:f41a543388bc, para 49; S:01c5c5c4045f, para 64] The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) plans central bank digital currency settlement and 24/7 operation for EnsembleTX (its tokenised-deposit pilot) by around end-2026. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 50]

Dates after 2026 are fewer, and about half of them are stated targets rather than law. The SFC plans to switch on market and anti-money laundering (AML) surveillance tools in 2027. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 51(iii)] A tax-reporting bill would bring in new crypto tax reporting from 1 January 2027, if LegCo passes it. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(3)-(4)]

Industry forecasts of market size differ widely; the forecasts box below shows the spread. The official sources reviewed give no market-size forecast. The three scenarios below have no probabilities, and none is treated as the expected one.

## Industry view

This section holds the site's only forecasts. Read the box before quoting any number.

> **How to read market-size forecasts**
>
> **Today's size depends on the definition.** Real-world assets (RWA) are traditional assets, such as bonds or funds, recorded as tokens. On 26 September 2026 the data firm RWA.xyz showed US$38.62 billion of freely transferable tokenised assets (global, reported). [I:1627a37255f8, Dashboard header, 'Distributed Asset Value'] It showed a separate US$358.07 billion of assets recorded on a ledger but not freely transferable as tokens (global, reported). [I:1627a37255f8, Dashboard header, 'Represented Asset Value'] Citi Institute, the research arm of the bank Citigroup, put the tokenised asset market at about US$17 billion in 2026 (global, reported). [I:fd490b9a4b90, p.6 summary] That figure uses April 2026 data from DefiLlama (another data firm). [I:fd490b9a4b90, p.19]
>
> **Forecasts for tokenised assets (global).**
> - McKinsey (a consultancy), June 2024: about US$2 trillion of tokenised market value by 2030 (forecast). [I:5f69773bc91f, pp.41-42] The International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) repeated this figure in a 2025 report. [I:5f69773bc91f, pp.41-42] McKinsey's range is US$1–4 trillion, and it leaves out cryptocurrencies and stablecoins. [I:5f69773bc91f, pp.41-42]
> - Citi Institute (a bank's research arm), June 2026: US$5.5 trillion of tokenised financial assets by 2030 in its central (base) case (forecast). [I:fd490b9a4b90, p.6 summary] Its low (bear) case is US$2.7 trillion and its high (bull) case US$8.2 trillion. [I:fd490b9a4b90, p.6 summary] Citi adds up five market segments, such as public bonds, public shares and private credit. For each segment, it assumes what share is tokenised. [I:fd490b9a4b90, p.21, Figure 5]
> - Ripple (a crypto firm) and Boston Consulting Group (BCG), a consultancy, April 2025: tokenised real-world assets grow from about US$0.6 trillion in 2025 to US$18.9 trillion by 2033 (forecast). [I:7f93581e44fe, PDF p.17] The study's other scenarios give US$12.5 trillion and US$23.4 trillion. [I:7f93581e44fe, PDF p.17] The Global Financial Markets Association (GFMA), a trade body, repeated these figures in 2025. [I:7f93581e44fe, PDF p.17]
> - Citi also lists other firms' 2030 forecasts. They run from US$1.0–4.0 trillion (McKinsey) to US$16.1 trillion (BCG with ADDX, a digital securities exchange). [I:fd490b9a4b90, p.20, Figure 4]
> - Deloitte (a consultancy), April 2025: US$4 trillion of tokenised real estate by 2035 (global forecast, real estate only). [I:c7585f525f1f, Intro section and Figure 1]
>
> **Forecasts for stablecoins (global).**
> - Citi Institute (a bank's research arm), September 2025: US$1.9 trillion of stablecoins outstanding by 2030 in its central case (forecast). [I:842f23c0f404, p.20] Its low case is US$0.9 trillion and its high case US$4.0 trillion. [I:842f23c0f404, p.20]
> - J.P. Morgan Global Research (a bank's research arm), 2025: a stablecoin market of US$500–750 billion in the coming years (forecast, no year given). [I:602adc61d62c, article summary]
> - The Bank for International Settlements (BIS), 2026, cites in a footnote some industry forecasts of US$2–4 trillion by 2030. It does not make its own forecast there. [I:efa37f6d77e9, footnote 41, p.113]
>
> **Why they differ.**
> - *Scope.* McKinsey's figure leaves out cryptocurrencies and stablecoins. [I:5f69773bc91f, pp.41-42] Citi's covers five segments. [I:fd490b9a4b90, p.21, Figure 5] Deloitte's covers real estate only. [I:c7585f525f1f, Intro section and Figure 1]
> - *Definition.* Counting only transferable tokens, or also assets recorded on a ledger, gives very different totals. [I:1627a37255f8, Dashboard header, 'Distributed Asset Value' and 'Represented Asset Value']
> - *Method.* Citi multiplies each market's size by an assumed tokenised share. [I:fd490b9a4b90, p.21, Figure 5] Ripple and BCG's figures imply growth of over 50% a year. [I:7f93581e44fe, PDF p.17] Deloitte's imply 27% a year. [I:c7585f525f1f, Intro section and Figure 1]
> - *Sponsor.* Citi's report quotes the head of digital assets in its own Citi Services unit. [I:fd490b9a4b90, p.23] Its disclosures say the firm may have interests different from the reader's. [I:fd490b9a4b90, p.55, Important disclosures] The GFMA figures come from a study co-written by Ripple, a crypto firm. [I:7f93581e44fe, PDF p.17] Deloitte's figure is part of its own yearly predictions series. [I:c7585f525f1f, Intro section]
>
> **What official bodies say about them.** The Financial Stability Board (FSB) said in 2024 that industry growth estimates vary widely and are poorly supported. [I:8e0d12827d4d, PDF p.13] IOSCO says estimates vary across studies. It also says its report does not aim to encourage or endorse tokenisation. [I:5f69773bc91f, p.41]
>
> **How to use this box.** A range from several sources tells you more than any single number. Each figure above names its publisher, year, scope and definition. This site does not endorse any forecast. (concept)

## How it works commercially

A scenario is a story about the future used to test a plan. It is not a prediction. (concept) Each scenario below is named after the main force that shapes it. Each gets the same structure and the same weight. None is a base case, and none carries a probability.

> **Analysis — not official**
> **Question:** Scenario 1, *Settlement moves onto ledgers*: what if tokenised deposits and central bank money become common for settlement by 2030–35?
> **How to think about it:**
> - Picture payments, fund trades and bond settlement running 24/7 on shared ledgers.
> - A bank's income would rely more on deposit balances, settlement fees and running infrastructure for others. (concept)
> - The skills in demand would be ledger operations, liquidity management around the clock and resilient systems. (concept)
> **What it depends on:** pilot results turning into live services; links between platforms; clients' treasury needs; capital treatment of tokenised assets.
> **Official signposts:** EnsembleTX's move to 24/7 and central bank money settlement [S:ef3648ff1610, para 50]; the CMU OmniClear digital asset platform [S:654fa5eba8cf, para 114]; tokenised Exchange Fund Bill tests [S:ef3648ff1610, para 35(ii)].
> **What this is not:** Not a forecast, not advice, and not the regulator's view.

> **Analysis — not official**
> **Question:** Scenario 2, *Investor products grow through licensed firms*: what if client demand for VA and tokenised products grows by 2030–35, with settlement mostly traditional?
> **How to think about it:**
> - Picture more clients buying VA funds, tokenised funds and stablecoins through licensed firms.
> - A bank's income would rely more on distribution, dealing, custody and financing fees. (concept)
> - The skills in demand would be suitability controls (checking a product fits the client), custody security and partner due diligence. (concept)
> **What it depends on:** start dates of the new VA regimes; the product range regulators allow; client interest; fee pressure among providers.
> **Official signposts:** the combined VA bill [S:c6df52e680de, Reply (3), 'In addition' para]; tokenised gold and other real-world asset products on licensed platforms [S:ef3648ff1610, para 49(ii)]; stablecoin trading on licensed platforms [S:ef3648ff1610, para 49(iii)].
> **What this is not:** Not a forecast, not advice, and not the regulator's view.

> **Analysis — not official**
> **Question:** Scenario 3, *Demand stays narrow*: what if client use stays small and concentrated in a few services by 2030–35?
> **How to think about it:**
> - Picture a few specialist firms serving most clients, with banks as suppliers of accounts, reserves and custody.
> - A bank's income would rely more on serving other firms, such as holding stablecoin reserves. (concept)
> - The skills in demand would be cost control, outsourcing oversight and clear decisions on when to scale down. (concept)
> **What it depends on:** client demand; capital costs; incidents or fraud that reduce trust; how many licences regulators grant.
> **Official signposts:** the small number of stablecoin licences [S:3f0fcc104e61, Reply (2)]; how the cryptoasset capital rules in force since 1 January 2026 affect costs [S:100b982aadca, p.90]; the number of licensed trading platforms [S:b14be2203d39, Licensed platforms table].
> **What this is not:** Not a forecast, not advice, and not the regulator's view.

> **Analysis — not official**
> **Question:** Which capabilities appear in all three scenarios, and which official rule or plan does each connect to?
> **How to think about it:**
> - Secure key custody. Link: the HKMA sets expected standards for banks that hold client digital assets [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.1; Implementation, p.2].
> - Monitoring blockchain transactions for money laundering. Link: the SFC plans its own AML surveillance tools for 2027 [S:ef3648ff1610, para 51(iii)].
> - Strong client login and device controls. Link: the SFC expects them of internet brokers and SFC-licensed VA trading platforms [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 5, 21(c)].
> - A board-level plan for digital change. Link: the HKMA expects each bank's board to endorse one [S:3ec5b269b5dc, letter body, p.2].
> - A way to track rule changes by date, such as the pipeline table above. (concept)
> **What it depends on:** the bank's size and client mix; which lines it runs; its partners; its budget.
> **Official signposts:** the combined VA bill [S:c6df52e680de, Reply (3), 'In addition' para]; the CrypTech timetable [S:ef3648ff1610, para 51(iii)]; the phishing-resistant login deadline [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 5, 21(c)].
> **What this is not:** Not a forecast, not advice, and not the regulator's view.

## Common mix-ups

- **Myth:** A Policy Address target is a legal deadline. **Fact:** Most items in the pipeline are stated targets. Only law or a regulator's requirement binds firms. (concept)
- **Myth:** The new dealing regime starts when the bill passes. **Fact:** The Government and the SFC will first consider when the regime should start. [S:6186c01c6649, para 32]
- **Myth:** Industry forecasts agree on the market size. **Fact:** Published forecasts differ widely. The forecasts box in Industry view shows the spread and the reasons. (concept)
- **Myth:** One scenario here is the expected one. **Fact:** All three get equal weight. They are tools for testing plans. (concept)

   **Answer:** By 9 September 2026, within six months of the HKMA's 9 March 2026 letter. [S:3ec5b269b5dc, letter body, p.2] Whether boards endorsed their plans on time is not public.
3. Is a Policy Address date for a digital-asset project a binding legal deadline?
   **Answer:** No. Most pipeline dates are stated targets, a Government or regulator aim. Only law or a regulator's requirement binds firms. (concept)
4. Is one of the three scenarios in this module the expected or base case?
