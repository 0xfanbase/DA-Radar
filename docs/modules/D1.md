# D1 Capital and prudential treatment of cryptoassets

This module is for the bank's Head of Compliance (digital assets). After it, you can explain three things. First, how Hong Kong turned the global Basel standard on cryptoassets into binding rules on capital, liquidity and exposure limits. Second, how a cryptoasset gets sorted into a group. Third, what the bank must tell the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA).

## In 60 seconds

The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) sets global standards for banks. It published a standard on banks' cryptoasset exposures in December 2022. It amended that standard in July 2024. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 1.1.1] The HKMA consulted on the standard in February 2024, in consultation paper (CP) 24.01. [S:27f8bc2f6b2a, paras 1-2] In January 2025, the HKMA sent draft rules to the banking industry associations. [S:ebc5f9b5ee0d, p.1] In July 2025, the government published three sets of amendment rules in the Gazette (the government's official record). [S:277820235891, para 1] These rules took effect on 1 January 2026. [S:277820235891, closing para] On the same date, the HKMA brought in a new guidance module, CRP-1 (Classification of Cryptoassets). It also revised nine modules of its Supervisory Policy Manual (SPM), the HKMA's set of guidance modules for banks. [S:30b686db29f9, pp.1-2] Every cryptoasset a bank is exposed to falls into one of four groups. [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 1.3.2-1.3.3, 2.1.8] Group 1a is tokenised traditional assets, such as bonds recorded as digital tokens. [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 1.3.2-1.3.3, 2.1.8] Group 1b is stablecoins (tokens built to hold a steady value) with an effective stabilisation mechanism. [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 1.3.2-1.3.3, 2.1.8] Group 2a is Group 2 assets that meet hedging criteria, so limited hedging (offsetting one position with another) is recognised. [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 1.3.2-1.3.3, 2.1.8] Group 2b is everything else, at a 1,250% risk weight. [S:9e8d84c87168, Part XII CAE1 explanatory note, column (c), pp.214-215] A risk weight scales how much capital a bank holds against an asset. At 1,250%, the bank in effect holds capital equal to the whole exposure. A bank must tell the HKMA before acquiring each new type of cryptoasset. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.2] The asset stays in Group 2b until the HKMA agrees with the bank's assessment. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.2] In June 2026, the HKMA clarified one Group 1 condition. It covers tokenised traditional assets on permissionless blockchains (open networks anyone can join). A bank can also meet that condition another way. [S:dca53ff0a62c, p.1]

## Key facts

- CP 24.01 proposed bringing the December 2022 Basel standard into Hong Kong rules no earlier than 1 July 2025. Comments were due by 6 May 2024. [S:27f8bc2f6b2a, paras 2, 4, 11]
- In January 2025, the HKMA sent draft rule amendments to the industry. It asked for comments by 20 February 2025. It aimed for a 1 January 2026 start, in line with the Basel timetable. [S:ebc5f9b5ee0d, p.1]
- The government published the three amendment rules in the Gazette on 11 July 2025. They were due to be laid before the Legislative Council on 16 July 2025. They took effect on 1 January 2026. [S:277820235891, paras 1-2 and closing para]
- On 27 November 2025, the HKMA finalised CRP-1. It also finalised changes to nine other modules and a code of practice. All took effect on 1 January 2026. [S:30b686db29f9, pp.1-2]
- CRP-1 is guidance. The binding classification rules sit in Part 12 of the Banking (Capital) Rules (BCR). If the two differ, the BCR prevails. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 1.1.4]
- A cryptoasset reaches Group 1 only if it meets all four classification conditions. [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 2.2.1, 2.3.1, 2.4.1, 2.5.1] Condition 1: the token carries the same rights and risks as the traditional asset, or has an effective stabilisation mechanism. [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 2.2.1, 2.3.1, 2.4.1, 2.5.1] Condition 2: its rights are clearly defined and legally enforceable, and settlement is final. [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 2.2.1, 2.3.1, 2.4.1, 2.5.1] Condition 3: its network risks are sufficiently reduced and managed. [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 2.2.1, 2.3.1, 2.4.1, 2.5.1] Condition 4: the entities doing key functions are regulated and supervised, or follow suitable risk management standards. [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 2.2.1, 2.3.1, 2.4.1, 2.5.1]
- Group 1a is tokenised traditional assets. Group 1b is stablecoins with an effective stabilisation mechanism. Anything else is Group 2. [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 1.3.1-1.3.2]
- A Group 2 asset reaches Group 2a only if it meets three hedging criteria. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 3.1.1] One is an average market capitalisation (total market value) of at least HK$78 billion. Another is 100 or more real price observations. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 3.1.1]
- Group 2b exposures take a 1,250% risk weight. [S:9e8d84c87168, Part XII CAE1 explanatory note, column (c), pp.214-215] This weight is also meant to cover their market risk (loss from price moves). [S:5d845665ba83, para 4.3.2] It also covers their credit valuation adjustment risk (the risk that a derivative loses value as the other party's credit weakens). [S:5d845665ba83, para 4.3.2]
- Before acquiring a new type of cryptoasset, a bank must notify the HKMA. The bank then treats the asset as Group 2b until the HKMA agrees with the bank's own classification. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.2]
- Exposures held before 1 January 2026 must sit in Group 2b until the HKMA agrees the bank's assessment. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 1.4.2]
- If the HKMA disagrees with a bank's classification, the HKMA will override it. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.6]
- Stablecoins issued under the HKMA's licensing regime can be treated as meeting the extra safeguards for permissionless blockchains. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.6.3] Other stablecoins on those blockchains must be under a regime the HKMA considers comparable to the Stablecoins Ordinance. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.6.3]
- The June 2026 letter covers the approval condition for tokenised traditional assets on permissionless blockchains. [S:dca53ff0a62c, p.1] That condition calls for approval of the tokenised instrument by a financial market regulator. [S:dca53ff0a62c, p.1] A bank can also meet it where a financial market regulator supervises the tokenisation business. The letter took effect immediately. [S:dca53ff0a62c, p.1]
- CA-G-1 section 8.7 covers the banking book (positions a bank holds rather than trades). [S:5d0c13e09312, paras 8.7.1-8.7.2] Group 1a exposures there generally follow the rules for the underlying asset. [S:5d0c13e09312, paras 8.7.1-8.7.2] For Group 1b, the bank calculates capital separately for the reference asset and for the redeemer (the party that pays holders back). [S:5d0c13e09312, paras 8.7.1-8.7.2]
- Group 2 exposure limit: this applies to a systemically important bank. That is a global or domestic bank whose failure could harm the wider system. [S:5d0c13e09312, para 8.7.4(d)] Such a bank faces a capital add-on (extra capital charge) if its Group 2 exposure exceeds 1% of Tier 1 capital. [S:5d0c13e09312, para 8.7.4(d)] Tier 1 capital is a bank's core capital, such as ordinary shares and retained earnings. Above 2%, a different and heavier treatment applies. [S:9e8d84c87168, Part XII CAE1 explanatory note, rows 8-10, p.215]
- LM-1 Annex 3 covers liquidity. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3 paras 1, 7] High-quality liquid assets (HQLA) are assets a bank can turn into cash quickly in a crisis. The liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) checks that a bank holds enough HQLA to cover 30 days of stressed cash outflows. A category 1 institution is a bank the HKMA has designated to follow the LCR. Such an institution may count a cryptoasset as HQLA only if it passes two tests. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3 paras 1, 7] It is a Group 1a token of an asset class that qualifies as HQLA, and it meets the HQLA requirements. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3 paras 1, 7] The issuer of a stablecoin that holders can redeem within the LCR period must generally apply a 100% outflow. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3 paras 1, 7] In other words, it assumes all such stablecoins are paid out.
- Market risk rules change in three ways. [S:b8d2d2a0f9c8, footnote 80, paras 6.1.9, 6.2.8] Any cryptoasset exposure removes the small-amount exception from market risk capital rules. [S:b8d2d2a0f9c8, footnote 80, paras 6.1.9, 6.2.8] The internal models approach (a bank's own approved risk models) cannot be used for instruments referencing Group 2 cryptoassets. [S:b8d2d2a0f9c8, footnote 80, paras 6.1.9, 6.2.8] For Group 2a, position risk (capital for price risk on a holding) is 100% of the net position. [S:b8d2d2a0f9c8, footnote 80, paras 6.1.9, 6.2.8]
- CR-G-8 values crypto exposures under new Division 5A of the Banking (Exposure Limits) Rules. [S:0cd3c1a74e5e, paras 2.8.8-2.8.9 and footnote 13] Some exposures are not linked to a counterparty's credit or default risk. The bank leaves those out when measuring large exposures (big concentrations of risk) to that counterparty or its group. [S:0cd3c1a74e5e, paras 2.8.8-2.8.9 and footnote 13]
- CR-L-5 covers the rules on HKMA consent for major acquisitions. Under it, a token that carries the economics of equity (the returns and risks of owning shares) counts as share capital. [S:13365a31e794, footnote 1]
- CA-G-5 says the HKMA may respond to crypto-related weaknesses in several ways. These include capital add-ons, provisions (money set aside for losses) and limits. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 3.6.6]

## What your bank must do

**Obligations**
- [ ] Classify every cryptoasset exposure under BCR Part 12 and CRP-1. Keep reassessing it on an ongoing basis. [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 1.1.4, 2.1.1]
- [ ] Move an asset to Group 2b at once if it stops meeting its conditions or criteria. This covers any asset the HKMA agreed as Group 1a, 1b or 2a. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.3]
- [ ] As a registered institution (RI), treat loans secured only by virtual asset collateral as clean (unsecured) loans for capital purposes. [S:e64b7b0267b2, para (9), p.5]

**Controls & monitoring**
- [ ] Keep policies, staff and technology systems able to assess cryptoasset risk. Document the evidence behind each classification. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.1]
- [ ] For stablecoins, keep a monitoring framework that checks the stabilisation mechanism works as intended. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.2.4]
- [ ] If the bank is systemically important, track its Group 2 exposure ratio. Compare it with the 1% and 2% thresholds used in disclosure. [S:9e8d84c87168, Part XII CAE1 explanatory note, rows 8-10, p.215]

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- [ ] Notify the HKMA before acquiring any new type of cryptoasset. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.2]
- [ ] Give the HKMA classification evidence when it asks. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.1]

**Counterparty due diligence**
- [ ] For Group 1 assets, check each entity with a key role. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.5.1] These roles are redemption, transfer, storage or settlement of the asset, and management of its reserves. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.5.1] Confirm each entity is regulated and supervised, or meets suitable risk standards. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.5.1] Confirm it also discloses a governance framework (its rules for oversight and decisions). [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.5.1]

## Talking points

**To the CCO**
- The capital rules are law from 1 January 2026. CRP-1 is guidance, and the BCR wins if they differ. [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 1.1.4, 1.4.1]
- We need to tell the HKMA before we acquire any new type of cryptoasset. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.2]

**To business heads**
- A new cryptoasset sits in Group 2b until the HKMA agrees its classification. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.2] That means a 1,250% risk weight, so capital equal to the whole exposure. [S:5d845665ba83, para 4.3.2]
- Stablecoins under the HKMA licensing regime can be treated as meeting the permissionless-blockchain safeguards. The four classification conditions still apply. [S:b7d630146e6c, paras 1.3.1, 2.6.3]

**To the CEO**
- Hong Kong's rules started on 1 January 2026, matching the Basel timetable. [S:277820235891, closing para]
- The HKMA can override our classifications. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 2.1.6] It can also add capital or limits if it sees weaknesses. [S:349a543ae2f0, para 3.6.6]

**To Risk / CRO**
- Any crypto exposure ends our small-amount exception from the market risk capital rules. [S:b8d2d2a0f9c8, footnote 80]
- For a category 1 institution, a stablecoin it holds is generally not HQLA. It counts as a 0% inflow under the LCR. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3 para 7] The net stable funding ratio tests that long-term assets have stable funding. Under it, the stablecoin has an 85% required stable funding factor. [S:69600121310a, Annex 3 para 7]

## Common mix-ups

- **Myth:** The June 2026 CRP-1 letter tightened the rules. **Fact:** The letter clarified the approval condition in para 2.6.4. A bank can also meet it where a financial market regulator supervises the tokenisation business. [S:dca53ff0a62c, p.1]
- **Myth:** Internal models are barred only for Group 2a. **Fact:** Market risk module MR-1 bars them for all instruments referencing Group 2 cryptoassets. [S:b8d2d2a0f9c8, para 6.1.9]
- **Myth:** The 1%/2% Group 2 limit applies to every bank. **Fact:** Only systemically important banks face the add-on. [S:5d0c13e09312, para 8.7.4(d)] The HKMA's instructions for its regulatory return (a report banks file) have all Hong Kong-incorporated AIs report the ratio. [S:d1fbf6c0736a, paras 11-12]
- **Myth:** Legacy holdings keep their old treatment. **Fact:** Holdings from before 1 January 2026 sit in Group 2b until the HKMA agrees the assessment. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 1.4.2]
- **Myth:** Central bank digital currencies are cryptoassets. **Fact:** They sit outside the standard. They are generally treated like the central bank's ordinary currency. [S:b7d630146e6c, para 1.2.3 and footnote 4]

## Open items

- The April 2026 BCR proposals closed for comment on 1 June 2026. [S:f6b5ade120fe, landing page] One of these proposals is to remove cryptoassets from the Part 6 "cash item" definition. [S:1e9b351e81e7, Items 19, 45] Another is a set of new rules on hedging counterparty credit risk (the risk the other side of a deal defaults). [S:1e9b351e81e7, Items 19, 45] Those rules cover netting sets (groups of trades that can be offset) holding Group 2b cryptoasset derivatives. [S:1e9b351e81e7, Items 19, 45] The documents reviewed for this guide hold no final rules. [S:f6b5ade120fe, landing page]
- The June 2026 letter says CRP-1's wording will reflect the clarification in due course. The documents reviewed hold no updated CRP-1 text. [S:dca53ff0a62c, p.1]
- The documents reviewed for this guide do not include the Gazette text of Banking (Capital) Rules Part 12. Section references come from HKMA modules and templates.
- For two sets of HKMA letters, only catalogue pages were reviewed. One is the December 2022 letter passing on the BCBS standard. The others are the July and August 2025 letters on the Gazette publication.
