# C3 Custody of digital assets and key management

This module is for the Head of Compliance at a bank that holds, or plans to hold, clients' crypto or tokenised assets. After reading it, you can explain what the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) expects in its May 2026 custody standards. You can also explain what changed from 2024 and how staking fits in. Finally, you can explain how rules from the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) and for stablecoins set the benchmark for counterparties.

## In 60 seconds

Custody means safekeeping assets for clients. In virtual asset (VA) custody, the core risk sits with whoever safekeeps the private keys, or similar tools, that can move the assets [S:f41a543388bc, para 7]. A private key is the secret code that authorises a transfer. So much of the rulebook is about key management [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10–11]. The HKMA set custody standards for banks, which it calls authorized institutions (AIs), in February 2024 [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2]. On 27 May 2026 it replaced them with an updated annex (a set of detailed standards) of 26 paragraphs [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2; S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 1–26]. The new annex keeps the rule that 98% of client virtual assets (VAs) sit in cold storage, meaning their keys are kept offline [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b), (l) and fn 8]. It also keeps the rule that keys stay in Hong Kong [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b), (l) and fn 8]. It adds detailed controls on how wallets, the tools that hold keys, are run [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10–11, fn 7, para 14(iii); S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8]. It drops the fixed insurance percentages [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10–11, fn 7, para 14(iii); S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8]. With HKMA consent, a bank can now appoint a licensed stablecoin issuer to hold that issuer's own coins [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10–11, fn 7, para 14(iii); S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8]. A stablecoin is a token designed to keep a steady value against a currency. The SFC sets custody rules for licensed crypto exchanges, known as virtual asset trading platforms (VATPs) [S:f1bbafccd556, Part X]. These rules are in Part X of the SFC's guidelines for VATP operators, called the VATP Guidelines [S:f1bbafccd556, Part X]. The SFC says the standards in its August 2025 custody circular will also be core expectations for future VA custodians [S:562c0f8addce, p.2]. A circular is a formal notice to regulated firms. In December 2025, the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau (FSTB) and the SFC published consultation conclusions on licensing custodians [S:f41a543388bc, Introduction, p.3]. Consultation conclusions are the final response to public comments. The conclusions stated an aim to put a bill, or draft law, before the Legislative Council (LegCo) in 2026 [S:f41a543388bc, Introduction, p.3].

> **Commercial and customer benefits**
> **For customers:**
> - The HKMA says its May 2026 guidance aims to keep client digital assets safe. [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.1]
> - The HKMA expects banks to keep 98% of client VAs in cold storage (keys kept offline). [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b) and fn 8]
> - The HKMA expects a bank to be liable to clients for losses from incidents attributable to it. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]
> **For the bank:**
> - The HKMA says demand for digital-asset custody is growing. [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.1]
> - The updated standards let banks match arrangements to each asset's nature and risks. [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.1]
> - Under the proposed licensing regime, banks already assessed for VA custody would get faster approval. [S:f41a543388bc, para 51]
> - Holding client assets can lead to other income, such as dealing and lending. (concept)
> **Evidence so far:**
> - In its 2025 annual report, OSL Group Limited (OSL) reported custodian income of about HK$16.1 million for 2025, against about HK$9.6 million in 2024. [I:e2df03a8c8a6, PDF p.126, Note 5] These are group figures, covering Hong Kong and global business. [I:e2df03a8c8a6, PDF p.126, Note 5]
> - China Asset Management (Hong Kong) Limited's April 2026 key facts for its Hong Kong-listed ether exchange-traded fund set a custodian and administrator fee of up to 1% of net asset value a year. [I:ee50e4efff6f, PDF p.11]
> **Limits:**
> - The HKMA also expects the bank to hold enough resources, possibly including insurance, to cover such losses. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]
> - Under the proposals, banks safekeeping client VA keys would need a new licence or registration, with no interim status. [S:f41a543388bc, paras 35(b), 48]
> **Business lines:** [Virtual-asset custody](#b-custody); [Staking services](#b-staking)

## Key facts

- The 2026 annex covers client digital assets held by AIs [S:ce05e67bcdce, preamble and fn 1–4]. It also covers those held by subsidiaries of locally incorporated AIs (AIs set up as companies in Hong Kong) [S:ce05e67bcdce, preamble and fn 1–4]. Digital assets here include VAs, tokenised securities and other tokenised assets [S:ce05e67bcdce, preamble and fn 1–4]. Tokenised means ownership is recorded on a blockchain or similar ledger. Two groups fall outside the annex: limited purpose digital tokens, and assets the bank or its group holds for itself [S:ce05e67bcdce, preamble and fn 1–4].
- The 2026 letter replaces the February 2024 circular [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2]. Banks already offering custody should review and revise their systems and controls to meet the latest standards [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2]. The 2024 circular set a six-month deadline to notify the HKMA and confirm compliance [S:6bccca749ac2, Implementation, p.2]. The 2026 letter sets no such deadline [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2].
- For client VAs, the annex's key controls are generally required [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11 chapeau]. For other digital assets, the bank may take a risk-based approach, matching controls to the risk [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11 chapeau]. Tokens on public permissionless chains, which anyone can join, call for extra caution [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11 chapeau].
- The rule to keep 98% of client VAs in cold storage has not changed [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(l) and fn 8; S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 6].
- **Compensation changed.** The 2024 annex asked banks for insurance or compensation cover [S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8]. The cover was 50% of client VAs in cold storage and 100% in hot and other storage [S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8]. The 2026 annex drops these figures [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]. Instead, the HKMA expects the bank to be liable for losses from incidents attributable to the bank [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]. Adequate financial resources, which may include insurance, back that liability [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]. The 50%/100% figures still apply to VATPs under an SFC rule [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.22]. For VATPs, the cover takes the form of a compensation arrangement the SFC approves [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.22].
- **Key location.** Seeds and private keys should still be generated, stored and backed up in Hong Kong [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b) and fn 7; para 14(iii)]. A seed is the master secret from which keys are made. There is one new exception [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b) and fn 7; para 14(iii)]. With HKMA consent, a bank may appoint an HKMA-licensed stablecoin issuer to hold that issuer's own coins [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b) and fn 7; para 14(iii)]. The issuer's keys can then be in Hong Kong or in another location the HKMA accepts [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b) and fn 7; para 14(iii)].
- **New operating controls.** The annex adds cold-wallet whitelists, lists of approved addresses, with a system check on every transfer [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 10]. It also adds detection of intrusions into vaults, signing devices and code repositories, where software code is stored [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 10]. The annex also sets out these controls [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a), (c), (f), (g), (i)]:
  - due diligence on hardware security module (HSM) vendors, who supply secure devices that store keys;
  - no smart contracts, or self-running blockchain programs, in cold wallets on public chains;
  - segregation of duties over wallet code, so no one person controls it alone;
  - dedicated devices for approving transactions;
  - integrity checks on critical transaction data, using devices kept off networks (air-gapped);
  - measures to prevent blind signing.
- Security monitoring should run 24/7 [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 23].
- **Anti-money laundering section removed.** The 2024 annex had a separate paragraph on anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-financing of terrorism (CFT), or AML/CFT [S:86e064b2a8d0, para 23]. The 2026 annex has none. The cover letter reminds AIs that every other law and rule that applies to them still applies [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.2].
- Transfers to third parties are now allowed if effective controls are in place [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(m) and fn 9]. They can support payment services or carry out client settlement instructions [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(m) and fn 9]. The definition of client now also covers clients of SFC-licensed VATPs [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(m) and fn 9].
- For staking from custody, the annex points to the HKMA's April 2025 staking guidance [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 26]. Staking means locking up tokens to help run a blockchain in return for rewards. The annex asks banks to have due regard to the SFC's staking terms for VATPs [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 26]. Under the HKMA guidance, the bank should keep control of everything needed to unstake, or withdraw staked tokens [S:4641e6c404d2, sections A–B, Implementation p.3]. It should disclose staking risks such as slashing, a penalty that takes away staked tokens [S:4641e6c404d2, sections A–B, Implementation p.3]. It should also discuss its plans with the HKMA before launch [S:4641e6c404d2, sections A–B, Implementation p.3]. VATPs may not use third-party custody for staked assets [S:50bf95ba3143, para 6].
- The SFC's August 2025 circular took effect immediately [S:562c0f8addce, pp.1–2, Way forward p.7]. It describes security incidents at overseas platforms. The SFC says these incidents point to potential weaknesses in any custody solution [S:562c0f8addce, pp.1–2, Way forward p.7]. That includes an HSM, multi-party computation (MPC) or multi-signature set-up [S:562c0f8addce, pp.1–2, Way forward p.7]. Its standards will also be core expectations for future VA custodians [S:562c0f8addce, pp.1–2, Way forward p.7].
- Under the custodian regime conclusions, the licence will cover custodians that safekeep private keys or similar tools that let VAs be transferred [S:f41a543388bc, paras 6–8, 12]. Custodians of tokenised securities that do not hold VAs fall outside the regime [S:f41a543388bc, paras 6–8, 12].
- The SFC will set a baseline of HK$10 million paid-up share capital, the money owners have put in [S:f41a543388bc, paras 41–42]. It will also set HK$3 million liquid capital, meaning assets that can quickly be turned into cash [S:f41a543388bc, paras 41–42]. Banks remain subject to HKMA capital requirements [S:f41a543388bc, paras 41–42].
- The FSTB and SFC do not plan to let existing providers carry on while they apply for a licence [S:f41a543388bc, paras 48, 51]. But banks already assessed by the HKMA and already providing VA custody get a faster licensing process [S:f41a543388bc, paras 48, 51].
- The consultation paper proposed that the SFC license and register VA custodians and set standards [S:30b60438f017, para 2.12(a)]. It also proposed that the HKMA act as frontline regulator, supervising banks' VA custodian activities [S:30b60438f017, para 2.12(a)]. The conclusions list banks that safekeep keys among those needing a licence or registration [S:f41a543388bc, para 35(b)]. The list also covers subsidiaries of locally incorporated banks that safekeep keys [S:f41a543388bc, para 35(b)].

**Key-management terms, as the documents use them**

- **Hot and cold wallets.** In a hot wallet the private keys sit online, which makes them easier to attack. In a cold wallet they are kept offline [S:8a016a912528, fn 19].
- **HSM (hardware security module).** A secure, tamper-resistant device that creates and stores seeds and keys. Banks should check that the vendor keeps up patching and re-certification [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a)].
- **Key sharding and multi-signature.** Sharding splits a key among several authorised staff, so no one person holds all of it [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(i)]. A set number of shard-holders must act together to sign [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(i)]. Multi-signature uses several keys to approve one transaction. For stablecoin issuers, those keys should be stored in separate, isolated environments [S:240be6833157, para 6.5.7(iv)].
- **MPC (multi-party computation).** Another way to spread signing power across parties. For licensing, a key question is whether the provider can move client assets on its own [S:f41a543388bc, para 11].
- **Blind signing.** Approving a transaction without reviewing its contents. Banks should show signers every detail in human-readable form before they sign [S:562c0f8addce, fn 6; S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(i)].
- **Whitelisting.** Deposits and withdrawals go only to approved addresses shown to belong to the client [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10, 11(m)]. The bank can check this, for example, by message signing or by a micropayment test (a tiny trial transfer) [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10, 11(m)]. The third-party transfer exception still applies [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10, 11(m)]. Changes to a cold-wallet whitelist are tightly controlled [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10, 11(m)].
- **Air-gapped devices.** Devices kept off networks, stored in a cold vault and changed only with physical access. Banks should use them to check that critical transaction data is intact [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(g)]. Stablecoin issuers should generate, and use, their most significant keys in air-gapped environments [S:240be6833157, para 6.5.7(ii), (vii)].

## What your bank must do

**Obligations**
- Keep client assets and wallet addresses separate from the bank's own [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 6–7 and fn 6].
- Do not lend, pledge or otherwise use client assets as security [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 6–7 and fn 6]. Three exceptions apply: settling transactions or fees the client owes the bank, explicit written client consent given in advance, or where the law requires it [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 6–7 and fn 6].
- Be liable for losses attributable to the bank, and hold adequate financial resources to cover them [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)].
- Disclose the custody arrangements to clients fully and fairly [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 20]. Cover rights on insolvency, segregation, insurance or compensation, and commingling, which means mixing assets together [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 20]. Also explain how forks and airdrops are handled [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 20]. A fork splits a blockchain in two; an airdrop is a free token handout.

**Controls & monitoring**
- Keep 98% of client VAs in cold storage, and keep seeds, keys and backups in Hong Kong [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b), (l) and fn 8].
- Train transaction signers in depth and run drills of the business continuity plan [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 3, 5].
- Reconcile, or match, each client's holdings regularly against both on-chain records (on the blockchain) and off-chain records (kept outside it) [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 21]. Escalate any discrepancies to senior management [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 21].
- Monitor security 24/7 [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 23–24]. Cover outside dependencies such as vendors, blockchain protocols, encryption methods and shared code libraries [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 23–24].

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- Discuss plans with the HKMA before launching custody, and show that the bank meets the standards [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2].
- Discuss staking plans with the HKMA before launch [S:4641e6c404d2, Implementation, p.3].
- Provide custody records to the HKMA promptly when asked [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 22].

**Counterparty due diligence**
- As a general principle, delegate VA custody only to one of three types of firm [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 14]:
  - another AI, or a subsidiary of an AI that is itself incorporated in Hong Kong;
  - an SFC-licensed VATP;
  - a licensed stablecoin issuer for the coins it issues, with HKMA consent.
- Before taking on a custodian, get independent reviews of its code [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 15]. Also understand how it builds and releases software [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 15].
- After taking on a custodian, check its security controls regularly [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 15]. Require it to report incidents promptly, and test its disaster recovery [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 15].
- For a VATP counterparty, the SFC's own custody standards are in the VATP Guidelines [S:f1bbafccd556, paras 10.6–10.8, 10.22; S:562c0f8addce, pp.1–2]. Paras 10.6–10.8 cover cold storage, approved addresses and key management [S:f1bbafccd556, paras 10.6–10.8, 10.22]. Para 10.22 covers the compensation arrangement [S:f1bbafccd556, paras 10.6–10.8, 10.22]. The SFC circular 25EC44 adds detail to these [S:562c0f8addce, pp.1–2]. The HKMA annex points to the VATP Guidelines for reference on delegation [S:ce05e67bcdce, fn 10].

## Talking points

**To the CCO (chief compliance officer)**
- The 2026 annex replaced the 2024 one. Existing services should review and revise their systems and controls to meet the latest standards. The letter sets no transition period [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2].
- The custody annex no longer has an AML/CFT section [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.2]. But the cover letter reminds AIs to comply with all applicable requirements [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.2]. The HKMA's AML/CFT Guideline sets the standards AIs should meet [S:7bfdad704174, para 1.3].

**To business heads**
- With HKMA consent, a bank may now delegate custody of a licensed stablecoin to its issuer [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 14(iii)].
- Transfers to third parties for payments and settlement are now allowed if effective controls are in place [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(m)].

**To the CEO**
- Fixed insurance percentages are gone [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n); S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8]. In their place, the bank is liable for losses attributable to it, backed by adequate financial resources [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n); S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8].
- The FSTB and SFC conclusions propose a custodian licence set by law [S:f41a543388bc, paras 48, 51]. The conclusions give existing providers no right to carry on while they apply. Banks the HKMA has already assessed get a faster process [S:f41a543388bc, paras 48, 51].

**To Risk / CRO (chief risk officer)**
- The SFC says weaknesses shown by overseas cybersecurity incidents can arise whichever custody solution is used: HSM, MPC or multi-signature [S:562c0f8addce, p.1].
- The HKMA lists slashing, lock-up periods and validator inactivity among staking risks to disclose [S:4641e6c404d2, sections A–B]. A validator is a node that checks blockchain transactions. The bank should keep control of the means to unstake [S:4641e6c404d2, sections A–B].

## Common mix-ups

- "Banks need 50%/100% insurance cover." That was the 2024 rule for banks [S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8]. It remains the rule for VATPs [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.22]. It is not the rule for banks from 2026 [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)].
- "The February 2024 annex is still current." It was superseded on 27 May 2026 [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2].
- "Keys may never leave Hong Kong." The general rule stands [S:ce05e67bcdce, fn 7]. But an issuer appointed to hold its own coins may keep keys elsewhere if the HKMA accepts the location [S:ce05e67bcdce, fn 7].
- "The custodian regime covers tokenised securities." It does not, because the legal definition of VA excludes securities [S:f41a543388bc, para 12].
- "MPC or HSM equals compliance." The SFC says weaknesses can exist in any custody solution [S:562c0f8addce, p.1].

## Open items

- The FSTB and SFC stated an aim to put the custodian bill before LegCo in 2026 [S:f41a543388bc, Introduction, p.3]. The documents reviewed do not show that it has been introduced. No start date for the regime is set [S:f41a543388bc, para 49].
- The SFC said it would consult on custody for private funds, in a separate paper issued the same day [S:f41a543388bc, para 32]. The question is whether firms providing VA management services must use SFC-regulated custodians for private funds' VAs [S:f41a543388bc, para 32].
- The 2024 annex referred to AML/CFT guidance from the HKMA on custody of digital assets [S:86e064b2a8d0, para 23]. The documents reviewed do not show whether such separate guidance exists.

## Check yourself

1. Which regulator sets the custody standards for a bank that safekeeps clients' VAs?
   **Answer:** The HKMA. It set custody standards for authorized institutions (AIs) in February 2024 and replaced them with an updated annex on 27 May 2026 [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2].
2. What share of client virtual assets does the HKMA's 2026 annex expect a bank to keep in cold storage?
   **Answer:** 98% of client VAs, with their keys kept offline [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b), (l) and fn 8].
3. Is the February 2024 custody circular still in force?
   **Answer:** No, it is superseded. The HKMA replaced it with an updated annex on 27 May 2026 [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2].
4. What does the HKMA expect a bank to do before launching digital-asset custody services?
   **Answer:** The bank should discuss its plans with the HKMA first, and satisfy the HKMA that it meets the standards [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2].
5. Do the old 50%/100% insurance percentages still apply to banks' custody of client VAs?
   **Answer:** No. The 2026 annex drops them. The bank is instead liable for losses from incidents attributable to it, backed by adequate financial resources [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]. The 50%/100% figures still apply to VATPs [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.22].
