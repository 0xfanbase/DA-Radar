# C3 Custody of digital assets and key management

For the Head of Compliance at a bank that holds, or plans to hold, clients' crypto or tokenised assets: after this module you can explain what the HKMA's May 2026 custody standards require, what changed from 2024, how staking fits in, and how the SFC and stablecoin rules set the benchmark for counterparties.

## In 60 seconds

Holding a crypto asset really means controlling the private key that can move it. Custody regulation is therefore mostly about key management. The HKMA set custody standards for banks in February 2024 and replaced them on 27 May 2026 with a longer annex of 26 paragraphs. The new annex keeps the 98% cold-storage rule and the rule that keys stay in Hong Kong. It adds detailed controls on how wallets are operated, drops the fixed insurance percentages, and lets a licensed stablecoin issuer act as custodian of its own coin. The SFC applies parallel and, in places, stricter rules to licensed trading platforms (VATPs). Its August 2025 custody circular is the benchmark it says the future custodian licensing regime will use. That regime reached consultation conclusions in December 2025, and a bill was planned for 2026.

## Key facts

- The 2026 annex applies to client digital assets held by AIs and by subsidiaries of locally incorporated AIs. Digital assets here means VAs, tokenised securities and other tokenised assets. Limited purpose tokens and the bank's own holdings are outside it [S:ce05e67bcdce, preamble and fn 1–4].
- The 2026 letter replaces the February 2024 circular. Existing providers are told to review and revise their controls. Unlike 2024, it sets no six-month deadline for confirming compliance [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2; S:6bccca749ac2, Implementation, p.2].
- For client VAs, the annex's key controls are generally required. For other digital assets the bank may take a risk-based approach, but tokens on public permissionless chains call for extra caution [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11 chapeau].
- The 98% cold-storage rule for client VAs has not changed [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(l) and fn 8; S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 6].
- **Compensation changed.** The 2024 annex asked for insurance or compensation covering 50% of client VAs in cold storage and 100% in hot storage [S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8]. The 2026 annex instead makes the bank liable for losses from incidents attributable to it, backed by adequate financial resources, which may include insurance [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]. The 50%/100% figure now appears only in the SFC's rule for VATPs [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.22].
- **Key location.** Keys and seeds must still be generated, stored and backed up in Hong Kong. There is one new exception. With HKMA consent, a bank may appoint an HKMA-licensed stablecoin issuer to hold that issuer's own coins, with keys in Hong Kong or another location the HKMA accepts [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b) and fn 7; para 14(iii)].
- **New operating controls.** These include cold-wallet whitelists with a system check on every transfer, and detection of intrusions into vaults, signing devices and code repositories [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 10]. The annex also requires due diligence on HSM vendors, no smart contracts in cold wallets on public chains, segregation of duties over wallet code, dedicated approval devices, air-gapped integrity checks, and prevention of blind signing [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a), (c), (f), (g), (i)]. Monitoring must run 24/7 [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 23].
- **AML section removed.** The 2024 annex had a separate AML/CFT paragraph [S:86e064b2a8d0, para 23]. The 2026 annex has none. The cover letter says meeting the annex does not displace other legal and regulatory requirements [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.2].
- Transfers to third parties are now allowed for payment services or client settlement instructions, if effective controls are in place. The definition of client now also covers clients of SFC-licensed VATPs [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(m) and fn 9].
- Staking from custody must follow the HKMA's April 2025 guidance, having regard to the SFC's staking terms for VATPs [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 26]. Under the HKMA guidance, the bank keeps control of everything needed to unstake, discloses staking risks such as slashing, and discusses its plans with the HKMA before launch [S:4641e6c404d2, sections A–C, Implementation]. VATPs may not use third-party custody for staked assets [S:50bf95ba3143, T&C para 4].
- The SFC's August 2025 circular took effect immediately. It says the weaknesses it describes exist whatever technology is used, whether HSM, MPC or multi-signature. The same standards will be core expectations for future VA custodians [S:562c0f8addce, pp.1–2, Way forward p.7].
- Under the custodian regime conclusions, a licence attaches to whoever safekeeps private keys and can move client VAs. Custody of tokenised securities is outside the regime [S:f41a543388bc, paras 6–8, 12]. Non-bank custodians need HK$10 million paid-up capital and HK$3 million liquid capital; banks remain under HKMA capital rules [S:f41a543388bc, para 42]. There will be no grandfathering, but banks already assessed by the HKMA get an expedited process [S:f41a543388bc, paras 48, 51].
- The consultation proposed that banks register with the SFC and that the HKMA be their frontline supervisor [S:30b60438f017, para 2.12(a)].

**Key-management terms, as the documents use them**

- **Hot and cold wallets.** In a hot wallet the private keys sit online, which makes them easier to attack. In a cold wallet they are kept offline [S:8a016a912528, fn 19].
- **HSM (hardware security module).** A tamper-resistant device used to generate and store keys, offline where practicable. Banks must vet the vendor's patching and re-certification [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a)].
- **Key sharding and multi-signature.** A key is split among several authorised staff, or several separate keys are required, so that no one individual can ever hold or use the whole key alone; a set number of shard-holders must act together to sign [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(i)]. For stablecoin issuers, the keys in a multi-signature set must be stored in separate, isolated environments [S:240be6833157, para 6.5.7(iv)].
- **MPC (multi-party computation).** Another way of distributing signing power. For licensing, the question is whether the provider can move client assets on its own [S:f41a543388bc, para 11].
- **Blind signing.** Approving a transaction without seeing its contents. Banks must show signers every detail in human-readable form before they sign [S:562c0f8addce, fn 6; S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(i)].
- **Whitelisting.** Deposits and withdrawals go only to addresses proved to belong to the client, for example by message signing or a micropayment test. Changes to a cold-wallet whitelist are tightly controlled [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10, 11(m)].
- **Air-gapped signing.** Devices that are never connected to a network, kept in a vault and changed only with physical access. They are used for integrity checks [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(g)]. Stablecoin issuers must generate and use their most important keys in air-gapped environments [S:240be6833157, para 6.5.7(ii), (vii)].

## What your bank must do

**Obligations**
- Keep client assets and wallet addresses segregated from the bank's own. Do not lend, pledge or encumber client assets except for settlement, with prior explicit written consent, or where the law requires it [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 6–7].
- Accept liability for losses attributable to the bank, and hold adequate resources to cover them [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)].
- Disclose the custody arrangements to clients in full. This covers rights on insolvency, segregation, insurance or compensation, commingling, and how forks and airdrops are handled [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 20].

**Controls & monitoring**
- Keep 98% of client VAs in cold storage, and keep keys and backups in Hong Kong [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b), (l) and fn 8].
- Train transaction signers in depth and run drills of the business continuity plan [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 3, 5].
- Reconcile each client's holdings regularly against both on-chain and off-chain records, and escalate breaks to senior management [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 21].
- Monitor 24/7, including vendors, protocols, cryptography and common libraries [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 23–24].

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- Talk to the HKMA before launching custody, and show that the bank meets the standards [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2].
- Discuss staking plans with the HKMA before launch [S:4641e6c404d2, Implementation, p.3].
- Provide custody records to the HKMA promptly when asked [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 22].

**Counterparty due diligence**
- Delegate VA custody only to another AI (or a local AI's subsidiary), to an SFC-licensed VATP, or, with HKMA consent, to a licensed issuer for its own coins [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 14].
- Before onboarding a custodian, have its code reviewed independently and understand how it releases software. After onboarding, test its security, incident reporting and disaster recovery [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 15].
- When the counterparty is a VATP, check it against VATP Guidelines paras 10.6–10.8 and 10.22 and against 25EC44 [S:f1bbafccd556; S:562c0f8addce].

## Talking points

**To the CCO**
- The 2026 annex replaced the 2024 one. The standard for an existing service is to review and revise, but the new wallet controls are expected now [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2].
- AML/CFT for custody still falls under the HKMA AML Guideline, even though the custody annex no longer repeats it [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.2; S:7bfdad704174, para 1.3].

**To business heads**
- We can now hold a licensed stablecoin through its issuer as delegate, with HKMA consent [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 14(iii)].
- Transfers to third parties for payments and settlement are now allowed if controls are in place [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(m)].

**To the CEO**
- The fixed insurance percentages have been replaced by bank liability plus adequate resources, so sizing the exposure is now for us to decide [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)].
- A statutory custodian licence is coming, with no grandfathering. Banks already assessed by the HKMA get an expedited process [S:f41a543388bc, paras 48, 51].

**To Risk / CRO**
- The SFC says choosing HSM, MPC or multi-signature does not by itself remove the vulnerabilities. Process controls decide the outcome [S:562c0f8addce, p.1].
- Staking adds slashing, lock-up and validator risk. The bank must keep the means to unstake [S:4641e6c404d2, sections A–B].

## Common mix-ups

- "Banks need 50%/100% insurance cover." That was the 2024 rule for banks and remains the rule for VATPs, not for banks from 2026 [S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8; S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.22; S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)].
- "The February 2024 annex is still current." It was superseded on 27 May 2026. One canon entry (86e064b2a8d0) still shows it as in force, which is wrong according to the 2026 letter [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2].
- "Keys may never leave Hong Kong." The general rule stands, but an issuer delegated to hold its own coins may keep keys elsewhere if the HKMA accepts the location [S:ce05e67bcdce, fn 7].
- "The custodian regime covers tokenised securities." It does not, because the statutory VA definition excludes securities [S:f41a543388bc, para 12].
- "MPC or HSM equals compliance." The SFC rejects that view [S:562c0f8addce, p.1].

## Open items

- The corpus has only the BRDR landing page for the HKMA staking circular of 7 April 2025, not the PDF text. Points cited to 4641e6c404d2 rely on the canon summary and should be checked against the PDF.
- The custodian bill was planned for the 2026 LegCo session [S:f41a543388bc, Introduction, p.3]. The corpus does not show it has been introduced, and no commencement date is set [S:f41a543388bc, para 49].
- The SFC will consult separately on whether private VA funds must use SFC-regulated custodians [S:f41a543388bc, para 32].
- The corpus does not say whether a separate custody-specific AML/CFT guidance from the HKMA exists, as the 2024 annex mentioned.
