# C3 Custody of digital assets and key management

For the Head of Compliance at a bank that holds, or plans to hold, clients' crypto or tokenised assets: after this module you can explain what the Hong Kong Monetary Authority's (HKMA's) May 2026 custody standards expect, what changed from 2024, how staking fits in, and how the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) and stablecoin rules set the benchmark for counterparties.

## In 60 seconds

In virtual asset (VA) custody, the core risk sits with whoever safekeeps the private keys, or similar instruments, that can move the assets [S:f41a543388bc, para 7]. Much of the rulebook is therefore about key management [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10–11]. The HKMA set custody standards for banks (authorized institutions, or AIs) in February 2024 and replaced them on 27 May 2026 with an updated annex of 26 paragraphs [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2; S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 1–26]. The new annex keeps the 98% cold-storage rule for client VAs and the rule that keys are kept in Hong Kong [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b), (l) and fn 8]. It adds detailed controls on how wallets are operated, drops the fixed insurance percentages, and, with HKMA consent, lets a bank appoint a licensed stablecoin issuer to hold that issuer's own coins [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10–11, fn 7, para 14(iii); S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8]. The SFC sets its own custody rules for licensed virtual asset trading platforms (VATPs) [S:f1bbafccd556, Part X]. It says the standards in its August 2025 custody circular will also be core expectations for future VA custodians [S:562c0f8addce, p.2]. The Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau (FSTB) published, with the SFC, consultation conclusions on a custodian licensing regime in December 2025, with a stated aim of introducing a bill into the Legislative Council (LegCo) in 2026 [S:f41a543388bc, Introduction, p.3].

## Key facts

- The 2026 annex applies to client digital assets held by AIs and by subsidiaries of locally incorporated AIs. Examples of digital assets it covers are VAs, tokenised securities and other tokenised assets. Limited purpose digital tokens and the bank's or its group's own holdings are outside it [S:ce05e67bcdce, preamble and fn 1–4].
- The 2026 letter replaces the February 2024 circular. Existing providers should review and revise their systems and controls to meet the latest standards. Unlike 2024, it sets no six-month deadline for notifying the HKMA and confirming compliance [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2; S:6bccca749ac2, Implementation, p.2].
- For client VAs, the annex's key controls are generally required. For other digital assets the bank may take a risk-based approach, but tokens on public permissionless chains call for extra caution [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11 chapeau].
- The 98% cold-storage rule for client VAs has not changed [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(l) and fn 8; S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 6].
- **Compensation changed.** The 2024 annex asked for insurance or compensation covering 50% of client VAs in cold storage and 100% in hot and other storage [S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8]. The 2026 annex instead expects the bank to be liable for losses from incidents attributable to it, backed by adequate financial resources, which may include insurance [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]. The 50%/100% figure remains in the SFC's rule for VATPs, as an SFC-approved compensation arrangement [S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.22].
- **Key location.** Seeds and private keys should still be generated, stored and backed up in Hong Kong. There is one new exception. With HKMA consent, a bank may appoint an HKMA-licensed stablecoin issuer to hold that issuer's own coins, with keys in Hong Kong or another location the HKMA accepts [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b) and fn 7; para 14(iii)].
- **New operating controls.** These include cold-wallet whitelists with a system check on every transfer, and detection of intrusions into vaults, signing devices and code repositories [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 10]. The annex also sets out due diligence on hardware security module (HSM) vendors, no smart contracts in cold wallets on public chains, segregation of duties over wallet code, dedicated approval devices, air-gapped integrity checks, and prevention of blind signing [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a), (c), (f), (g), (i)]. Security monitoring should run 24/7 [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 23].
- **AML section removed.** The 2024 annex had a separate anti-money laundering and counter-financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) paragraph [S:86e064b2a8d0, para 23]. The 2026 annex has none. The cover letter reminds AIs to also comply with all applicable legal and regulatory requirements [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.2].
- Transfers to third parties are now allowed to support payment services or carry out client settlement instructions, if effective controls are in place. The definition of client now also covers clients of SFC-licensed VATPs [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(m) and fn 9].
- For staking from custody, the annex points to the HKMA's April 2025 staking guidance and asks banks to have due regard to the SFC's staking terms for VATPs [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 26]. Under the HKMA guidance, the bank should keep control of everything needed to unstake, disclose staking risks such as slashing, and discuss its plans with the HKMA before launch [S:4641e6c404d2, sections A–B, Implementation p.3]. VATPs may not use third-party custody for staked assets [S:50bf95ba3143, para 6].
- The SFC's August 2025 circular took effect immediately. It says the overseas incidents it describes point to potential weaknesses whatever custody solution is used, whether HSM, multi-party computation (MPC) or multi-signature. Its standards will also be core expectations for future VA custodians [S:562c0f8addce, pp.1–2, Way forward p.7].
- Under the custodian regime conclusions, the licence will cover custodians that safekeep private keys or similar instruments that enable VAs to be transferred. Custodians of tokenised securities that do not hold VAs fall outside the regime [S:f41a543388bc, paras 6–8, 12]. The SFC will set baseline requirements of HK$10 million paid-up share capital and HK$3 million liquid capital; banks remain subject to HKMA capital requirements [S:f41a543388bc, paras 41–42]. No deeming arrangement is planned for existing providers, but banks already assessed by the HKMA and already providing VA custody get an expedited process [S:f41a543388bc, paras 48, 51].
- The consultation paper proposed that the SFC license and register VA custodians and set standards, with the HKMA as frontline regulator supervising banks' VA custodian activities [S:30b60438f017, para 2.12(a)]. The conclusions list banks and subsidiaries of locally incorporated banks that safekeep keys among those needing a licence or registration [S:f41a543388bc, para 35(b)].

**Key-management terms, as the documents use them**

- **Hot and cold wallets.** In a hot wallet the private keys sit online, which makes them easier to attack. In a cold wallet they are kept offline [S:8a016a912528, fn 19].
- **HSM (hardware security module).** A secure, tamper-resistant device for generating and storing seeds and keys. Banks should check that the vendor keeps up patching and re-certification [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a)].
- **Key sharding and multi-signature.** With sharding, a key is split among several authorised staff so that no one person holds all of it, and a set number of shard-holders must act together to sign [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(i)]. With multi-signature, several keys are involved; for stablecoin issuers, those keys should be stored in separate, isolated environments [S:240be6833157, para 6.5.7(iv)].
- **MPC (multi-party computation).** Another way of distributing signing power. For licensing, a key question is whether the provider can move client assets on its own [S:f41a543388bc, para 11].
- **Blind signing.** Approving a transaction without reviewing its contents. Banks should show signers every detail in human-readable form before they sign [S:562c0f8addce, fn 6; S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(i)].
- **Whitelisting.** Deposits and withdrawals go only to whitelisted addresses shown to belong to the client, for example by message signing or a micropayment test, subject to the third-party transfer exception. Changes to a cold-wallet whitelist are tightly controlled [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10, 11(m)].
- **Air-gapped devices.** Devices kept off networks, stored in a cold vault and changed only with physical access. Banks should use them for integrity checks on critical transaction data [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(g)]. Stablecoin issuers should generate, and use, their most significant keys in air-gapped environments [S:240be6833157, para 6.5.7(ii), (vii)].

## What your bank must do

**Obligations**
- Keep client assets and wallet addresses segregated from the bank's own. Do not lend, pledge or encumber client assets except for settlement or fees owed, with prior explicit written consent, or where the law requires it [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 6–7 and fn 6].
- Be liable for losses attributable to the bank, and hold adequate financial resources to cover them [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)].
- Disclose the custody arrangements to clients fully and fairly. This covers rights on insolvency, segregation, insurance or compensation, commingling, and how forks and airdrops are handled [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 20].

**Controls & monitoring**
- Keep 98% of client VAs in cold storage, and keep seeds, keys and backups in Hong Kong [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(b), (l) and fn 8].
- Train transaction signers in depth and run drills of the business continuity plan [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 3, 5].
- Reconcile each client's holdings regularly against both on-chain and off-chain records, and escalate discrepancies to senior management [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 21].
- Monitor security 24/7, covering dependencies such as vendors, blockchain protocols, encryption algorithms and common libraries [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 23–24].

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- Discuss plans with the HKMA before launching custody, and show that the bank meets the standards [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2].
- Discuss staking plans with the HKMA before launch [S:4641e6c404d2, Implementation, p.3].
- Provide custody records to the HKMA promptly when asked [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 22].

**Counterparty due diligence**
- As a general principle, delegate VA custody only to another AI (or a local AI's subsidiary), to an SFC-licensed VATP, or, with HKMA consent, to a licensed stablecoin issuer for the coins it issues [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 14].
- Before onboarding a custodian, include independent code reviews and understand how it develops and releases software. After onboarding, regularly evaluate its security controls, require timely incident reporting, and test its disaster recovery [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 15].
- For a VATP counterparty, the SFC's own custody standards are in the VATP Guidelines, for example paras 10.6–10.8 (cold storage, whitelisted addresses, key management) and 10.22 (compensation arrangement), as elaborated in 25EC44 [S:f1bbafccd556, paras 10.6–10.8, 10.22; S:562c0f8addce, pp.1–2]. The HKMA annex cites the VATP Guidelines for reference on delegation [S:ce05e67bcdce, fn 10].

## Talking points

**To the CCO (chief compliance officer)**
- The 2026 annex replaced the 2024 one. An existing service should review and revise its systems and controls to meet the latest standards; the letter sets no transition period [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2].
- The custody annex no longer has an AML/CFT section, but the cover letter reminds AIs to comply with all applicable requirements, and the HKMA's AML/CFT Guideline sets the standards AIs should meet [S:ed61d7c335e7, p.2; S:7bfdad704174, para 1.3].

**To business heads**
- With HKMA consent, a bank may now delegate custody of a licensed stablecoin to its issuer [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 14(iii)].
- Transfers to third parties for payments and settlement are now allowed if effective controls are in place [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(m)].

**To the CEO**
- The fixed insurance percentages have been replaced by bank liability plus adequate financial resources; the annex sets no fixed percentage [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n); S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8].
- The FSTB and SFC conclusions propose a statutory custodian licence with no deeming arrangement. Banks already assessed by the HKMA get an expedited process [S:f41a543388bc, paras 48, 51].

**To Risk / CRO (chief risk officer)**
- The SFC says the weaknesses shown by overseas incidents can arise whichever custody solution is used, whether HSM, MPC or multi-signature [S:562c0f8addce, p.1].
- The HKMA lists slashing, lock-up and validator inactivity among staking risks to disclose. The bank should keep control of the means to unstake [S:4641e6c404d2, sections A–B].

## Common mix-ups

- "Banks need 50%/100% insurance cover." That was the 2024 rule for banks and remains the rule for VATPs, not for banks from 2026 [S:86e064b2a8d0, fn 8; S:f1bbafccd556, para 10.22; S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)].
- "The February 2024 annex is still current." It was superseded on 27 May 2026 [S:ed61d7c335e7, Implementation, p.2].
- "Keys may never leave Hong Kong." The general rule stands, but an issuer delegated to hold its own coins may keep keys elsewhere if the HKMA accepts the location [S:ce05e67bcdce, fn 7].
- "The custodian regime covers tokenised securities." It does not, because the statutory VA definition excludes securities [S:f41a543388bc, para 12].
- "MPC or HSM equals compliance." The SFC says weaknesses can exist whichever custody solution is used [S:562c0f8addce, p.1].

## Open items

- The custodian bill had a stated aim of introduction into LegCo in 2026 [S:f41a543388bc, Introduction, p.3]. The corpus does not show it has been introduced, and no commencement date is set [S:f41a543388bc, para 49].
- The SFC said it would consult, in a separate paper issued the same day, on whether VA management service providers must use SFC-regulated custodians for private funds' VAs [S:f41a543388bc, para 32].
- The 2024 annex referred to AML/CFT guidance from the HKMA on custodial activities of digital assets [S:86e064b2a8d0, para 23]. The corpus does not show whether such separate guidance exists.
