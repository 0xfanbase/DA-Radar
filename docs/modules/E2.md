# E2 Wholesale money, CBDC and cross-border projects

For the bank's Head of Compliance (digital assets): after this module you can explain the HKMA's central bank digital currency (CBDC) work, from e-HKD to mBridge and Ensemble, and say what compliance role the published designs give to banks.

## In 60 seconds

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) has studied a digital Hong Kong dollar (e-HKD) since 2017, starting with wholesale (bank-to-bank) money. It consulted twice on a retail e-HKD in 2021-2022 and ran two industry pilot phases in 2023-2025. In October 2025 it decided wholesale uses come first, and it has not decided whether or when to launch a retail e-HKD. Its cross-border work grew from Project Inthanon-LionRock with the Bank of Thailand (BoT) into mBridge, a multi-CBDC platform that reached minimum viable product (MVP) stage in June 2024. Project Ensemble now links tokenised deposits and wholesale central bank money at home, with tie-ups in France, Thailand, Brazil and Shanghai. In every published design, banks keep the customer-facing compliance work: know-your-customer (KYC), anti-money laundering (AML) and sanctions checks.

## Key facts

- The HKMA's e-HKD work began in 2017 with Project LionRock, a proof of concept with the three note-issuing banks and Hong Kong Interbank Clearing Limited. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Project LionRock' section]
- The October 2021 technical paper proposed a two-tier design: the central bank issues e-HKD, and banks distribute it. It said the design was not a blueprint. [S:59a20457a2fc, Executive Summary points 3-4]
- The April 2022 policy paper ruled out full anonymity so e-HKD could support AML/CFT compliance, and floated tiered wallets. [S:3b42966a786e, Section 5.3]
- In September 2022 the HKMA set a three-rail approach and found existing law not adequate to issue digital legal tender with enough certainty. [S:35cb94c89a11, Rail 1 section]
- Phase 1 of the pilot (16 firms, 14 pilots) found possible value in programmability, tokenisation and atomic settlement. [S:9f8886b0fb21, Executive Summary, p.6]
- In September 2024 the project became Project e-HKD+, covering both e-HKD and tokenised deposits; Phase 2 ran 11 pilot groups. [S:2f93aa2b9560, paras 1-2]
- On 28 October 2025 the HKMA made wholesale payments the near-term e-HKD priority, not retail use. [S:6bd9e65817c4, para 4]
- The HKMA's September 2026 CBDC page says it has not decided whether or when to introduce e-HKD for retail use. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Policy Stance and Three-Rail Approach']
- Inthanon-LionRock began in Q4 2019. In February 2021 the PBoC's Digital Currency Institute and the Central Bank of the UAE joined, and it was renamed the m-CBDC Bridge. [S:6b0fae230518, para 1 and fn 1]
- The HKMA page says the Bank for International Settlements Innovation Hub (BISIH) Hong Kong Centre gave 'strong support' as the project became mBridge. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Project mBridge' section]
- In the 2022 pilot, 20 banks in four jurisdictions ran more than 160 payment and foreign exchange (FX) trades worth over US$22 million. [S:947fa49606ab, s.1 Executive Summary, p.4]
- At MVP in June 2024, the project team listed was BISIH Hong Kong Centre, Bank of Thailand, PBoC DCI, Central Bank of the UAE and the HKMA; the Saudi Central Bank joined as a full participant. [S:db56d97e2206, fn 1, para 3]
- In October 2025 the government told LegCo that mBridge had become part of Hong Kong's financial market infrastructure, with participating banks setting their own fees. [S:2ffb8727bb2f, Reply (4)]
- Aurum built a two-tier prototype with a wholesale interbank system and retail wallets; Sela tested letting non-bank Access Enablers connect directly to a central bank ledger. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Collaborative Projects']
- Project Ensemble explores settling tokenised money between banks using wholesale CBDC, starting with tokenised deposits. [S:5a9cad53f2be, para 2]
- The November 2025 government digital green bond was the first digital bond to settle using e-HKD and e-CNY as tokenised central bank money, alongside traditional rails. [S:ac6cd085a325, para 4]
- Tier-4 personal e-CNY wallets for Hong Kong residents cap balances at RMB10,000 and allow retail purchases only, not person-to-person transfers. [S:08caafe0cfe7, Annex 2]
- About 80,000 e-CNY wallets had been registered with Hong Kong mobile numbers by end-January 2026, and 18 banks support top-ups through the Faster Payment System (FPS). [S:062b0a1547b9, Reply (1)]

## What your bank must do

These are pilot and research designs, not binding rules. The lines below show what the published designs expect of participating banks.

**Obligations**
- [ ] On mBridge, run AML, counter-terrorist financing (CTF) and sanctions checks before sending or confirming each payment; the platform does not run them. [S:947fa49606ab, s.6.5.2, p.30]
- [ ] On mBridge, meet each jurisdiction's AML/CTF/sanctions laws; the platform records only a pass or fail certificate from the bank's off-platform check. [S:947fa49606ab, s.7.2(d), p.35]

**Controls & monitoring**
- [ ] Keep KYC, AML and CFT compliance off-bridge in the bank's own systems. [S:89b8fcadfaa8, p.3]
- [ ] For any e-HKD distribution role, plan for customer due diligence and record-keeping at the intermediary. [S:3b42966a786e, Section 5.4]

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- [ ] Use the HKMA Supervisory Incubator for distributed ledger technology (DLT) to check risk controls before a tokenised-deposit launch. [S:1b1a0de5de5a, paras 2-3]

**Counterparty due diligence**
- [ ] On mBridge, only domestically licensed banks can request issuance or redemption of their own jurisdiction's CBDC. Check which role each counterparty has. [S:947fa49606ab, s.7.1.1, pp.31-32]

## Talking points

**To the CCO**
- mBridge leaves AML and sanctions screening to each bank, off the platform. [S:947fa49606ab, s.6.5.2, p.30]
- The retail e-HKD designs put customer due diligence on distributing intermediaries, not the HKMA. [S:3b42966a786e, Section 5.4]

**To business heads**
- The HKMA's e-HKD focus is now wholesale: tokenisation and cross-border settlement. [S:6bd9e65817c4, para 4]
- Phase 2 found banks slightly prefer tokenised deposits over e-HKD. [S:bdefe7fc69d4, Executive Summary, p.2]

**To the CEO**
- mBridge reached MVP in June 2024; the government calls it part of Hong Kong's financial market infrastructure. [S:db56d97e2206, para 1]; [S:2ffb8727bb2f, Reply (4)]
- There is no decision to issue a retail e-HKD. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Policy Stance and Three-Rail Approach']

**To Risk / CRO**
- The 2022 mBridge pilot ran on one cloud environment in Hong Kong, flagged as a privacy and governance point to revisit. [S:947fa49606ab, s.7.1.3, p.33]
- Phase 2 found privacy tools raised gas fees by up to 320 times. [S:bdefe7fc69d4, Section 4 'Privacy', p.31]

## Common mix-ups

- "Hong Kong is about to launch a retail e-HKD." The HKMA has made wholesale the priority and has made no launch decision. [S:6bd9e65817c4, paras 4-5]
- "mBridge screens payments for sanctions." Each bank screens off-platform. [S:947fa49606ab, s.6.5.2, p.30]
- "Any bank can mint any CBDC on mBridge." Only domestic banks can request issuance or redemption of their home CBDC. [S:89b8fcadfaa8, p.2]
- "e-CNY wallets work like general wallets." Person-to-person transfers are not allowed. [S:08caafe0cfe7, Annex 2 'Usage']
- "Inthanon-LionRock and mBridge are different projects." mBridge is the renamed later phase. [S:3044ec37475a, Executive Summary, p.7]

## Open items

- The corpus has no official Hong Kong document after June 2024 describing any change in BIS Innovation Hub's role in mBridge. This module states BISIH's role only as of the 2024 MVP release and the HKMA's CBDC page.
- The HKMA said retail e-HKD groundwork would be done by H1 2026. Its September 2026 page says the preparatory work is completed but gives no launch decision. [S:d51dc8884c8a, 'Project e-HKD+' section]
