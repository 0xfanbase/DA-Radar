# D5 Tax and information reporting

For the bank's Head of Compliance (digital assets): after this module you can explain what the Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) bill would require, where it overlaps with the bank's existing Common Reporting Standard (CRS) duties, and what the government has said about tax concessions for digital-asset funds.

## In 60 seconds

CARF is an Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) standard for yearly exchange of crypto-asset tax data. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview] Hong Kong committed to it in December 2024 and consulted on it in December 2025. [S:ea81f92ea327, para 1]; [S:252a7f86684e, para 1] The Inland Revenue (Amendment) (CARF and Amended CRS) Bill 2026 appeared in the Gazette on 22 May 2026 and had its First Reading in the Legislative Council (LegCo) on 3 June 2026; the documents reviewed show no passage as of 25 September 2026. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview] If passed, CARF duties would start on 1 January 2027 and amended CRS duties on 1 January 2028. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(3)-(4)] A business that carries out crypto exchange transactions for customers would be a Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Provider (RCASP). With a Hong Kong link, it would have to register, run due diligence, report and keep records, with fines for failure. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, ss.50M, 50O-50Q, 80R] Bank accounts are already covered by CRS, and the amended CRS brings in crypto-linked assets and digital money products. [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 4.3-4.5] Separately, the government said it would extend fund and family-office tax concessions to digital assets, aiming at year of assessment 2025/26. [S:a29b51bffc6b, para 15]; [S:654fa5eba8cf, para 98]

## Key facts

- On 13 December 2024 the government told the OECD Global Forum it would implement CARF, aiming for local law by 2026 and first exchanges from 2028, on a reciprocal basis with partners that protect data. [S:ea81f92ea327, paras 1, 4]
- The December 2025 consultation paper proposed amending the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO, Cap. 112) for both CARF and the amended CRS; comments closed on 6 February 2026. [S:252a7f86684e, paras 4, 7]
- The consultation called CARF and the amended CRS finalised global standards and sought views only on local implementation choices. [S:275d5cc93a88, para 8, PDF p.8 (printed p.4)]
- The bill appeared in the Gazette on 22 May 2026 and was put to LegCo for First Reading on 3 June 2026. LegCo's notice for that sitting said the Second Reading debate would be adjourned. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview]; [S:d5097f447cf9, para 1]
- If passed, Part 2 (CARF) would commence on 1 January 2027 and Part 3 (amended CRS) on 1 January 2028; the rest would start on the day the enacted Ordinance is published in the Gazette. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(2)-(4)]
- The bill defines an RCASP as any individual or entity whose business carries out crypto exchange transactions on customers' behalf, whether by acting as the other side of the trade, by brokering it, or by running a trading venue. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50M "reporting crypto-asset service provider"]
- Under the bill, an RCASP would be a Hong Kong RCASP (HKRCASP) through Hong Kong tax residence, Hong Kong incorporation (with legal personality or a duty to file tax returns with the IRD), management from Hong Kong, a regular Hong Kong place of business, or a Hong Kong branch through which relevant transactions are carried out. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50N(1)-(2)]
- The bill would require an HKRCASP to register with the Commissioner by 31 January of the year after it first meets a nexus test. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50O(1)-(2)]
- Three kinds of transaction are reportable: crypto-to-fiat exchanges, crypto-to-crypto exchanges, and transfers. Central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) and specified electronic money products are outside the relevant crypto-assets CARF covers. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Scope of crypto-assets covered' and 'Transactions subject to reporting']
- RCASPs would have to get tax-residence self-certifications from new customers and check they are reasonable. For existing users they would have 12 months after the rules start. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Due diligence requirements']
- The bill proposes that records be kept for six years after the related return's due date. If a former RCASP entity is dissolved, its last directors would have to make sure the records are still kept. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Record keeping requirements']
- Failing to register, do due diligence or file a return without reasonable excuse would be an offence, with a fine at level 3 on conviction. Using a service provider would not, on its own, be a reasonable excuse. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(1)-(3)]
- Extra fines would apply: $500 a day while a registration, return or notice failure continues after conviction. A due-diligence breach could instead be fined $1,000 per affected user or controlling person, if that is higher than the level 3 fine. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(4)-(5)]
- The bill also proposes a financial penalty, set by the Commissioner, in place of prosecution, with written representations and appeal to the Board of Review. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Penalty provisions']; [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, ss.82E-82F]
- Under the amended CRS, CBDCs and specified electronic money products join the depository-account concept. Derivatives referencing crypto-assets become financial assets, and crypto-assets become eligible investments for investment entities. [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 4.3-4.5]
- The consultation proposed that an entity that is both a CRS reporting financial institution (RFI) and an RCASP report crypto sale or redemption proceeds under both regimes, not once. [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 4.14-4.17]
- Policy Statement 2.0 said the government would propose adding specified digital assets to the qualifying transactions for profits tax concessions of privately offered funds and FIHVs, effective from year of assessment 2025/26 if LegCo approves. [S:a29b51bffc6b, para 15]
- The 2026-27 Budget said a bill to be introduced in the first half of 2026 would class digital assets as qualifying investments for these concessions, aiming at year of assessment 2025/26. [S:654fa5eba8cf, para 98]

## What your bank must do

These duties bind only once the bill passes and commences. The bill is not yet law.

**Obligations**
- [ ] Map whether any group entity, as a business, carries out crypto exchange transactions for customers, including as intermediary. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50M]
- [ ] Test each such entity against the Hong Kong nexus rules, including branches. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50N(1)-(2)]
- [ ] Plan registration by 31 January of the year after nexus is first met. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50O(2)]
- [ ] Plan for due diligence from 1 January 2027, including self-certifications for existing users within 12 months. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(3)]; [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Due diligence requirements']
- [ ] Prepare CRS systems for amended CRS due diligence from 1 January 2028. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(4)]; [S:275d5cc93a88, para 4.18]

**Controls & monitoring**
- [ ] Keep CARF due-diligence evidence and reporting records for six years after each return's due date. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Record keeping requirements']
- [ ] If CARF work is outsourced, keep oversight; under the bill, using a service provider would not in itself be a reasonable excuse. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(2)]
- [ ] If using self-developed software, plan to submit test data files to the IRD for validation; the consultation's plan set the fourth quarter of 2027 for this. [S:275d5cc93a88, Chapter 6 item (1)(c)]

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- [ ] CARF registration and returns would go to the IRD through its planned CARF Portal, not to the HKMA or SFC; watch for the portal and data schema. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Reporting portal and mandatory registration requirement']

**Counterparty due diligence**
- [ ] Where the bank partners with a crypto trading platform or broker, note that the counterparty may itself be an RCASP with its own reporting. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Individuals and entities subject to due diligence and reporting requirements']

## Talking points

**To the CCO**
- The CARF bill had its First Reading on 3 June 2026. If passed, CARF duties would start on 1 January 2027 and amended CRS on 1 January 2028. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview]
- Outsourcing CARF work to a vendor is not, in itself, a reasonable excuse for failing to register, do due diligence or report. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(2)]

**To business heads**
- Providing crypto exchange services for customers as a business, even as intermediary, could make the entity an RCASP. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50M]
- New crypto customers will need a tax-residence self-certification; existing ones within 12 months of start. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Due diligence requirements']

**To the CEO**
- Hong Kong committed to CARF in December 2024, with first data exchanges targeted from 2028. [S:ea81f92ea327, para 4]
- The government linked CARF to Hong Kong's reputation as a financial centre. [S:ab980a841782, para 3]

**To Risk / CRO**
- Under the bill, fines could scale: $500 a day for failures that continue after conviction, or $1,000 per affected user or controlling person for due-diligence breaches. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(4)-(5)]
- The consultation proposed dual CARF and CRS reporting of crypto proceeds for entities in both regimes. [S:275d5cc93a88, para 4.17]

## Common mix-ups

- "CARF is already law." It is a bill before LegCo; the IRD says the dates apply subject to passage. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview]
- "Registration is due by 31 December of the year nexus is first met." That was the consultation's main proposal. The bill sets 31 January of the year after nexus is first met. [S:275d5cc93a88, Chapter 6 item (1)(b) and footnote 45]; [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50O(2)]
- "Amended CRS starts in 2029." Duties start on 1 January 2028; 2029 is the first amended-CRS exchange. [S:275d5cc93a88, para 4.18]; [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(4)]
- "CARF covers CBDCs and e-money." Those are excluded from CARF but brought into the amended CRS. [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 1.5, 4.3]
- "The HKMA or SFC supervises CARF." The IRD runs registration, returns and penalties. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Reporting portal' and 'Penalty provisions']

## Open items

- Passage of the CARF bill is not recorded in the documents reviewed for this guide as of 25 September 2026. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview]; [S:d5097f447cf9, para 1]
- The consultation also proposed mandatory CRS registration for all RFIs from January 2027 and heavier CRS penalties. The bill's list of provisions in the documents reviewed for this guide shows no RFI registration clause. Whether that proposal moved to other legislation is not stated. [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 5.3, 5.9-5.12, 5.15, Chapter 6 item (2)(a)]; [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Contents]
- Whether the final bill keeps the proposed dual-reporting default is not stated on the IRD page.
- The IRD page says the lists of reportable and partner jurisdictions, the CARF Portal and the data schema will come in due course. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Lists of reportable jurisdictions' and 'Reporting portal']
- Fund/FIHV concessions: the 2026 Policy Address says a bill easing taxes on fund vehicles, single-family offices and carried-interest earners has already passed. No document reviewed for this guide has a bill text confirming how digital assets are treated in the enacted version. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 40]
- The documents reviewed do not say whether tokenised deposits count as specified electronic money products for CRS.
