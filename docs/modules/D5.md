# D5 Tax and information reporting

For the bank's Head of Compliance (digital assets): after this module you can explain what the Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) bill would require, where it overlaps with the bank's existing Common Reporting Standard (CRS) duties, and what the government has said about tax concessions for digital-asset funds.

## In 60 seconds

CARF is an Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) standard for yearly exchange of crypto-asset tax data; Hong Kong committed to it in December 2024 and consulted in December 2025. The Inland Revenue (Amendment) (CARF and Amended CRS) Bill 2026 appeared in the Gazette on 22 May 2026 and is now before the Legislative Council (LegCo); the corpus shows no passage as of 25 September 2026. If passed, CARF duties start on 1 January 2027 and amended CRS duties on 1 January 2028. A business that carries out crypto exchange transactions for customers is a Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Provider (RCASP) and would have to register, run due diligence, report and keep records, with fines for failure. Banks already report under CRS, and the amended CRS pulls in crypto-linked assets and digital money products. Separately, the government said fund and family-office tax concessions would cover digital assets from year of assessment 2025/26.

## Key facts

- On 13 December 2024 the government told the OECD Global Forum it would implement CARF, aiming for local law by 2026 and first exchanges from 2028, on a reciprocal basis with partners that protect data. [S:ea81f92ea327, paras 1, 4]
- The December 2025 consultation paper proposed amending the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO, Cap. 112) for both CARF and the amended CRS; comments closed on 6 February 2026. [S:252a7f86684e, paras 4, 7]
- The consultation called CARF and the amended CRS finalised global standards and sought views only on local implementation choices. [S:275d5cc93a88, para 8, PDF p.4]
- The bill appeared in the Gazette on 22 May 2026 and was put to LegCo for First Reading on 3 June 2026. LegCo's notice for that sitting said the Second Reading debate would be adjourned. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview]; [S:d5097f447cf9, para 2]
- If passed, Part 2 (CARF) commences on 1 January 2027 and Part 3 (amended CRS) on 1 January 2028; the rest starts on gazettal. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(2)-(4)]
- The bill defines an RCASP as anyone whose business carries out crypto exchange transactions on customers' behalf, whether by acting as the other side of the trade, by brokering it, or by running a trading venue. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50M "reporting crypto-asset service provider"]
- An RCASP becomes a Hong Kong RCASP (HKRCASP) through Hong Kong tax residence, incorporation, management from Hong Kong, a regular place of business, or a Hong Kong branch that carries out relevant transactions. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50N(1)-(2)]
- The bill requires an HKRCASP to register with the Commissioner by 31 January of the year after it first meets a nexus test. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50O(1)-(2)]
- Three kinds of transaction are reportable: crypto-to-fiat exchanges, crypto-to-crypto exchanges, and transfers. Central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) and specified electronic money products are outside the relevant crypto-assets CARF covers. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Scope of crypto-assets covered' and 'Transactions subject to reporting']
- RCASPs must get tax-residence self-certifications from new customers. For existing users they have 12 months after the rules start. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Due diligence requirements']
- Records must be kept for six years after the return's due date. The duty passes to former directors if the entity is dissolved. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Record keeping requirements']
- Failing to register, do due diligence or file a return without reasonable excuse is an offence with a level 3 fine. Using a service provider is not, on its own, a reasonable excuse. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(1)-(3)]
- Extra fines apply: $500 a day for continuing registration or return failures. A due-diligence breach can be fined at $1,000 per affected user or controlling person, if higher. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(4)-(5)]
- The bill also provides a financial penalty in place of prosecution, with written representations and appeal to the Board of Review. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Penalty provisions']; [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, ss.82E-82F]
- Under the amended CRS, CBDCs and specified electronic money products join the depository-account concept. Derivatives referencing crypto-assets become financial assets, and crypto-assets become eligible investments for investment entities. [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 4.3-4.5]
- The consultation proposed that an entity that is both a CRS reporting financial institution (RFI) and an RCASP report crypto sale or redemption proceeds under both regimes, not once. [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 4.14-4.17]
- Policy Statement 2.0 said the government would propose adding specified digital assets to the qualifying transactions for profits tax concessions of privately offered funds and FIHVs, effective from year of assessment 2025/26 if LegCo approves. [S:a29b51bffc6b, para 15]
- The 2026-27 Budget said a bill to be introduced in the first half of 2026 would class digital assets as qualifying investments for these concessions, aiming at year of assessment 2025/26. [S:654fa5eba8cf, para 98]

## What your bank must do

These duties bind only once the bill passes and commences. The bill is not yet law.

**Obligations**
- [ ] Map whether any group entity, as a business, carries out crypto exchange transactions for customers, including as intermediary. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50M]
- [ ] Test each such entity against the Hong Kong nexus rules, including branches. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50N(1)-(2)]
- [ ] Plan registration by 31 January of the year after nexus is first met. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50O(2)]
- [ ] Plan for due diligence from 1 January 2027, including self-certifications for existing users within 12 months. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(3)]; [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Due diligence requirements']
- [ ] Prepare CRS systems for amended CRS due diligence from 1 January 2028. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(4)]; [S:275d5cc93a88, para 4.18]

**Controls & monitoring**
- [ ] Keep CARF due-diligence evidence and reporting records for six years after each return's due date. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Record keeping requirements']
- [ ] If CARF work is outsourced, keep oversight; outsourcing is not a defence. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(2)]
- [ ] Plan test-file validation of CARF data before filing; the consultation set Q4 2027 for this. [S:275d5cc93a88, Chapter 6 item (1)(c)]

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- [ ] CARF notices and returns go to the Inland Revenue Department (IRD), not the HKMA or SFC; watch for the IRD CARF Portal and data schema. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Reporting portal and mandatory registration requirement']

**Counterparty due diligence**
- [ ] Where the bank partners with a VA trading platform or custodian, note that the counterparty may itself be an RCASP with its own reporting. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Individuals and entities subject to due diligence and reporting requirements']

## Talking points

**To the CCO**
- The CARF bill had its First Reading on 3 June 2026. If passed, CARF duties start on 1 January 2027 and amended CRS on 1 January 2028. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview]
- Outsourcing CARF work to a vendor is not, in itself, a reasonable excuse for failing to register, do due diligence or report. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(2)]

**To business heads**
- Any customer crypto exchange service, even as intermediary, can make the entity an RCASP. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50M]
- New crypto customers will need a tax-residence self-certification; existing ones within 12 months of start. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Due diligence requirements']

**To the CEO**
- Hong Kong committed to CARF in December 2024, with first data exchanges targeted from 2028. [S:ea81f92ea327, para 4]
- The government linked CARF to Hong Kong's reputation as a financial centre. [S:ab980a841782, para 3]

**To Risk / CRO**
- Fines can scale: $500 a day for continuing failures, or $1,000 per affected user for due-diligence breaches. [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.80R(4)-(5)]
- The consultation proposed dual CARF and CRS reporting of crypto proceeds for entities in both regimes. [S:275d5cc93a88, para 4.17]

## Common mix-ups

- "CARF is already law." It is a bill before LegCo; the IRD says the dates apply subject to passage. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, Overview]
- "Register by September 2027." That was the consultation plan. The bill sets 31 January of the year after nexus is first met. [S:275d5cc93a88, Chapter 6 item (1)(b)]; [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, s.50O(2)]
- "Amended CRS starts in 2029." Duties start on 1 January 2028; 2029 is the first amended-CRS exchange. [S:275d5cc93a88, para 4.18]; [S:e8c0a71f0cc5, Clause 1(4)]
- "CARF covers CBDCs and e-money." Those are excluded from CARF but brought into the amended CRS. [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 1.5, 4.3]
- "The HKMA or SFC supervises CARF." The IRD runs registration, returns and penalties. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Reporting portal' and 'Penalty provisions']

## Open items

- Passage of the CARF bill is not recorded in the corpus as of 25 September 2026. [S:d5097f447cf9, para 2]
- The consultation also proposed mandatory CRS registration for all RFIs from January 2027 and heavier CRS penalties. The bill's list of provisions in the corpus shows no RFI registration clause. Whether that proposal moved to other legislation is not stated. [S:275d5cc93a88, paras 5.1-5.2, Chapter 6 item (2)(a)]
- Whether the final bill keeps the proposed dual-reporting default is not stated on the IRD page.
- The IRD has not yet published the lists of reportable and partner jurisdictions, the portal or the data schema. [S:d70ae2cbc9d1, 'Lists of reportable jurisdictions' and 'Reporting portal']
- Fund/FIHV concessions: the 2026 Policy Address says a bill easing taxes on fund vehicles, single-family offices and carried-interest earners has already passed. The corpus has no bill text confirming how digital assets are treated in the enacted version. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 40]
- The corpus does not say whether tokenised deposits count as specified electronic money products for CRS.
