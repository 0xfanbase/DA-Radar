# C6 Financing, shared order books and client withdrawals

For a bank's Head of Compliance (digital assets) whose bank is a registered institution (RI). An RI is a bank registered with the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) to do securities business. This module suits RIs that offer, or plan to offer, virtual asset (VA) dealing. VA dealing means buying and selling virtual assets (VAs), such as bitcoin, for clients. The module explains what the SFC changed in February 2026. It shows how the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) applied the change to banks in May 2026. It also sets out which controls the regulators expect.

## In 60 seconds

An omnibus account holds many clients' assets together in one account at a trading platform. SFC-licensed firms are called licensed corporations (LCs). Under the omnibus-account terms and conditions, LCs and RIs that dealt in VAs for clients could not lend clients money to buy VAs. [S:e64b7b0267b2, Background, p.1] On 11 February 2026 the SFC changed this for VA brokers that already offer securities margin financing. Margin financing means lending clients money to buy assets, secured on their holdings. Those brokers can now lend to their securities margin clients to buy VAs. [S:73dac3a5a832, paras 3, 5] If a broker relies on VA collateral, the SFC expects only bitcoin and ether to count. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 7] The broker should apply a haircut (a cut to the value it counts) of 60% or more. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 7] On 27 May 2026 the HKMA let RIs offer VA financing if they observe similar standards. [S:e64b7b0267b2, p.2] The HKMA also accepts stablecoins from HKMA-licensed issuers as collateral. [S:e64b7b0267b2, items (4), (9)] It says loans secured only by VA must be treated as clean (unsecured) loans when the bank works out its capital. [S:e64b7b0267b2, items (4), (9)] The same circulars let brokers and RIs trade for clients on a shared order book. A shared order book lets a virtual asset trading platform (VATP) pool its orders with affiliated platforms abroad. [S:73dac3a5a832, paras 15-17]; [S:e64b7b0267b2, p.6] Retail clients get access only after a clear risk explanation and after they expressly opt in. [S:73dac3a5a832, paras 15-17]; [S:e64b7b0267b2, p.6] The circulars also set anti-fraud standards for firms that let clients withdraw VAs. [S:73dac3a5a832, paras 18-20] The SFC circular is addressed to LCs. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 1] The HKMA applies the standards to RIs. [S:e64b7b0267b2, pp.2, 6]

## Key facts

- Before the SFC's circular of 11 February 2026, LCs and RIs could not give clients credit to buy VA. [S:e64b7b0267b2, Background, p.1] The omnibus-account terms and conditions set this ban. [S:e64b7b0267b2, Background, p.1]
- The revised terms of 27 May 2026 keep the ban, with one exception. [S:31b4f67e0233, Part I para 4.3, p.10] A licensed corporation or RI may give such financing only if it does securities margin financing. [S:31b4f67e0233, Part I para 4.3, p.10] It also needs a Type 1 licence or registration (dealing in securities) with these terms attached as conditions. [S:31b4f67e0233, Part I para 4.3, p.10]
- The SFC calls VA financing a first step under Pillar P (Product) of ASPIRe — the SFC's roadmap for the VA market. [S:73dac3a5a832, paras 3-5] Only VA brokers that already provide securities margin financing can offer it. [S:73dac3a5a832, paras 3-5]
- The HKMA allows RIs that provide VA dealing to offer VA financing if they observe standards similar to the SFC's. [S:e64b7b0267b2, 'HKMA's guidance for RIs', p.2] The HKMA lists the standards for RIs as items (1)-(9). [S:e64b7b0267b2, 'HKMA's guidance for RIs', p.2]
- Only the RI's securities margin financing clients can borrow. [S:e64b7b0267b2, items (2)-(3), p.2] The HKMA does not expect an RI to raise a client's credit limit just because VA financing is available. [S:e64b7b0267b2, items (2)-(3), p.2]
- An RI may take a client's VA holdings into account when it judges the client's financial capability. If it does, its assessment should cover seven risk factors. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (3)(a)-(g), p.3] These include price-gap risk (sudden jumps in price), too much exposure to the digital-asset sector, and shares and digital assets falling together in a crisis. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (3)(a)-(g), p.3]
- An RI can choose to rely on VA collateral. If it does, only bitcoin, ether and stablecoins from HKMA-licensed issuers ('Relevant Stablecoins') should count as eligible collateral, with a prudent haircut. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (4), pp.3-4]
- The haircut for bitcoin and ether should be 60% or more. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (4) and footnote 5, p.4] The HKMA may revise the haircut after giving advance notice. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (4) and footnote 5, p.4] The HKMA sets no numeric haircut for Relevant Stablecoins. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (4) and footnote 5, p.4]
- The HKMA generally expects an RI to value VA collateral at prices on the VATP where the client's orders are carried out. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (6) and footnote 6, p.4] For Relevant Stablecoins, the RI may use the licensed issuer's prices instead. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (6) and footnote 6, p.4] RIs should keep a contingency plan for when the VATP goes down. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (6) and footnote 6, p.4]
- VA financing counts as part of the client's margin loan. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (7)(a)-(c), pp.4-5] Concentration controls (limits on holding too much of one kind of collateral) should cover VA collateral too. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (7)(a)-(c), pp.4-5] The RI should not repledge (pledge again to someone else) or reuse VA collateral. The only exception is enforcing it when a client defaults. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (7)(a)-(c), pp.4-5]
- Two cases call for action. One is when the loan is larger than the collateral value. The other is when a client has an unpaid margin call and a poor record of meeting margin calls. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (8), p.5] In either case the RI should stop new advances, stop VA purchases on margin, and review the client's credit and trading limits. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (8), p.5]
- The Banking (Capital) Rules do not recognise VA as collateral. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (9), p.5] So loans secured solely by VA must be treated as clean loans to the client. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (9), p.5]
- Until the SFC revises capital requirements, LCs follow the Securities and Futures (Financial Resources) Rules (FRR). [S:73dac3a5a832, para 14] The FRR apply a 100% haircut to VA collateral that LCs hold. In effect, that collateral counts for nothing in the capital sums. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 14]
- The HKMA circular does not cover tokenised products, because the financing ban does not apply to buying them. [S:e64b7b0267b2, footnote 4, p.2] For selling and distributing tokenised products, RIs should refer to HKMA guidance. One example is its circular of 20 February 2024 on tokenised products. [S:e64b7b0267b2, footnote 4, p.2]
- A shared order book lets an SFC-licensed VATP combine its order book (its list of buy and sell orders) with those of affiliated platforms abroad. [S:f009f1a6d615, paras 1, 3, 7] The overseas platform should be in a member jurisdiction of the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) or of a FATF-style regional body. [S:f009f1a6d615, paras 1, 3, 7] Its regulation should be substantially aligned with international standards. [S:f009f1a6d615, paras 1, 3, 7]
- A shared order book should accept only fully pre-funded orders, where the client has the cash or assets before trading. [S:f009f1a6d615, paras 10, 13, 15, 17] Delivery-versus-payment is central to settlement: both sides' assets change hands at the same time. [S:f009f1a6d615, paras 10, 13, 15, 17] The VATP is required to hold a client-compensation reserve on trust in Hong Kong. The reserve must be no smaller than the Unsettled Trade Limit (a set cap on trades not yet settled). [S:f009f1a6d615, paras 10, 13, 15, 17]
- Some RIs trade for clients (as their agent) on a shared order book and also give retail clients access to it. Such RIs should follow SFC circular 26EC5, paras 15-17. [S:e64b7b0267b2, 'Shared Order Book', p.6] Before trading, a broker should assess these points: [S:73dac3a5a832, paras 16-17]
  - conflicts of interest
  - settlement
  - default handling
  - reserve funds and insurance
  - client recourse (how clients can claim if something goes wrong).

  Retail access needs a prominent risk explanation and the client's express choice to take part. [S:73dac3a5a832, paras 16-17]
- Client VAs sometimes stay in custody (safekeeping) at the VATP. In that case, a broker's withdrawal function adds cyber and operational risk. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 19] If a broker's system is hacked, an impostor could send fake withdrawal instructions. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 19]
- SFC 26EC5 paras 18-20 also apply to RIs that let clients withdraw VAs. [S:e64b7b0267b2, 'Client VA withdrawals', p.6] They apply on top of HKMA requirements, such as those on e-banking and technology risk management. [S:e64b7b0267b2, 'Client VA withdrawals', p.6] The paragraphs expect: [S:73dac3a5a832, para 20]
  - robust authentication (checking who gives an instruction)
  - continuous monitoring
  - the power to suspend withdrawals inside and outside office hours
  - working with VATPs on withdrawal limits.
- SFC circular 26EC6 lets a VATP accept an affiliated market maker, subject to conditions. A market maker is a firm that keeps quoting prices to buy and sell. [S:84b3c1a0cee1, paras 7, 9-10] The conditions include: [S:84b3c1a0cee1, paras 7, 9-10]
  - the market maker works independently of the platform
  - client orders at the same price go first
  - the market maker's trades are identified after execution
  - disclosure.

  The VATP must notify the SFC first, with a report from an independent firm. [S:84b3c1a0cee1, paras 7, 9-10]

## What your bank must do

**Obligations**
- Offer VA financing only to securities margin financing clients. [S:e64b7b0267b2, items (1)-(2), p.2] Base it on the client's financial capability and the collateral the client posts. [S:e64b7b0267b2, items (1)-(2), p.2]
- Treat loans secured solely by VA as clean (unsecured) loans for capital. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (9), p.5]
- Do not repledge, reuse or pledge VA collateral as security for anything else. The only exception is enforcing it when a client defaults. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (7)(c), p.5]

**Controls & monitoring**
- If relying on VA collateral, write down in the margin lending policy how the bank assesses VA risks. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (5)(a), p.4] Monitor vulnerable VA exposures on an ongoing basis. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (5)(a), p.4]
- If relying on VA collateral, keep the ability to watch its price swings in real time and act promptly. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (5)(b), p.4]
- Set prudent triggers for margin calls and for stopping new advances. Act on them. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (8), p.5]
- Monitor withdrawals continuously. Be able to escalate and suspend withdrawals outside office hours too. [S:e64b7b0267b2, p.6]; [S:73dac3a5a832, para 20(b)]

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- The HKMA circular sets no separate notification step. For new digital-asset activity, the HKMA's existing expectation is that banks discuss plans with it before launch. [S:3ec5b269b5dc, letter body para 5, p.2]
- The circular names contacts for queries, by topic. [S:e64b7b0267b2, p.6]

**Counterparty due diligence**
- Before trading on a shared order book, assess these points about the platform operators: [S:e64b7b0267b2, p.6]; [S:73dac3a5a832, para 16]
  - conflicts of interest
  - the settlement mechanism
  - failure and default processes
  - reserve fund and insurance
  - client recourse.
- Work with the partner VATP as needed on withdrawal limits and on blocking suspicious withdrawals. [S:e64b7b0267b2, p.6]; [S:73dac3a5a832, para 20(c)]
- Keep a contingency plan for outages at the VATP whose prices the bank uses to value collateral. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (6), p.4]

## Talking points

**To the Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)**
- Under the HKMA's circular of 27 May 2026, our bank, as an RI, can offer VA margin financing. The condition is that we observe standards similar to the SFC's. [S:e64b7b0267b2, items (1)-(4), pp.2-4] If we rely on VA collateral, it should be only bitcoin, ether or stablecoins from HKMA-licensed issuers. [S:e64b7b0267b2, items (1)-(4), pp.2-4]
- The withdrawal and shared-order-book standards come from SFC circular 26EC5, paras 15-20. The HKMA applies them to RIs. [S:e64b7b0267b2, p.6]

**To business heads**
- Only securities margin financing clients qualify. Their limits are not expected to rise just because VA financing exists. [S:e64b7b0267b2, items (2)-(3), p.2]
- Retail clients can use a shared order book only after a prominent explanation of the overseas risks and their express opt-in. [S:e64b7b0267b2, p.6]; [S:73dac3a5a832, para 17]

**To the Chief Executive Officer (CEO)**
- The SFC calls this a first step to bring margin trading into VA. The aim is more liquidity in a risk-controlled setting. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 3]
- The Banking (Capital) Rules give VA collateral no recognition. So loans secured solely by VA are treated as clean loans. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (9), p.5]

**To Risk / Chief Risk Officer (CRO)**
- Bitcoin and ether collateral should get a haircut of 60% or more. For stablecoins, the HKMA asks only for a prudent haircut. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (4), footnote 5, pp.3-4]
- The HKMA notes that VAs have a short track record in major market-wide shocks. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (3), closing paragraph, p.3] Even the most traded VA has fallen sharply within a day and over several days. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (3), closing paragraph, p.3]

## Common mix-ups

- **Myth:** The SFC circular binds banks directly. **Fact:** It is addressed to LCs. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 1] The HKMA's circular of 27 May 2026 brings in banks, as RIs. [S:e64b7b0267b2, p.2]
- **Myth:** Bank collateral rules are the same as the SFC's. **Fact:** The HKMA adds Relevant Stablecoins as eligible collateral. [S:e64b7b0267b2, items (4), (9)] Banks treat VA-only loans as clean loans under the Banking (Capital) Rules. [S:e64b7b0267b2, items (4), (9)] LCs instead apply the FRR 100% haircut. [S:73dac3a5a832, paras 7, 14]
- **Myth:** The financing ban covered tokenised products. **Fact:** The HKMA says the ban does not apply to buying tokenised products. [S:e64b7b0267b2, footnote 4, p.2]
- **Myth:** A shared order book can link to any overseas exchange. **Fact:** As a first step, it is limited to platforms in the same group. [S:f009f1a6d615, para 3]

## Open items

- Paragraph numbers differ. SFC 26EC5 cites the financing ban as para 4.4 of the omnibus terms and conditions. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 2] The HKMA cites para 4.3 'as renumbered on 30 September 2025'. [S:e64b7b0267b2, p.1 footnote 3] The revised terms of 27 May 2026 also number it 4.3. [S:31b4f67e0233, Part I para 4.3, p.10]
- Dates differ. The HKMA's footnote 4 dates the joint circular 23 December 2023. [S:e64b7b0267b2, footnote 4] The SFC's version gives 22 December 2023. [S:8a016a912528, p.1] So does the HKMA's own stablecoin circular of 27 May 2026. [S:a0bd48559762, p.1]
- The SFC has not published a date for its consultation on capital requirements for VA activities. [S:73dac3a5a832, para 14]
- The revised terms and conditions are Appendices A1 and A2 to the HKMA's stablecoin circular of 27 May 2026. [S:553c7d718bec, landing page] This module reviewed only para 4.3 of Appendix A1. [S:553c7d718bec, landing page]
- The HKMA specifies no numeric haircut for Relevant Stablecoin collateral. [S:e64b7b0267b2, item (4) and footnote 5, p.4]
