# D4 Cyber, fraud and technology risk

This module is for the Head of Compliance (digital assets) at a Hong Kong bank. After it, you can:
- Name the technology-risk expectations the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) sets for the bank.
- Tell them apart from the rules the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) sets for the bank's platform and broker counterparties.
- Name the dated SFC deadlines.
- Describe the scam patterns that regulators have flagged.

## In 60 seconds

For a bank, the technology-risk texts on digital assets come from the HKMA. [S:1884530ff235, Annex; S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10–11] The first is an April 2024 list of risk points for authorized institutions (AIs). AIs are banks and other deposit-takers that the HKMA authorizes. [S:1884530ff235, Annex] It covers AIs that use distributed ledger technology (DLT), the shared record-keeping technology behind blockchains. [S:1884530ff235, Annex] The second is the HKMA custody standards. In May 2026 the HKMA added detailed controls for wallets and for managing the secret keys that control digital assets. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10–11]

The SFC has a parallel, more detailed set of rules. It covers licensed virtual asset trading platform (VATP) operators and internet brokers. Four SFC circulars stand out:
- An August 2025 custody circular, prompted by incidents at overseas platforms. [S:562c0f8addce, p.1]
- A January 2025 circular on what SFC inspections found. [S:0c93d9a066e6, p.1]
- A June 2026 circular on cyberattacks that use artificial intelligence. [S:a4929c8f9f3e, para 1]
- A July 2026 circular on client login. It expects firms to stop relying on one-time passwords (OTPs) when clients log in or bind (register) a device. [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 7, 21(c)] Firms are to move to phishing-resistant methods by 8 July 2027. [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 7, 21(c)] Phishing means fake messages or websites that trick people into giving away login details. Phishing-resistant methods are logins that a fake website is unable to capture and reuse.

These SFC circulars are addressed to SFC-licensed firms, not to the bank. [S:724f4d9f786e, para 1] But they show what the bank's VATP and broker counterparties are expected to have. [S:724f4d9f786e, para 1] Also, many controls in the HKMA's 2026 custody update use the same wording as the SFC's 2025 custody circular. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(c), (f), (i); S:562c0f8addce, paras 5, 14, 28]

On fraud, the HKMA has warned the public about three things:
- Crypto firms that call themselves 'banks'. [S:19c8611c621a, para 1]
- Tokens falsely linked to licensed stablecoin issuers. [S:275e7319a841, para 2]
- Entities that falsely claim to be part of its e-HKD (digital Hong Kong dollar) Pilot Programme. [S:5f67b02549b0, para 1]

## Key facts

- The HKMA says an AI's board and senior management carry full responsibility for using DLT and for its risks. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 1] The HKMA also says the AI should update its policies on technology risk, business continuity and outsourcing for DLT as needed. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 1]
- The HKMA expects banks to understand the types of DLT network and pick one that suits the application's risks. [S:1884530ff235, Annex items 2, 5] Anyone can join a permissionless network. The HKMA sees such networks as likely unsuitable for highly critical or sensitive functions. [S:1884530ff235, Annex items 2, 5] The exception is where adequate compensating controls (extra safeguards that offset the risk) exist. [S:1884530ff235, Annex items 2, 5]
- A bank acting as custodian (holding assets for clients) would generally be expected to have stricter procedures for private keys. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 8] A private key is the secret code that controls a digital asset. The procedures cover creating, storing and backing up keys securely. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 8] Examples include limited access, cold storage (keys kept offline) and offsite backups. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 8]
- The HKMA's May 2026 custody update includes three controls: [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10, 11(c), 11(i)]
  - System-based whitelists (lists of pre-approved addresses) for transfers out of cold wallets. A cold wallet is one whose keys are kept offline.
  - No smart contracts in cold wallets on public blockchains. A smart contract is code on a blockchain that runs by itself.
  - Measures to prevent 'blind signing', which means approving a transaction without seeing its details.
- Keys and seeds should be created and stored in tamper-resistant devices, such as hardware security modules (HSMs). [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a)–(b)] A seed is a master secret from which keys can be rebuilt. HSMs are sealed devices built to protect keys. Keys and seeds should also be created, stored and backed up in Hong Kong. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a)–(b)]
- Where the assets in custody are virtual assets (VAs), a bank custodian should hold 98% of client VAs in cold storage. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(l), fn 8]
- Wallet software code must be managed with strictly enforced segregation of duties, so no single person controls the whole process. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(f)] Administrator access should follow least privilege, meaning staff get only the access their job needs. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(f)] The segregation line is the annex's only 'must'. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(f)]
- Custody security monitoring should run 24/7, including holidays. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 23–24] It should also cover outside dependencies, such as vendors, blockchain protocols and encryption algorithms. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 23–24]
- The SFC's August 2025 custody circular took effect immediately for VATP operators. [S:562c0f8addce, p.1; 'Way forward', p.7] The SFC says overseas incidents showed potential critical weaknesses, whatever custody solution was used. [S:562c0f8addce, p.1; 'Way forward', p.7] Examples of such solutions are HSMs, multi-signature wallets and multi-party computation (MPC). A multi-signature wallet needs several approvers to sign a transfer. MPC splits a key into shares held by different parties or devices.
- The SFC inspected platforms that were treated as licensed while their applications were pending. [S:0c93d9a066e6, Appendix 2 paras 6, 18, 22] It found four problems: [S:0c93d9a066e6, Appendix 2 paras 6, 18, 22]
  - Keys for one hot wallet (a wallet connected to the internet) were kept partly outside Hong Kong.
  - One cold wallet was built as a multi-signature smart contract.
  - Some encryption algorithms were outdated.
  - Hot-wallet calculations under the 98/2 rule had errors. The rule keeps 98% of client assets in cold storage and at most 2% in hot wallets.
- The SFC's June 2026 circular reminds firms that senior managers bear ultimate responsibility for cybersecurity risk. [S:a4929c8f9f3e, paras 3, 9–15] These managers include the Manager-in-Charge of information technology. [S:a4929c8f9f3e, paras 3, 9–15] The circular asks firms to review five areas: [S:a4929c8f9f3e, paras 3, 9–15]
  - patching (installing security fixes);
  - access controls;
  - detection of attacks;
  - supply chain;
  - incident response.
- The Hong Kong Computer Emergency Response Team Coordination Centre (HKCERT) recorded 15,877 cybersecurity incidents in 2025. [S:a4929c8f9f3e, fn 1] That was up from 12,536 in 2024, as cited by the SFC. [S:a4929c8f9f3e, fn 1]
- The SFC does not consider OTPs sent by text message (SMS) or email to be phishing-resistant. [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 7–8, 21(c)] Internet brokers and VASPs are expected to adopt robust login methods no later than 8 July 2027. [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 7–8, 21(c)] Examples include passkeys and bound devices. [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 7–8, 21(c)] A passkey is a password-free login stored on the client's device. A bound device is a phone or computer linked to the client's account. Large internet brokers are expected to act immediately. [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 7–8, 21(c)]
- This point is about large-scale unauthorised transactions through client accounts after hacking incidents. [S:724f4d9f786e, para 25] If a firm lacks adequate measures to prevent, detect and halt them, the SFC will hold it accountable for clients' losses. [S:724f4d9f786e, para 25]
- In February 2025 the SFC only strongly urged licensed corporations to drop OTPs for login and device binding. [S:4ce7d53d6956, Note 4] The July 2026 circular goes further. [S:4ce7d53d6956, Note 4]
- Under the Banking Ordinance, only AIs may take deposits. [S:fcbcbb5d9d99, para 3, Notes 1–2] A business that is not a bank commits an offence if it uses the word 'bank' in its business name. [S:fcbcbb5d9d99, para 3, Notes 1–2] The exception is where the Monetary Authority has given written consent (section 97). [S:fcbcbb5d9d99, para 3, Notes 1–2]
- In April 2026 the HKMA warned about tokens with the tickers (trading symbols) 'HKDAP' and 'HSBC'. [S:275e7319a841, paras 2–3] These tokens were not issued by, or linked to, licensed stablecoin issuers. [S:275e7319a841, paras 2–3] Both licensed issuers had confirmed they had not issued any regulated stablecoins. [S:275e7319a841, paras 2–3]
- An HKMA presentation of 25 September 2026 gives the sector's first Quantum Preparedness Index score as 2.3 out of 10. [S:a50e9dc16dc7, slide 11] The index tracks how ready the sector is for risks from quantum computing. The objective is full readiness across the sector by 2030. [S:a50e9dc16dc7, slide 11]

## What your bank must do

**Obligations**
- Keep the board and senior management in charge of DLT risk. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 1] Update technology-risk, continuity and outsourcing policies. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 1]
- For custody, run a full risk assessment before launch. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 1] Make sure the board and senior management oversee the activity before and during it. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 1]
- For client VAs, meet the key-management controls in para 11 of the custody annex: [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11, fn 8]
  - use HSMs;
  - keep keys in Hong Kong;
  - keep smart contracts out of cold wallets on public blockchains;
  - prevent blind signing;
  - hold 98% in cold storage.

**Controls & monitoring**
- Use system controls and whitelists to stop unauthorised transfers out of cold wallets. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 10] Check each transaction through a systematic process. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 10]
- Run security monitoring 24/7. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 23] Test system changes thoroughly before they go live. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 23]
- Add DLT-specific scenarios to continuity plans for critical functions. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 10] Examples are cyberattacks, loss or theft of keys, and forks (a split of a blockchain into two versions). [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 10]
- An HKMA speech says banks should secure their own cyber basics and those of critical service providers. [S:a50e9dc16dc7, slide 10] It also says the risks of frontier artificial intelligence (the most advanced models) should not be underestimated. [S:a50e9dc16dc7, slide 10]

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- Discuss virtual asset activity with the HKMA before launch, and get its feedback on risk controls. [S:642680fa4467, p.5]
- SFC-licensed firms in the bank's group should promptly report material cyber incidents to the SFC. [S:a4929c8f9f3e, para 18]
- Internet brokers or VASPs should tell their SFC case officer at once if they expect trouble meeting the July 2027 login deadline. [S:724f4d9f786e, para 23]

**Counterparty due diligence**
- Before relying on a DLT network, check that its node operators are trustworthy, reliable and diverse. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 5] Node operators are the parties that run the computers that keep the network going.
- For custody delegates (firms the bank hands custody to), check for: [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 15]
  - independent code reviews;
  - an understanding of how software releases are made;
  - regular evaluation of security controls and disaster-recovery testing.
- A bank may delegate custody of VAs to an SFC-licensed VATP operator after due diligence. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 14–15] The SFC's own custody standards for VATP operators show what such a platform is expected to have. [S:562c0f8addce, paras 2, 7, 10, 18, 24] They include: [S:562c0f8addce, paras 2, 7, 10, 18, 24]
  - HSMs;
  - air-gapped signing devices (devices never connected to a network);
  - a security operations centre (a team that watches for attacks);
  - 24/7 monitoring.

## Common mix-ups

- **Myth:** "MPC or multi-signature makes custody safe." **Fact:** The SFC says overseas incidents pointed to potential critical weaknesses, whatever the custody solution. [S:562c0f8addce, p.1]
- **Myth:** "The 50%/100% compensation cover applies to bank custodians." **Fact:** That cover is an SFC requirement for VATP operators. [S:0c93d9a066e6, Appendix 2 para 33] It covers potential loss of 50% of client VAs in cold wallets and 100% in hot wallets (VATP Guidelines para 10.22). [S:0c93d9a066e6, Appendix 2 para 33] The 2026 HKMA annex takes a different approach. It makes the bank liable for losses from incidents attributable to the bank. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)] It also requires adequate financial resources, which may include insurance. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]
- **Myth:** "OTPs are fine for client login." **Fact:** Not for SFC-licensed internet brokers and VASPs. They are expected to stop using OTPs for login and device binding by 8 July 2027. [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 7, 21(c)]
- **Myth:** "The HKMA DLT considerations are hard rules." **Fact:** The HKMA calls them non-binding and not a complete list. It discusses detailed expectations with each bank one to one. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, Introduction]
- **Myth:** "A licensed issuer's name on a token means it is regulated." **Fact:** The HKMA warned these tokens were not issued by, or linked to, the licensed issuers. [S:275e7319a841, paras 2–3]

## Read these first

1. **Updated Guidance on Expected Standards on Provision of Custodial Services for Digital Assets by AIs** — HKMA, 27 May 2026. Read paras 10–11, 14–15, 23–25. Why: it sets the bank's own baseline for key and wallet controls. https://brdr.hkma.gov.hk/eng/doc-ldg/docId/getPdf/20260527-7-EN/20260527-7-EN.pdf
2. **Risk management considerations related to the use of DLT** — HKMA, 16 Apr 2024. Read all ten items. Why: it shows how the HKMA reviews bank DLT projects. https://brdr.hkma.gov.hk/eng/doc-ldg/docId/getPdf/20240416-2-EN/20240416-2-EN.pdf
3. **Circular 26EC35 on robust authentication and surveillance** — SFC, 9 Jul 2026. Read paras 5–13, 21–25. Why: it sets the counterparty deadline and the line on liability for losses. https://apps.sfc.hk/edistributionWeb/api/circular/openFile?lang=EN&refNo=26EC35

## Open items

- The documents reviewed for this guide include no HKMA circular on phishing-resistant authentication for banks. The 2026 Policy Address says the HKMA is driving the use of 'iAM Smart' step-up authentication in banks. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 51(i)] The iAM Smart app is the government's digital identity tool. Step-up authentication means an extra identity check for riskier actions. First-phase testing on remote account opening is under way. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 51(i)]
- The SFC cybersecurity circular of 6 February 2025 is referred to, but it is not among the documents reviewed for this guide. [S:4ce7d53d6956, Note 4]
- Two items are outside this module. One is the staking guidance that the HKMA custody annex cross-refers to. Staking means locking up tokens to help run a blockchain in return for rewards. The other is the SFC's November 2024 circular on generative artificial intelligence.
