# D4 Cyber, fraud and technology risk

For the Head of Compliance (digital assets) at a Hong Kong bank: after this module you can separate the technology-risk rules that bind the bank (HKMA) from the SFC rules that bind its platform and broker counterparties, name the dated SFC deadlines, and describe the scam patterns regulators have flagged.

## In 60 seconds

For a bank, the technology-risk texts for digital assets are HKMA ones: the April 2024 list of risk considerations for distributed ledger technology (DLT) and the custody standards, updated in May 2026 with detailed wallet and key-management controls. The SFC has built a parallel, more prescriptive set for licensed virtual asset trading platforms (VATPs) and brokers: the August 2025 custody circular after overseas hacks, a January 2025 inspection report, a June 2026 circular on AI-enabled cyberattacks, and a July 2026 mandate to replace SMS and email one-time passwords (OTPs) with phishing-resistant login by 8 July 2027. Those SFC circulars do not bind the bank as such, but they describe what the bank's VATP and broker counterparties must now have, and the HKMA's 2026 custody update mirrors many of them. On fraud, the HKMA has repeatedly warned about crypto firms calling themselves 'banks', fake tokens trading on licensed stablecoin issuers' names, and false claims of links to HKMA pilots.

## Key facts

- The HKMA says an AI's board and senior management carry full responsibility for DLT adoption and its risks, and should update technology-risk, continuity and outsourcing policies for DLT. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 1]
- The HKMA expects banks to justify their choice of DLT network type and generally avoid permissionless networks for highly critical functions unless compensating controls exist. [S:1884530ff235, Annex items 2, 5]
- Banks acting as custodian should apply more rigorous private-key procedures covering generation, storage, backup, access and cold storage. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 8]
- The May 2026 HKMA custody update adds system-enforced whitelists for cold-wallet transfers, no smart contracts in cold wallets on public chains, and blocks on 'blind signing'. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10, 11(c), 11(i)]
- Keys and seeds should be generated and held in tamper-resistant devices such as hardware security modules (HSMs), and generated, stored and backed up in Hong Kong. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a)–(b)]
- At least 98% of client VAs held by a bank custodian should be in offline cold storage. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(l), fn 8]
- Wallet code needs strict segregation of duties and least-privilege admin access — one of the few 'must' lines in the annex. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(f)]
- Custody monitoring should run 24/7 including holidays and track vendor, protocol and cryptographic dependencies. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 23–25]
- The SFC's August 2025 custody circular took immediate effect for VATPs and says weaknesses persist whatever custody technology is used. [S:562c0f8addce, p.2; 'Way forward', p.7]
- SFC inspections found keys held outside Hong Kong, a cold wallet using a multi-signature smart contract, weak encryption, and hot-wallet miscalculations against the 98/2 rule. [S:0c93d9a066e6, Appendix 2 paras 5–6, 17–22]
- The SFC's AI-cyberattack circular holds senior management, together with the Manager-in-Charge of IT, ultimately accountable, and asks firms to review patching, access, detection, supply chain and incident response. [S:a4929c8f9f3e, paras 3, 6–15]
- HKCERT recorded 15,877 cybersecurity incidents in Hong Kong for 2025, up from 12,536 the year before. [S:a4929c8f9f3e, fn 1]
- The SFC no longer treats SMS or email OTP as phishing-resistant; brokers and VASPs must adopt passkeys or robust device binding by 8 July 2027, and large internet brokers immediately. [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 7, 21(c)]
- If a firm lacks adequate controls to stop large-scale unauthorised transactions through client accounts following a hacking incident, the SFC will hold it accountable for clients' losses. [S:724f4d9f786e, para 25]
- The OTP requirement grew out of a February 2025 SFC circular that only encouraged firms to stop using OTPs. [S:4ce7d53d6956, Notes]
- Only AIs may use the word 'bank' or take deposits; using it without HKMA consent is an offence under the Banking Ordinance. [S:fcbcbb5d9d99, para 3, note 1]
- In April 2026 the HKMA warned that tokens with the tickers 'HKDAP' and 'HSBC' were not issued by the two licensed stablecoin issuers, neither of which had issued a regulated stablecoin. [S:275e7319a841, paras 2–3]
- The HKMA's first Quantum Preparedness Index score for the sector is 2.3 out of 10, against an objective of full readiness by 2030. [S:a50e9dc16dc7, slide 11]

## What your bank must do

**Obligations**
- Keep board and senior management ownership of DLT risk and update technology-risk, continuity and outsourcing policies. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 1]
- For custody: run a full risk assessment before launch, with board oversight before and after. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 1]
- Meet the key-management controls in para 11 (HSMs, Hong Kong keys, no smart contracts in cold wallets, anti-blind-signing, 98% cold). [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11]

**Controls & monitoring**
- Enforce cold-wallet whitelists by system and verify each transaction. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 10]
- Run 24/7 security monitoring, and test all changes before deployment. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 23–24]
- Build DLT-specific scenarios (attacks, key loss, forks) into continuity plans for critical functions. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 10]
- Secure the bank's and critical service providers' cyber fundamentals; frontier AI cyber risks should not be underestimated. [S:a50e9dc16dc7, slide 10]

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- Discuss VA activity with the HKMA before launch. [S:642680fa4467, p.5]
- Where the bank's group has SFC-licensed entities: report material cyber incidents to the SFC promptly, and notify the case officer at once if the July 2027 authentication deadline cannot be met. [S:a4929c8f9f3e, para 18; S:724f4d9f786e, para 23]

**Counterparty due diligence**
- Assess node operators' trustworthiness and diversity before relying on a DLT network. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 5]
- For custody delegates: independent code review, understanding of release processes, and periodic security and recovery testing. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 15]
- When relying on a VATP, check it meets SFC custody standards (HSMs, air-gapped signing, 24/7 security operations centre). [S:562c0f8addce, Sections II–V]

## Talking points

**To the CCO**
- The SFC OTP ban binds brokers and platforms, not the bank directly; our own standard is the HKMA custody annex. [S:724f4d9f786e, para 1; S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11]
- The HKMA 2026 custody text now mirrors the SFC's 2025 wallet controls. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10–11; S:562c0f8addce, Section III]

**To business heads**
- Custody keys stay in Hong Kong in certified hardware; smart contracts are out for cold wallets. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a)–(c)]
- Counterparty platforms must switch clients off SMS OTP login by 8 July 2027. [S:724f4d9f786e, para 21(c)]

**To the CEO**
- Accountability sits with the board and senior management, not the vendor or the technology choice. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 1]
- Phishing made up 57% of reported incidents in 2025, per the SFC. [S:724f4d9f786e, fn 5]

**To Risk / CRO**

- Fake 'bank' branding and look-alike stablecoin tokens are live impersonation risks to our name and customers. [S:19c8611c621a, para 2; S:275e7319a841, para 2]

## Common mix-ups

- 'Multi-party computation or multi-signature makes custody safe.' The SFC says weaknesses persist regardless of the custody solution. [S:562c0f8addce, p.2]
- 'The 50%/100% compensation cover applies to bank custodians.' That is the SFC VATP rule (compensation for 50% of cold-wallet and 100% of hot-wallet losses under VATP Guidelines para 10.22); the 2026 HKMA annex instead makes the bank liable for losses it causes and requires adequate financial resources. [S:0c93d9a066e6, Appendix 1 para 33; S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]
- 'OTP is fine as a second factor.' Not for SFC-licensed brokers and VASPs from July 2027. [S:724f4d9f786e, para 7]
- 'The HKMA DLT considerations are hard rules.' They are non-binding supervisory considerations, without deadlines. [S:1884530ff235, Annex]
- 'A licensed issuer's name on a token means it is regulated.' The HKMA warned this is false. [S:275e7319a841, paras 2–3]
