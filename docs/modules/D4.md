# D4 Cyber, fraud and technology risk

For the Head of Compliance (digital assets) at a Hong Kong bank: after this module you can separate the technology-risk expectations the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) sets for the bank from the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) rules for its platform and broker counterparties, name the dated SFC deadlines, and describe the scam patterns regulators have flagged.

## In 60 seconds

For a bank, the technology-risk texts for digital assets are HKMA ones: the April 2024 list of risk considerations for authorized institutions (AIs) using distributed ledger technology (DLT), and the custody standards, updated in May 2026 with detailed wallet and key-management controls. [S:1884530ff235, Annex; S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10–11] The SFC has a parallel, more prescriptive set for licensed virtual asset trading platforms (VATPs) and internet brokers: an August 2025 custody circular prompted by overseas platform incidents, a January 2025 circular on inspection findings, a June 2026 circular on cyberattacks enabled by artificial intelligence, and a July 2026 circular expecting firms to stop relying on one-time passwords (OTPs) when clients log in or bind devices, moving to phishing-resistant methods by 8 July 2027. [S:562c0f8addce, p.1; S:0c93d9a066e6, p.1; S:a4929c8f9f3e, para 1; S:724f4d9f786e, paras 7, 21(c)] Those SFC circulars are addressed to SFC-licensed firms, not to the bank, but they describe what the bank's VATP and broker counterparties are expected to have, and many controls in the HKMA's 2026 custody update use the same wording as the SFC's 2025 custody circular. [S:724f4d9f786e, para 1; S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(c), (f), (i); S:562c0f8addce, paras 5, 14, 28] On fraud, the HKMA has warned the public about crypto firms calling themselves 'banks', tokens falsely linked to licensed stablecoin issuers, and entities falsely claiming to be part of its e-HKD Pilot Programme. [S:19c8611c621a, para 1; S:275e7319a841, para 2; S:5f67b02549b0, para 1]

## Key facts

- The HKMA says an AI's board and senior management carry full responsibility for DLT adoption and its risks, and that the AI should update its technology-risk, continuity and outsourcing policies for DLT as needed. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 1]
- The HKMA expects banks to understand the types of DLT network and pick one suited to the application's risks; it sees permissionless networks as likely inappropriate for highly critical or sensitive functions unless adequate compensating controls exist. [S:1884530ff235, Annex items 2, 5]
- A bank acting as custodian would generally be expected to have more rigorous private-key procedures, covering secure generation, storage and backup, with measures such as limited access, cold storage and offsite backups. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 8]
- The May 2026 HKMA custody update includes system-based whitelists for cold-wallet transfers, no smart contracts in cold wallets on public blockchains, and measures to prevent 'blind signing'. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10, 11(c), 11(i)]
- Keys and seeds should be generated and stored in tamper-resistant devices such as hardware security modules (HSMs), and generated, stored and backed up in Hong Kong. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a)–(b)]
- Where the assets in custody are virtual assets (VAs), a bank custodian should hold 98% of client VAs in cold storage. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(l), fn 8]
- Wallet code management needs strictly enforced segregation of duties, and administrator access should follow least privilege; the segregation line is the annex's only 'must'. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(f)]
- Custody security monitoring should run 24/7 including holidays and cover dependencies such as vendors, blockchain protocols and encryption algorithms. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 23–24]
- The SFC's August 2025 custody circular took immediate effect for VATPs; it says overseas incidents showed potential critical weaknesses whatever custody solution was used, such as HSMs, multi-signature wallets or multi-party computation (MPC). [S:562c0f8addce, p.1; 'Way forward', p.7]
- SFC inspections of deemed VATP applicants found one hot wallet's keys kept partly outside Hong Kong, a cold wallet built as a multi-signature smart contract, outdated encryption algorithms, and errors in hot-wallet calculations under the 98/2 rule. [S:0c93d9a066e6, Appendix 2 paras 6, 18, 22]
- The SFC's June 2026 circular reminds firms that senior managers, their Manager-in-Charge for information technology (MIC-IT) among them, bear ultimate responsibility for cybersecurity risk, and asks firms to review patching, access controls, detection, supply chain and incident response. [S:a4929c8f9f3e, paras 3, 9–15]
- HKCERT, Hong Kong's computer emergency response coordination centre, recorded 15,877 cybersecurity incidents in 2025, up from 12,536 in 2024, as cited by the SFC. [S:a4929c8f9f3e, fn 1]
- The SFC does not consider SMS or email OTPs phishing-resistant; internet brokers and VASPs are expected to adopt robust methods (examples include passkeys and bound devices) no later than 8 July 2027, and large internet brokers immediately. [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 7–8, 21(c)]
- If a firm lacks adequate measures to prevent, detect and halt large-scale unauthorised transactions through client accounts after hacking incidents, the SFC will hold it accountable for clients' losses. [S:724f4d9f786e, para 25]
- In February 2025 the SFC had only strongly urged licensed corporations to drop OTPs for login and device binding; the July 2026 circular goes further. [S:4ce7d53d6956, Note 4]
- Under the Banking Ordinance only AIs may take deposits, and anyone other than a bank using the word 'bank' in its business name without the Monetary Authority's written consent commits an offence (section 97). [S:fcbcbb5d9d99, para 3, Notes 1–2]
- In April 2026 the HKMA warned that tokens with the tickers 'HKDAP' and 'HSBC' were not issued by or associated with licensed stablecoin issuers; both licensed issuers had confirmed they had not issued any regulated stablecoins. [S:275e7319a841, paras 2–3]
- An HKMA presentation of 25 September 2026 gives the sector's first Quantum Preparedness Index score as 2.3 out of 10, with an objective of full sectoral readiness by 2030. [S:a50e9dc16dc7, slide 11]

## What your bank must do

**Obligations**
- Keep board and senior management ownership of DLT risk and update technology-risk, continuity and outsourcing policies. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 1]
- For custody: run a comprehensive risk assessment before launch, with board and senior management oversight before and during the activity. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 1]
- For client VAs, meet the key-management controls in para 11 (HSMs, keys in Hong Kong, no smart contracts in cold wallets on public blockchains, anti-blind-signing, 98% cold storage). [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11, fn 8]

**Controls & monitoring**
- Use system controls and whitelists to stop unauthorised cold-wallet transfers, and verify each transaction systematically. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 10]
- Run 24/7 security monitoring, and test system changes thoroughly before deployment. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 23]
- Build DLT-specific scenarios (cyberattacks, key loss or theft, forks) into continuity plans for critical functions. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 10]
- An HKMA speech says banks should secure their own and critical service providers' cyber fundamentals, and that frontier artificial intelligence risks should not be underestimated. [S:a50e9dc16dc7, slide 10]

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- Discuss VA activity with the HKMA, and get its feedback on risk controls, before launch. [S:642680fa4467, p.5]
- SFC-licensed firms in the bank's group should promptly report material cyber incidents to the SFC, and internet brokers or VASPs should notify their case officer at once if they expect difficulty meeting the July 2027 authentication deadline. [S:a4929c8f9f3e, para 18; S:724f4d9f786e, para 23]

**Counterparty due diligence**
- Assess whether node operators are trustworthy, reliable and diverse before relying on a DLT network. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 5]
- For custody delegates: independent code reviews, understanding of release processes, and regular evaluation of security controls and disaster-recovery testing. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 15]
- A bank may delegate VA custody to an SFC-licensed VATP after due diligence; the SFC's own VATP custody standards (HSMs, air-gapped signing devices, a security operations centre, 24/7 monitoring) show what such a platform is expected to have. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 14–15; S:562c0f8addce, paras 2, 7, 10, 18, 24]

## Talking points

**To the chief compliance officer (CCO)**
- The SFC OTP circular is addressed to internet brokers and VASPs, not the bank; our own custody baseline is the HKMA custody annex. [S:724f4d9f786e, para 1; S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11]
- Several wallet controls in the HKMA 2026 custody annex use the same wording as the SFC's 2025 custody circular. [S:ce05e67bcdce, paras 10–11; S:562c0f8addce, Sections II–III]

**To business heads**
- Custody keys are to be kept in Hong Kong in tamper-resistant devices such as HSMs; smart contracts are out for cold wallets on public blockchains. [S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(a)–(c)]
- Counterparty platforms and internet brokers are expected to move client login and device binding off OTPs by 8 July 2027. [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 7, 21(c)]

**To the CEO**
- The HKMA places responsibility for DLT adoption and its risks with the board and senior management. [S:1884530ff235, Annex item 1]
- Phishing made up 57% of security incidents reported to HKCERT in 2025, as cited by the SFC. [S:724f4d9f786e, fn 5]

**To Risk / chief risk officer (CRO)**
- The HKMA's first quantum readiness score for the sector is 2.3 of 10, with 2030 as the objective for full readiness. [S:a50e9dc16dc7, slide 11]
- The HKMA has warned about crypto firms presenting themselves as 'banks' and about tokens falsely linked to licensed stablecoin issuers. [S:19c8611c621a, para 2; S:275e7319a841, para 2]

## Common mix-ups

- 'MPC or multi-signature makes custody safe.' The SFC says overseas incidents pointed to potential critical weaknesses whatever the custody solution. [S:562c0f8addce, p.1]
- 'The 50%/100% compensation cover applies to bank custodians.' That is the SFC VATP requirement (cover for potential loss of 50% of client VAs in cold wallets and 100% in hot wallets, under VATP Guidelines para 10.22); the 2026 HKMA annex instead makes the bank liable for losses attributable to it and requires adequate financial resources, which may include insurance. [S:0c93d9a066e6, Appendix 2 para 33; S:ce05e67bcdce, para 11(n)]
- 'OTP is fine for client login.' Not for SFC-licensed internet brokers and VASPs, which are expected to stop using it for login and device binding by 8 July 2027. [S:724f4d9f786e, paras 7, 21(c)]
- 'The HKMA DLT considerations are hard rules.' The HKMA calls them non-binding and non-exhaustive, with detailed expectations discussed bilaterally. [S:1884530ff235, Annex, Introduction]
- 'A licensed issuer's name on a token means it is regulated.' The HKMA warned that such tokens were not issued by or associated with the licensed issuers. [S:275e7319a841, paras 2–3]

## Open items

- No HKMA circular on phishing-resistant authentication for banks is in the documents reviewed for this guide; the 2026 Policy Address says the HKMA is driving adoption of 'iAM Smart' step-up authentication in banks, with first-phase testing on remote account opening under way. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 51(i)]
- The SFC cybersecurity circular of 6 February 2025 is referred to but not itself in the documents reviewed for this guide. [S:4ce7d53d6956, Note 4]
