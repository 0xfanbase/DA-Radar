# D7 Sanctions, wallet screening, blockchain analytics and fraud

This module is for the Head of Compliance (digital assets) at a Hong Kong bank. After it, you can explain how Hong Kong sanctions law reaches the bank. You can also say what regulators expect when crypto moves in or out, and where scam money meets crypto platforms.

## In 60 seconds

Hong Kong puts most United Nations (UN) sanctions into law under the United Nations Sanctions Ordinance (UNSO), Cap. 537. [S:7bfdad704174, para 1.26; Glossary] Under the UNSO regulations, making funds available to a designated person without a licence is an offence. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.7] The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) expects every bank to keep a sanctions database and screen customers against it. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 6.13–6.16] In the HKMA's anti-money laundering (AML) Guideline, ‘should’ marks a mandatory requirement, just like ‘must’. [S:7bfdad704174, para 1.6]

The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has its own AML Guideline for the firms it licenses. It adds rules for virtual assets (VAs), such as cryptocurrencies. Those firms should screen virtual asset (VA) transactions and the linked wallet addresses, using tools such as blockchain analytic tools. [S:08a762f7233e, paras 12.7.2–12.7.3] A bank's SFC-registered business that lets clients deposit or withdraw VAs should also follow those rules for those deposits and withdrawals. [S:8a016a912528, para 20(b)]

Fraud is the other big risk. Police intelligence shared with the SFC shows scam money moving through bank transfers to SFC-licensed firms. Some was then turned into VAs and withdrawn to unhosted wallets. [S:be46918f8aa2, para 8] The HKMA counts cutting fraud and mule accounts among its highest priorities. [S:100b982aadca, p.83] Mule accounts are bank accounts used to pass on criminal money.

## Key facts

**Sanctions law and the bank**

- The UNSO lets the Chief Executive make regulations that carry out UN Security Council (UNSC) sanctions. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.7] These include targeted financial sanctions (TFS) against persons and entities the UNSC or its committees designate. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.7] TFS means both freezing assets and banning anyone from making assets available to those named. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.7 fn 61]
- Without a licence from the Chief Executive, it is an offence to deal with the assets of a designated party. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.7] It is also an offence to make funds available to them, to those acting for them, or to entities they own or control. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.7] Names appear in the Gazette or on the Commerce and Economic Development Bureau website. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.7]
- Proliferation financing (PF) means funding the spread of weapons of mass destruction. [S:7bfdad704174, Glossary] Hong Kong tackles it through UNSO regulations aimed at North Korea and Iran. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.10] It also uses a separate ordinance. That ordinance bans providing services that a person believes or suspects, on reasonable grounds, may be linked to PF. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.10]
- The HKMA says banks do not normally have a Hong Kong legal duty to take account of one-sided (unilateral) sanctions set by other places. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.11] A bank that operates internationally will still need to know the scope and focus of those regimes. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.11] Where they may affect its operations, the bank should consider the implications and take suitable steps. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.11]
- A UN listing may give grounds for knowledge or suspicion under Hong Kong's money laundering, terrorist financing and PF laws. This applies whether or not Hong Kong has legislated for it. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.14] Banks should add new UN names to the database as soon as practicable. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.14]
- SFC circulars pass on statements from the FATF, the global AML standard-setter. [S:43fd79de14c1, p.1] They repeat the FATF call for all countries to apply UN TFS against North Korea firmly. [S:43fd79de14c1, p.1] The SFC reminds its firms to take extra care with higher-risk jurisdictions. [S:43fd79de14c1, p.4; S:00c9fbd119bf, p.3]

**What the HKMA expects on screening**

- Each bank should keep a database that brings together the various terrorist and sanctions lists made known to it. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 6.13, 6.15] It may subscribe to a third-party database instead. It should then take steps, such as regular sample tests, to make sure the database is complete and accurate. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.13]
- The bank should screen customers and their beneficial owners when the relationship starts. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.16] It should screen them again against new designations as soon as practicable. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.16] It should screen all parties to a cross-border wire transfer before sending it. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.16] Screening applies whatever the customer's risk rating. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.16 fn 62]
- When a name may match, the bank should run enhanced checks and keep records of the results. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.18] If it suspects terrorist financing, PF or a sanctions breach, it should report to the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (JFIU), the unit that receives suspicious transaction reports. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.18]
- A bank may use an overseas office for the database or screening. Ultimate responsibility stays with the Hong Kong bank. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.19]

**Wallet screening and blockchain analytics**

- The SFC expects its firms to screen every relevant party in a VA transfer before carrying it out. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.8.1] This includes the recipient when VAs go to an unhosted wallet, and the sender when VAs come from one. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.8.1] An unhosted wallet is one where the person holds their own private key. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.1.9 fn 116]
- If an incoming VA transfer arrives unscreened, or with missing sender or recipient details, the firm should take risk-reducing steps and write them down. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.8.3] Examples include blocking the recipient's access to the assets, or holding the receiving wallet, until screening finds no concern. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.8.3 fn 135]
- Firms should screen before a transfer, or before releasing received assets to the customer. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.7.3 fn 128] They should also re-screen afterwards on a risk basis. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.7.3 fn 128]
- Blockchain analytic tools trace a token's on-chain history. They compare it with a database of wallet addresses tied to crime, and flag matches. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.7.3 fn 130] A firm using an outside tool stays responsible for its AML duties. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.7.4] It should check the tool's quality, data coverage and limits before use. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.7.4] The SFC gives examples of limits. A tool may have limited reach, or may not cope with mixers or privacy coins. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.7.4(c)]
- Mixers (also called tumblers) pool VAs from many users and pay out to new addresses, breaking the link to the source. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.1.8 fn 115] The SFC also names privacy coins and privacy wallets as tools used to hide where VAs come from. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.1.8] A peel chain moves a large sum through many steps, sending a slightly smaller amount to a new address each time. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.1.8 fn 113]
- ‘Know your transaction’ (KYT) is an industry label (concept) for ongoing screening of wallets and transactions.
- Licensed stablecoin issuers should also screen stablecoin transfers and wallet addresses, using tools such as blockchain analytic tools. [S:4d98c6a80b4d, para 5.4] Possible measures they may use include blacklisting sanctioned wallet addresses. Another is freezing coins promptly when regulators, law enforcement or courts ask. [S:4d98c6a80b4d, para 5.10] The HKMA says these measures are not yet proven to work. [S:4d98c6a80b4d, para 5.11] Unless an issuer shows the HKMA they work, each holder's identity should be verified. [S:4d98c6a80b4d, para 5.11]

**Fraud and mules at the bank–platform interface**

- The SFC has seen clients make many deposits, sometimes in small or odd amounts, from several bank accounts in their own names. [S:be46918f8aa2, para 7(a)] Most of the money was withdrawn soon after, with little or no trading. [S:be46918f8aa2, paras 5, 7] Splitting large sums into small ones to avoid notice is called smurfing. [S:be46918f8aa2, para 7 fn 1]
- The SFC lists red flags that include turning money into VAs with no logical or clear reason. [S:be46918f8aa2, para 11(h)] Another is unrelated clients using the same internet protocol (IP) address or device. [S:be46918f8aa2, para 11(i)]
- The SFC expects licensed firms to screen VA transactions and wallet addresses before acting for clients. [S:be46918f8aa2, para 17] It also expects them to register client bank accounts and whitelist client wallets. [S:be46918f8aa2, para 20] A firm should confirm wallet ownership, for example with a small test payment or a signed message. [S:be46918f8aa2, para 20(a)]
- Electronic Direct Debit Authorisation (eDDA) lets a payee pull money from an account in the payer's own name, once the payer has agreed. [S:cfc4a0d9f1be, para 1 fn 1] Under a ‘simplified’ setup, the broker or platform starts the request. The customer's bank may or may not ask the customer to confirm. [S:cfc4a0d9f1be, paras 3–4; fn 2] The SFC says stolen details can be used to set one up without the account owner knowing. [S:cfc4a0d9f1be, para 6(a)]
- The HKMA's Suspicious Account Alert started in November 2023. It warns a sender who pays an account that the Police ‘Scameter’ database flags as suspicious. [S:100b982aadca, p.82 fn 5]
- In April 2025, the HKMA widened Scameter data use by retail banks and by stored value facility (SVF) licensees. [S:100b982aadca, p.83] In June 2025, the Banking Ordinance was changed so banks can share account data through set platforms, including FINEST, to detect and prevent crime. [S:100b982aadca, p.83] After FINEST was upgraded in mid-December 2025, shared cases rose from 120 to about 750 a month. That covers the first 1.5 months, to the end of January 2026. [S:100b982aadca, p.83]
- The Police-led Fraud and Money Laundering Intelligence Taskforce (FMLIT) was set up in 2017. The HKMA, retail banks and SVF licensees take part. [S:100b982aadca, p.83 fn 6] In 2025, 1,377 intelligence-led suspicious transaction reports reached FMLIT. These reports helped find 22,740 new suspicious accounts. They also led to HK$306.7 million being restrained. [S:100b982aadca, p.83]
- Customs has a stated target to launch DATAS by 2029, to collect and analyse digital asset data. [S:ef3648ff1610, Annex, item 209] The Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau (FSTB) says it will consult the public later in 2026. The consultation is on widening the law so sectors such as technology and telecoms can better detect and remove fraud content. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 123(i)(b)]

## What your bank must do

**Obligations**
- [ ] Keep policies, procedures and controls for terrorist financing, sanctions and PF. Train staff on them. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.12]
- [ ] Do not make funds available to designated parties, or deal with their assets, without a licence. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.7]
- [ ] Apply in writing to the Commerce and Economic Development Bureau if the bank seeks a UNSO licence. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.8]
- [ ] Consider what other places' sanctions regimes mean for the bank's operations, and take suitable steps. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.11]

**Controls & monitoring**
- [ ] Keep one combined sanctions database, updated promptly and easy for staff to reach. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 6.13, 6.15]
- [ ] Sample-test any vendor database for completeness and accuracy. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.13]
- [ ] Screen customers, beneficial owners and cross-border wire parties as para 6.16 sets out. Extend screening to connected parties on a risk basis. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 6.16–6.17]
- [ ] Run enhanced checks on possible matches and keep all screening records. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.18]
- [ ] In the RI business, follow SFC Chapter 12 when handling client VA deposits and withdrawals. [S:8a016a912528, para 20(b)] That covers wallet screening and blockchain analytics. [S:08a762f7233e, paras 12.7.2–12.8.3]
- [ ] In the RI business, if an incoming VA transfer cannot be screened, take and record risk-reducing steps. One example is holding the assets or the receiving wallet until screening clears. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.8.3 fn 135; S:8a016a912528, para 20(b)]

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- [ ] Report suspected sanctions, terrorist financing or PF breaches to the JFIU. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.18]
- [ ] Add names the HKMA draws to the bank's attention to the database. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.15]
- [ ] For the SFC side, note that the SFC reminds its firms to consider reporting eDDA red flags to the JFIU or the Police Anti-Deception Coordination Centre. [S:cfc4a0d9f1be, para 11]

**Counterparty due diligence**
- [ ] Note that SFC-licensed firms should check how their respondent institutions screen VA transfers and wallet addresses. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.6.4]
- [ ] Before the RI uses a blockchain analytics vendor for client VA flows, assess its tools, data coverage and limits. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.7.4; S:8a016a912528, para 20(b)]
- [ ] Note that the SFC expects its licensed firms, including platforms, to register client bank accounts and whitelist wallets. [S:be46918f8aa2, para 20] They should also limit how many each client uses. [S:be46918f8aa2, para 20(b)]

## Talking points

**To the CEO**
- Hong Kong puts most UN sanctions into law through the UNSO. Making funds available to designated parties without a licence is an offence. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 1.26, 6.7]
- The HKMA counts fraud and mule accounts among its highest priorities. [S:100b982aadca, p.83]

**To the CCO**
- Hong Kong law does not normally require us to take account of overseas unilateral sanctions. Where those regimes may affect our operations, the HKMA expects us to consider them and act. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.11]
- In the HKMA Guideline, ‘should’ is mandatory. [S:7bfdad704174, para 1.6] So the screening steps in Chapter 6 are requirements. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 6.12–6.19]

**To business heads**
- In our RI business, client VA transfers in or out should be screened before they go ahead. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.8.1; S:8a016a912528, para 20(b)] If an incoming transfer cannot be screened, the assets may be held until checks clear. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.8.3 fn 135]
- The SFC flags turning money into VAs with no clear reason. [S:be46918f8aa2, para 11(h)]

**To Risk / CRO**
- Blockchain analytics tools have limits. They may have limited reach and may not cope with mixers. The user firm stays responsible either way. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.7.4]
- Screening can move offshore to group, but the responsibility cannot. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.19]

## Common mix-ups

- **Myth:** Hong Kong law makes the bank follow US or EU sanctions. **Fact:** The HKMA says banks do not normally have that duty under Hong Kong law. It still expects banks operating internationally to know those regimes, consider their effect and act. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.11]
- **Myth:** Only UN names already in Hong Kong law go in the database. **Fact:** Banks should add UN names as soon as practicable, whether or not Hong Kong has legislated. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.14]
- **Myth:** Low-risk customers can skip sanctions screening. **Fact:** Screening applies whatever the customer's risk profile. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.16 fn 62]
- **Myth:** A good blockchain analytics vendor moves the duty to the vendor. **Fact:** The firm stays responsible and should check the tool before using it. [S:08a762f7233e, para 12.7.4]
- **Myth:** Deposits from the client's own bank account are safe. **Fact:** The SFC found firms that ignored layering red flags because no third party seemed involved. [S:be46918f8aa2, para 10]

## Check yourself

1. Who can grant a licence to make funds available to a UN-designated party, and how does a bank apply?
   **Answer:** The Chief Executive grants the licence. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.7] A bank seeking one applies in writing to the Commerce and Economic Development Bureau. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.8]
2. When should a bank screen its customers and beneficial owners against its sanctions database?
   **Answer:** When the relationship starts, and again against new designations as soon as practicable. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.16] Screening applies whatever the customer's risk rating. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.16 fn 62]
3. How binding is ‘should’ in the HKMA's AML Guideline for banks?
   **Answer:** In that Guideline, ‘should’ marks a mandatory requirement, just like ‘must’. [S:7bfdad704174, para 1.6] So the screening steps in Chapter 6 are requirements. [S:7bfdad704174, paras 6.12–6.19]
4. What does SFC Chapter 12 mean for our RI business when clients move VAs in or out?
   **Answer:** The RI should follow Chapter 12 for client VA deposits and withdrawals. [S:8a016a912528, para 20(b)] That covers wallet screening and blockchain analytics. [S:08a762f7233e, paras 12.7.2–12.8.3]
5. Does Hong Kong law make the bank follow US or EU sanctions?
   **Answer:** The HKMA says banks do not normally have that duty under Hong Kong law. It still expects banks operating internationally to know those regimes, consider their effect and act. [S:7bfdad704174, para 6.11]
