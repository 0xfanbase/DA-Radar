# C2 VA funds and ETFs

For the Head of Compliance at a bank that distributes funds or acts as participating dealer: after this module you can explain which crypto funds and tokenised funds may be sold in Hong Kong, on what terms, and what the bank checks before it sells, creates or redeems units.

## In 60 seconds

Since October 2022 the SFC has authorised public funds that get exposure to virtual assets (VAs). It began with futures-based ETFs and extended to spot VA ETFs under a December 2023 circular. That circular was revised in April 2025 to permit staking and replaced again on 27 May 2026 (26EC31). A fund with more than 10% of its net asset value (NAV) in VAs needs prior SFC consultation and approval. It may hold only tokens the Hong Kong public can trade on SFC-licensed platforms, must not use leverage, and must keep VAs with an SFC-licensed platform or a bank that meets HKMA custody standards. Separate SFC circulars allow tokenised classes of authorised funds. Since April 2026 they may also be traded on the secondary market on licensed platforms. When a bank sells any of these products, the joint SFC–HKMA circular sets the selling rules. Banks acting as participating dealers must be registered and carry the prescribed terms.

## Key facts

- The first VA ETFs allowed were futures-based. They could hold only CME bitcoin and ether futures, and the manager needed at least three years of ETF experience [S:51dac2cabe78, paras 10, 12].
- The 2022 circular also capped net derivative exposure at 100% of NAV and applied the VA-knowledge test to distribution even without solicitation [S:51dac2cabe78, paras 14, 16].
- Under the December 2023 circular, funds could hold spot VAs directly [S:5b6530666cc2, para 1]. The first spot VA ETFs listed on the Hong Kong stock exchange on 30 April 2024 [S:e4236ed2600f, para 1].
- The current circular (26EC31) applies to public funds with more than 10% of NAV in VAs. It replaced the April 2025 version [S:c79c4686c5e0, paras 1, 3].
- 26EC31 does not cover fund exposure to Relevant Stablecoins (coins licensed under the Stablecoins Ordinance) or tokenised deposits. Those are dealt with in Q20G of the UT Code FAQs [S:c79c4686c5e0, para 2].
- Eligible VAs are limited to tokens the Hong Kong public can trade on SFC-licensed VATPs. VA futures must trade on conventional regulated futures exchanges, and leverage at fund level is not allowed [S:c79c4686c5e0, paras 11, 13, 15].
- Spot VAs are bought and sold through SFC-licensed VATPs, on or off platform, or through AIs that comply with HKMA requirements [S:c79c4686c5e0, para 17 and fn 7].
- Spot ETFs may create and redeem units either in cash or in kind. For in-kind creations, participating dealers (PDs) move VAs into the ETF's custody account at a VATP or an AI. The VAs may have been held locally or overseas [S:c79c4686c5e0, paras 17(b), 18].
- PDs for spot VA ETFs must hold an SFC licence or SFC registration, and carry the extra terms in Appendix 6 of the joint circular [S:c79c4686c5e0, para 19 and fn 8].
- The trustee may delegate VA custody only to an SFC-licensed VATP, to an AI that meets HKMA custody standards, or to another entity the SFC accepts. Most assets must be in cold storage, and keys must be kept in Hong Kong [S:c79c4686c5e0, paras 20–21].
- The SFC may permit VA funds to stake through VATPs or AIs. Conditions include disclosure of the amount that may be staked, reporting in interim and annual reports, and prior SFC approval [S:c79c4686c5e0, paras 28, 31]. The SFC caps how much a fund may stake, to keep enough of the portfolio liquid [S:36753b22e10a, para 4].
- On distribution, SFC-authorised VA funds are not PI-only. Units listed on SEHK can be sold execution-only with a knowledge test. Unlisted classes, or trades off exchange, need suitability, warnings and the knowledge test [S:8a016a912528, paras 9.1–9.2 and fn 14].
- The tokenisation circular uses a see-through approach, looking past the token to the underlying product. It permits primary dealing in tokenised authorised products and discourages public permissionless chains unless extra controls are in place [S:58ea8520974d, paras 5, 13].
- Secondary trading of tokenised funds is permitted for retail through on-screen matching on SFC-licensed VATPs. Controls include a price-deviation alert against the indicative NAV [S:a64e364fd912, paras 8, 12].
- In March 2026, 13 tokenised products were on offer to the Hong Kong public, with tokenised-class assets of about $10.7 billion. The first secondary-trading batch is expected to be tokenised money market funds [S:bbd10edd41cf, paras 4, 7].

## What your bank must do

**Obligations**
- Apply the joint circular's selling rules when distributing any SFC-authorised VA fund, including the knowledge test for all clients except institutional and qualified corporate PIs [S:c79c4686c5e0, para 27; S:8a016a912528, paras 6.2, 9].
- Sell non-authorised VA funds only to PIs, and first do the Appendix 4 due diligence on the manager, the fund and its counterparties [S:8a016a912528, paras 6.1, 12.3].
- As a distributor of tokenised authorised products, apply the usual onboarding and suitability rules [S:58ea8520974d, para 18].
- For tokenised products that are not SFO-regulated (and for tokenised deposits placed by customers), apply the rules for the underlying product. Assess the risks that tokenisation adds [S:b2719f3dcb37, p.1 and fn 1].

**Controls & monitoring**
- As a Connecting Broker, show the price-deviation alert, tell clients they can subscribe or redeem at NAV instead, and get their confirmation of the risks before onboarding [S:a64e364fd912, paras 13, 20(c)].
- Keep an online feed of indicative NAV that refreshes at least once every 15 seconds while the market is trading [S:a64e364fd912, para 20(b)].
- Control online channels so that selling restrictions are enforced [S:8a016a912528, para 11].

**Notify / consult HKMA or SFC**
- Give the SFC advance notice, and the HKMA too where relevant, before first letting clients subscribe or redeem with VAs, including in-kind flows [S:d5ebca32c92b, paras 7(a), 10(d)].
- As an RI, also notify the HKMA before secondary trading in tokenised products [S:a64e364fd912, fn 13].
- Discuss any tokenised-product activity with the HKMA before launch [S:b2719f3dcb37, Implementation, p.6].

**Counterparty due diligence**
- For in-kind flows, hold the VAs only at SFC-licensed platforms or AIs, and apply the SFC AML Guideline Chapter 12 controls to the transfers [S:d5ebca32c92b, para 7(b)–(c)].
- Before selling a VA fund, check its custody chain against 26EC31: an approved delegate, mostly cold storage, and keys in Hong Kong [S:c79c4686c5e0, paras 20–21].
- On tokenised products, check the key management, smart-contract audits and DLT contingency plans of the issuer and its technology vendor [S:b2719f3dcb37, Section A, pp.3–4].

## Talking points

**To the CCO**
- The VA fund circular is written for product providers. The bank's own duties as distributor or PD come from the joint circular and our registration terms [S:c79c4686c5e0, paras 19, 27].
- For tokenised secondary trading, RIs must notify both regulators. Licensed corporations do not have to notify the HKMA [S:a64e364fd912, fn 13].

**To business heads**
- On-exchange VA ETFs can be sold to retail with a knowledge test. Unlisted classes of the same ETF need full suitability [S:8a016a912528, paras 9.1–9.2, fn 14].
- In-kind creation is allowed, and the VAs may come from overseas holdings. They must land in custody at a VATP or AI [S:c79c4686c5e0, para 17(b)].

**To the CEO**
- Hong Kong has moved from futures-only VA ETFs (2022) to spot ETFs, staking and 24/7-style trading of tokenised funds (2026) [S:51dac2cabe78, para 12; S:bbd10edd41cf, para 5].
- Tokenised-class assets were about $10.7 billion across 13 products in March 2026 [S:bbd10edd41cf, para 4].

**To Risk / CRO**
- VA funds must not use leverage at fund level, and custody delegates are limited to VATPs, qualifying AIs, or entities the SFC accepts [S:c79c4686c5e0, paras 15, 20].
- Staking by funds needs prior SFC approval and must be reported as a share of NAV, along with revenue and costs [S:c79c4686c5e0, paras 28(f), 31].

## Common mix-ups

- "Hong Kong only has futures VA ETFs." Spot exposure has been allowed since the December 2023 circular [S:5b6530666cc2, para 1].
- "A fund holding licensed stablecoins is a VA fund." 26EC31 excludes Relevant Stablecoins and tokenised deposits [S:c79c4686c5e0, para 2].
- "The 2023 or 2025 fund circular still applies." Each was replaced, most recently by 26EC31 on 27 May 2026 [S:c79c4686c5e0, para 3].
- "Tokenised funds can only be subscribed and redeemed." Secondary trading on VATPs has been permitted since 20 April 2026 [S:58ea8520974d, para 6].
- "In-kind subscription makes the bank a VA dealer." The regulators say it does not, but notification, custody and AML conditions apply [S:d5ebca32c92b, para 7].

## Open items

- The exact staking cap for VA funds sits in Q20F of the UT Code FAQs, and Q20G covers stablecoin and tokenised-deposit exposure. Neither is in the corpus [S:c79c4686c5e0, fn 4, fn 11].
- The 22 December 2023 text in the corpus already provides for in-kind creation. The exact date in-kind creation was first permitted cannot be confirmed from the corpus.
- The SFC says it may later widen tokenised secondary trading beyond money market funds. No timeline has been given [S:bbd10edd41cf, para 7].
- The 2026 Policy Address lists stablecoin settlement of tokenised money market funds on licensed VATPs as a stated target [S:ef3648ff1610, para 49(iii)].
