# F3 Market structure: who does what

A P&L (profit and loss statement) shows what a business earns and spends. This module maps the regulated firms in Hong Kong's digital-asset market and how each type earns money. After reading, you can name each firm type and say how many hold a licence, using official registers. You can explain where a bank can compete, supply or partner. You can also read listed firms' published figures without mistaking them for market shares.

## In 60 seconds

Hong Kong licenses digital-asset firms by activity, such as running a trading platform or issuing stablecoins. [S:4a0342268bf2, para 6] The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) licenses virtual asset trading platforms (VATPs), which are exchanges for crypto tokens. It listed 13 licensed platforms at end-June 2026, with six applications under review. [S:c24d834af185, p.30] The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) licenses stablecoin issuers. A stablecoin is a token built to keep a steady value against a currency. The HKMA register, dated 23 April 2026, lists two licensed issuers. [S:270f1f7324ab, Register table] The HKMA granted those two licences from 36 first-batch applicants. [S:193316443831, Assessment of licence applications, paras 1-2] A digital bank serves clients mainly online. [S:5422156398b3, para 1] The HKMA listed eight digital banks at 31 August 2026. [S:577cadbd3cd8, List, rows 5-12]

Other firm types are still waiting for their own licence. Planned regimes for virtual asset (VA) dealers and custodians are at the conclusions stage: the final policy is published, but the law is not yet made. [S:6186c01c6649, para 49; S:f41a543388bc, para 65] So are regimes for VA advisers and managers. [S:4a0342268bf2, para 1] On 24 June 2026 the Government said it will put one bill covering all four to the Legislative Council (LegCo) in 2026. [S:c6df52e680de, Reply (3), 'In addition' para] Some licensed firms are joint ventures. The HKMA names one licensed stablecoin issuer as a joint venture of three firms, one of them a bank. [S:193316443831, para 1]

## Key facts

**Virtual asset trading platforms**
- The SFC's list showed 13 licensed platforms and six applicants at its 29 May 2026 update. [S:b14be2203d39, Licensed platforms table; Last update line] The SFC stresses that applicants are not licensed. [S:b14be2203d39, Applicant list and notes]
- At 30 June 2026 the SFC counted 159 licensees under Hong Kong's anti-money laundering ordinance (AMLO), Cap. 615, the law that licenses platforms. That is 13 licensed providers plus 146 licensed individuals. [S:c24d834af185, p.32, table 'Licensees under the AMLO']
- The first platform licence was granted in December 2020, for professional investors only. [S:60682e9fd7fa, main text and Note 1]
- Since November 2025, licensed platforms may pool orders with overseas affiliates in a shared order book. [S:8e1426ec511f, p.64, 'Increasing market accessibility'] Affiliates may also act as market makers, meaning firms that quote buy and sell prices. [S:8e1426ec511f, p.64, 'Increasing market accessibility']
- A bank offering VA dealing may partner only with SFC-licensed platforms. It either introduces clients to the platform or uses an omnibus account (one pooled account for many clients). [S:8a016a912528, para 17]
- A licensed platform must keep at least HK$5 million of paid-up capital. [S:f1bbafccd556, para 6.2]

**Stablecoin issuers**
- The register of 23 April 2026 lists Anchorpoint Financial Limited (FRS01) and The Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation Limited (FRS02). Both licences took effect on 10 April 2026. [S:270f1f7324ab, Register table]
- The HKMA received first-batch applications from 36 entities by 30 September 2025. [S:193316443831, Assessment of licence applications, para 1] It granted licences to two of them. [S:193316443831, Assessment of licence applications, para 2]
- On 10 June 2026 the Government said that if more licences are granted, the total number will stay very limited. [S:3f0fcc104e61, Reply (2)]
- On 28 April 2026 both licensees confirmed they had not yet issued regulated stablecoins. [S:275e7319a841, paras 2-3]
- An issuer must not pay interest to holders. [S:fe4baea8dd7d, para 3.13.1] A licensed issuer needs paid-up capital of at least HK$25 million. [S:240be6833157, para 5.1.1] A bank licensee follows the Banking Ordinance instead. [S:240be6833157, para 5.1.3]

**VA custodians**
- Under the planned regime, platform-linked firms, banks and local banks' subsidiaries that safekeep client keys would need a licence or registration. [S:f41a543388bc, para 35] The FSTB (Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau) and the SFC aim to put a bill to LegCo in 2026. [S:f41a543388bc, para 65]
- Under the planned dealing regime, the SFC is to require VA dealers to use SFC-regulated custodians. [S:6186c01c6649, para 26]
- The SFC expects the trustee of an SFC-authorised VA fund to delegate custody only to three kinds of firm. These are a licensed platform, a bank (or a local bank's subsidiary) meeting HKMA custody standards, or another firm the SFC accepts. [S:c79c4686c5e0, para 20]

**Tokenisation platforms**
- The Central Moneymarkets Unit (CMU) is Hong Kong's central securities depository, a system that holds securities and settles trades in them. CMU OmniClear (CMU OmniClear Limited) runs it for the HKMA. It sits under a holding company, CMU OmniClear Holdings Limited. [S:9c8855ec55a1, para 2] On 12 November 2025 the HKMA said Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEX) would buy newly issued shares for a 20% stake. [S:9c8855ec55a1, para 2] Once the deal completed, the HKMA said, the Exchange Fund would own 80% of the holding company and HKEX 20%. [S:9c8855ec55a1, para 2] The HKMA called the deal a joint venture. [S:9c8855ec55a1, para 2]
- The 2026 Policy Address says CMU OmniClear is to set up a digital asset platform in 2026 for issuing and settling digital bonds. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 35(iv)] It also says HKEX is building a multi-asset tokenisation platform on a blockchain. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 46(v)]
- It says digital bonds issued in Hong Kong took nearly 50% of the global market over 2025 and the first half of 2026. [S:ef3648ff1610, para 35]
- At end-June 2026, the SFC counted 15 tokenised retail products. Their tokenised classes had assets under management of HK$10.3 billion. [S:c24d834af185, p.33]

**VA fund managers and advisers**
- The SFC's terms and conditions for VA fund managers apply in two cases. One is a fund with a stated VA objective. The other is a fund that plans to put 10% or more of its gross assets into them. [S:40148cf96f41, Part I, p.3]
- The SFC counted 2,358 firms holding a Type 9 licence (asset management) at end-2025. The count covers all asset classes. [S:6d9deb07299d, Section VI, para 43] It gives no separate count for VA managers.
- The 11 VA spot exchange-traded funds (ETFs) had a combined market value of US$451 million at end-June 2026. [S:c24d834af185, p.31] Three of these ETFs may stake through licensed platforms. [S:c24d834af185, p.31]
- The Government and the SFC have yet to set a start date for the advisory and management regimes. [S:01c5c5c4045f, para 64]

**OTC dealers**
- An over-the-counter (OTC) dealer trades directly with clients rather than on an exchange. (concept)
- The dealing regime is to cover anyone who, as a business, makes or offers deals to buy or sell virtual assets, in line with securities dealing. [S:6186c01c6649, para 3] Payment firms offering conversion into or out of virtual assets may need a licence. [S:6186c01c6649, para 4]
- The Government and the SFC do not plan to treat existing VA dealers as licensed while they apply. [S:6186c01c6649, para 31] The regime takes full effect on its start date. [S:6186c01c6649, para 31] Licensed platforms, and SFC-licensed or SFC-registered firms that already deal in virtual assets, are to get faster approval. [S:6186c01c6649, paras 35-36]

**Digital banks**
- The HKMA listed eight digital banks at 31 August 2026, all incorporated in Hong Kong. [S:577cadbd3cd8, List, rows 5-12]
- A person holding more than 50% of a local bank should normally be a supervised bank or financial institution. Otherwise the HKMA expects the owner to hold the bank through a Hong Kong holding company that accepts supervisory conditions. [S:5422156398b3, para 9] Banks may apply to own a digital bank. So may technology companies and other non-financial firms. [S:5422156398b3, para 9]
- The HKMA would be concerned by a digital bank chasing market share with heavy early losses and no credible plan for profit. [S:5422156398b3, para 19]
- By end-July 2024, six of the eight digital banks (then called virtual banks) were registered with the SFC for securities business. [S:a4db9f935749, Executive Summary, para 12] In August 2024 the HKMA said it would remove the rule that they mainly serve retail clients. It pointed to their growing links with digital-asset and Web3 firms. [S:a4db9f935749, Executive Summary, para 14]

**Joint ventures and partnerships**
- On 10 April 2026 the HKMA described Anchorpoint as a joint venture. [S:193316443831, para 1] It named the three partners in this order: Standard Chartered Bank (Hong Kong) Limited, HKT (HKT Limited) and Animoca Brands Limited. [S:193316443831, para 1] The HKMA does not state the shareholdings. [S:193316443831, para 1]
- EnsembleTX (the HKMA's pilot for settling tokenised deposits between banks) has annexes dated November 2025. They name seven Tokenised Deposit Banks and 13 other industry pioneers. [S:d193f12a5f78, Annexes A-B] The seven banks include Fusion Bank Limited [S:d193f12a5f78, Annex A], which is on the HKMA's digital bank list. [S:577cadbd3cd8, List, rows 5-12]
- The Ensemble Sandbox list revised on 7 April 2025 shows banks in its trade theme. Technology firms there include Ant Digital Technologies and Microsoft Hong Kong Limited. [S:5f8362ecd97f, p.2]
- Project mBridge is a shared platform for cross-border payments in central bank digital currency. In June 2024 the HKMA was one of its project team members. [S:db56d97e2206, para 1 and fn 1]

## How it works commercially

- **Trading platforms** earn a fee on each trade. The take rate is fee income divided by trading volume. Platforms may also earn from listing, custody and staking. (concept)
- **Stablecoin issuers** earn the return on the reserve assets behind their coins. They pay distributors and run costs out of that return. Income moves with interest rates and the value of coins in circulation. (concept)
- **Custodians** charge a yearly fee on the value of assets they hold. Their main costs are key security, monitoring, audit and liability cover. (concept)
- **Tokenisation platforms** charge issuers to create tokens and charge users to settle and service them. (concept)
- **Fund managers** charge a management fee on assets under management (AUM), the value of client money they run. Some funds also pay a performance fee. (concept)
- **OTC dealers** earn the spread, the gap between the price they buy at and the price they sell at. (concept)
- **Digital banks** earn net interest margin, the gap between what they earn on loans and pay on deposits, plus fees. (concept)
- A bank can meet each firm type in three roles: competitor, supplier or joint-venture partner. As a supplier, it offers accounts, custody or credit. (concept) The business lines [Banking the digital-asset industry](#b-banking-da-industry) and [Infrastructure as a service](#b-infrastructure-service) cover the supplier role.

> **Analysis — not official**
> **Question:** For each firm type, is it a rival, a customer or a partner for our bank?
> **How to think about it:**
> - List each firm type and ask what it buys from banks: accounts, reserve deposits, custody, credit or settlement.
> - Mark where rules create demand a bank could serve, such as dealers needing regulated custodians [S:6186c01c6649, para 26] or issuers placing reserve assets with Hong Kong banks [S:c6df52e680de, Reply (1)].
> - Mark where the bank needs a licensed partner, such as a licensed platform for VA dealing [S:8a016a912528, para 17].
> - Compare building a unit, partnering, or holding a stake through a joint venture. Use the joint ventures and partnerships that regulators have already described as examples.
> **What it depends on:** how many firms hold each licence; when the new regimes start; the bank's own licences; how much risk from partners it accepts.
> **Official signposts:** the SFC platform list [S:b14be2203d39, Licensed platforms table]; the HKMA issuer register [S:270f1f7324ab, Register table]; the combined VA bill [S:c6df52e680de, Reply (3), 'In addition' para]; the CMU OmniClear platform [S:ef3648ff1610, para 35(iv)].
> **What this is not:** Not a forecast, not advice, and not the regulator's view.

## Common mix-ups

- **Myth:** Every name on the SFC platform list is licensed. **Fact:** The list has a separate section for applicants. The SFC says applicants are not licensed. [S:b14be2203d39, Applicant list and notes]
- **Myth:** VA custodians and OTC dealers already have their own licence. **Fact:** Both regimes are at the conclusions stage (policy final, law not yet made), with a bill targeted for 2026. [S:f41a543388bc, para 65; S:6186c01c6649, para 49]
- **Myth:** Only banks can own a digital bank. **Fact:** Technology companies and other non-financial firms may apply to own one. [S:5422156398b3, para 9]
- **Myth:** A listed platform's revenue shows its market share. **Fact:** Each filing covers its own firm, scope and period. Its revenue is not a share of a market. (concept)
